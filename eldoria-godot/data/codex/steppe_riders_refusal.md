# The Steppe-Rider's Refusal

*A fragment, found in the Crystal Caves on the lip of the under-stream, wedged
in the same crack as the Briarwood scribe's leaf and pressed beneath it. A
leaf of birch-paper, but cut squarer; the fold is folded once and pinned with
a small thorn of cold iron, not pine-wax. The hand is block-cut and runic, as
if each letter were carved before it was written — a Steppe-rider's hand,
taught to write slowly because Stone-Tongue is taught slowly. The cave-air
still smells of cold iron. Two and a half centuries old, by the thorn.*

---

## What the leaf says

> *I write this because the leaf below mine asked me to. I do not write often.
> Stone-Tongue does not write; it stands. But I will set this down for the
> cave to keep, as the woman of Briarwood set hers down, and I will keep my
> words few.*
>
> *I came south on the trail of a rider I will not name. She rode the Long
> Ride one summer and was given the Stone Crown that year, and the next
> summer she did not come back from a clan-feud none of us were told the
> shape of. I am not the rider who knows that shape now. I am the rider
> who came south to look. The High Steppe answers only to weather and to
> its own; weather had nothing to say, and the clans had said what they
> would say, and so I came south to the only place south of us that is
> older than any of us. The forest-line on a Foxthaw night.*
>
> *The Court was waiting. They did not know my name. They greeted me the
> way a court greets a guest from another house —* ***vael-i-thirren,***
> *they said, and I understood the word the way a rider understands a wind
> they have not met before. They were not unkind. They asked me to sit. I
> sat. They asked me to listen. I listened.*
>
> *They offered me the seat. They offered it the way the leaf below mine
> says they offer it — once, only once, with the cost laid out plain. One
> mortal year, remembered backwards. The Antler-King said* ***ai-mhorren,***
> *and I understood that one too: there is only one shape that word could
> be, and the Court spoke it slowly enough for a Steppe ear to follow.*
>
> *I said no. Not the way the woman of Briarwood said no. I said it in
> Stone-Tongue. I said* ***korthain*** *— "I refuse, but warmly" — and I
> laid the flat of my hand on the ground for the saying, the way a rider
> lays a hand on the saddle of a horse not their own. The Court understood.
> They are older than my saying. They are older than my refusing. They
> were glad of it the same.*
>
> *Then I asked the question the woman of Briarwood did not ask, because
> she was fourteen and frightened, and I am thirty-one and only tired. I
> asked: do you keep the names of those who did not come back from the
> High Steppe?*
>
> *The Antler-King said no. He said the Court keeps every name that has
> been* ***kerritha-ed*** *anywhere within the older lands, and that the
> High Steppe is older than the Court is; the names there are kept by
> the stone, not by the Court. He said —* ***drevenn-i-haern*** *— "the
> watching-stones already hold her," and he meant the cairns my own kin
> had built. He said I had ridden a long way to learn what I could have
> learned at home. He said this softly, and I believed him. I have spent
> ten winters not believing him, and twenty summers learning that he was
> right.*
>
> *I asked what I owed him, then, for the courtesy. He said nothing. He
> said the Court does not take. I said I was Stone-Tongue, and Stone-Tongue
> does not leave a courtesy unanswered. I said I would lay something down.
> He said: lay it where the cave will keep it. He said this fragment of the
> cave is a* ***thirre,*** *and the* ***thirre*** *is theirs and not theirs
> together; the cave does not belong to the Court, but the Court visits.*
>
> *And so I leave this leaf for the woman of Briarwood, whose words were
> under mine when I came in, and for whoever after her finds both of ours.
> I leave with this leaf one* ***thrunn*** *— a Stone-oath kept in writing,
> which is a strange thing for Stone-Tongue to do, because Stone-Tongue
> does not need writing to bind itself. The* ***thrunn*** *is this: I
> refused the seat. I will refuse it again if I am offered. I will not
> name the rider I came to find. The cave may keep her name with mine,
> and Vellum is patient; he has kept harder things.*
>
> *To the woman of Briarwood, if she still reads:* ***velhain-tor.*** *I
> have learned the word from your leaf. Go warmly. We were both kept by
> the same courtesy and the same refusing. The Court is glad of us both.*
>
> *To whoever after us:* ***korr*** *— what you owe to weather. Tend your
> cairns. Do not ride south on a Foxthaw night looking for a name. The
> stone already holds it.*
>
> — *written in a Steppe-rider's hand, pinned with a thorn of cold iron,
> folded once. The rider does not sign. Stone-Tongue does not sign; it
> stands.*

## Three new Stone-Tongue words enter canon here

These are the **first** Stone-Tongue words committed to canon. Stone-Tongue is
named in `lore/world.md` as "the runic, slow speech of the mountain clans —
each word is a small oath." Where Old Faerie *describes,* Stone-Tongue
*binds.* A Stone-Tongue word, once said with the proper gesture, is held to
have been promised. Riders use it sparingly, even amongst themselves, because
each word costs a little.

- ***korthain*** *(KOR-thayn)* — "I refuse, but warmly." A formal Stone-Tongue
  refusal of an offering one acknowledges as honorable. The saying is
  completed by a gesture: the speaker lays the flat of the dominant hand on
  the ground (or, indoors, on stone or worked timber). *Korthain* binds the
  speaker not to take the offer in the future either; once said, the
  refusal stands. It is the Steppe equivalent of *kerrithen* — *korthain*
  lays down a *no* the way *kerrithen* lays down a *yes.* The word may not
  be used to refuse a meal, a ride, or any ordinary kindness. It is reserved
  for the kind of offer one's whole life would turn around.
- ***thrunn*** *(THRUHN)* — "the Stone-oath kept in writing." Strange enough
  that the Steppe-rider in the fragment names the strangeness in the saying.
  Stone-Tongue is an oral tongue; an oath spoken in it is held by the
  speaker's bones, not by ink. To *write* a *thrunn* is to bind the oath
  twice — once in the speaker's bones and once in whatever the writing rests
  on. Steppe-folk do not do this lightly. A *thrunn* written and then
  destroyed (burned, dissolved, lost) is held by Stone-Tongue custom to be
  *still binding,* because the speaker's bones still know it. The fragment
  is itself a *thrunn.*
- ***korr*** *(KOR)* — "what you owe to weather." The irreducible debt every
  Steppe-rider carries to the world. The mountain clans answer only to
  weather and to each other (`world.md`); *korr* is the weather half of
  that answering. Used as a small benediction at parting — *korr,* "tend
  your weather-debt" — and as a private word said quietly before a hard
  ride. Not an oath; an acknowledgement. The closest Briarwood equivalent
  is the *velhain-tor* the Steppe-rider learns from the scribe's leaf —
  but *velhain-tor* wishes a warm hearth to return to, and *korr* assumes
  no hearth is owed. Stone-Tongue is colder.

### And one Old Faerie compound from the Antler-King's mouth

- ***drevenn-i-haern*** *(DREV-en ee HAYRN)* — "the watching-stones already
  hold her." A Court phrasing the Antler-King uses when speaking to a
  Steppe-rider about a kin lost on the High Steppe. The compound is Old
  Faerie in form (*drevenn,* "watching-stone, cairn"; *i,* possessive
  genitive; *haern,* "she who has been set down"), but the Court is
  reaching across to Steppe-custom in saying it — acknowledging that the
  cairns of the High Steppe are a keeping-system the Court does not need
  to duplicate. The phrase is what makes this fragment a *gentle* refusal
  of the rider's question, not a hostile one. Briarwood scholars who learn
  *drevenn-i-haern* use it (rarely, gently) for any Steppe death. It is
  not used for Briarwood deaths.

## How the player finds this fragment

The leaf is wedged in the same crack as the first fragment, pressed beneath
the Briarwood scribe's leaf. After reading **The Stag-Court's Courtesy**, a
small thorn of cold iron becomes visible at the base of the crack on a return
visit. Examining the thorn dislodges the second leaf. The unlock toast reads:

> *A second leaf, stiffer than the first, falls from the same crack. The hand
> is not a Briarwood hand.*

Reading the codex unlocks an entry in the Codex panel under
**Fragments → The Steppe-Rider's Refusal.** The two fragments should display
adjacently in the panel and share a small linking glyph (a leaf-and-leaf
mark) so the player understands they are paired.

Builder note: the leaf should not appear before player_level 8 *and* not
before the player has read the first fragment. The prerequisite is
narrative, not mechanical — the Crystal Caves are a *thirre* (`world.md`),
and the second leaf was placed *responding to* the first. A player who
finds the second before the first will read it as gibberish; the gating
prevents that. If the codex system already supports a `prerequisite_codex`
field, this is the canonical first use of it — please treat the precedent
gently.

## What this fragment establishes (and what it does not)

**Establishes (canon, downstream agents may build on this):**

- **Stone-Tongue is now writable in canon.** Three Stone-Tongue words —
  *korthain, thrunn, korr* — and the cultural rule that Stone-Tongue *binds*
  rather than describes. Future writers may add Stone-Tongue words; please
  keep the binding-not-describing rule. A Stone-Tongue word that is a mere
  description (a noun for a hill, a verb for *to walk*) is not Stone-Tongue.
  It is dialect.
- **The Stag-Court's offer is universal.** The Court offers the seat at the
  forest-line to *any* mortal who walks close enough on the right night —
  not only Briarwood mortals. A Steppe-rider received the same offer with
  the same wording and the same cost. Future Lorekeepers may show the offer
  given to other origins (a southern merchant, a wandering bard) but the
  shape of the offer must remain the shape established here and in the
  first fragment.
- **The Court's authority has a limit.** The Court does not keep High Steppe
  names; the cairns do. This is the first canonical limit on the Stag-Court
  in any direction, and it is a *kindness* (the Court declining to claim
  what is not theirs to claim), not a weakness. Future writing may show
  the Court's authority limited by other older systems — the smoke-cities'
  iron magistrates, perhaps; the Hollow King's winter — but the limits
  should always read as humility, not impotence.
- **The Crystal Caves are a *thirre* held jointly.** The Antler-King names
  it: *"the cave does not belong to the Court, but the Court visits."* The
  caves are Vellum's wound and the Court's listening-post both. Future
  fragments seeded here may carry hands from any direction (Briarwood,
  Steppe, Court itself, the Iron Crown's lost agents) and the cave will
  hold them all. This is the **canonical reason** the Crystal Caves are
  the right place for fragments-as-codex-pages.
- **The Steppe-rider in this fragment is unnamed and unsigned, by custom.**
  Stone-Tongue does not sign. Future writers must not name this rider, even
  in a later fragment. They may *quote* this rider, refer to *the rider
  who left the second leaf,* or describe the leaf — but the rider remains
  anonymous, the way the Briarwood scribe remains anonymous. This is a
  Lore Keeper rule, not a Builder rule.
- **The kin the Steppe-rider came south to find is also unnamed, and is
  explicitly a *she.*** This is deliberate: the kin is **not Cailen** of
  Briarwood (who left at nineteen for the Stone Crown — `elder_maeve.md`,
  `stablemaster_roan.md`). The Steppe loses its own riders in its own way,
  and the High Steppe's grief is not a mirror of Briarwood's. Future writers
  may invent a name for this lost rider only if there is a load-bearing
  reason; otherwise leave her as the Steppe-rider's leaf leaves her — held
  by the cairns.
- **A new Briarwood-Steppe linguistic bridge:** *velhain-tor* is a
  Briarwood-side word the Steppe-rider learns from the scribe's leaf and
  uses back. *Korr* is the Steppe-side equivalent the scribe never gets
  to learn. The two words pair — warmth-with-hearth (Briarwood) vs
  weather-debt (Steppe) — and any future NPC who knows both is canonically
  someone who has crossed between the two cultures (Roan being the prime
  candidate; Maeve being the silent candidate).

**Does NOT establish (please do not contradict in future runs):**

- The fragment does **not** confirm that the Steppe-rider's lost kin was
  among Cailen's clan, near Cailen, or known to him. It does not say. The
  High Steppe is wide, and many riders disappear there. Future Lorekeepers
  may or may not choose to braid the two stories; for now they are
  parallel, not joined.
- The fragment does not name the Antler-King, and continues the rule from
  the first fragment that the Antler-King is the speaking voice of the
  Court at the moment of the offering, not necessarily a single individual
  across centuries. The Steppe-rider's Antler-King may or may not be the
  Briarwood scribe's Antler-King. The fragment is silent on this. Please
  leave it silent.
- The fragment does not describe the Stag-Court's geography in the
  Whisperwood. The forest-line is the threshold; what lies beyond is still
  withheld canon. The Steppe-rider does not see past it. Neither did the
  scribe. Future writing should preserve this — the Court is encountered
  *at* the line, not *past* it.
- The fragment does not teach the player Stone-Tongue. The three words are
  glossed in the codex's lexicon section, but the language is not made
  available as a player-learnable system. Builder may not gate quests on
  the player "knowing Stone-Tongue." A player who has read this fragment
  may have a single optional dialogue line with Roan unlocked (see
  hooks below) but that is the only reach.
- The fragment does not undo the first fragment's central rule. The Court
  is glad when mortals refuse the seat, in either tongue, by either custom.
  *Korthain* is a refusal the Court honors as warmly as the scribe's
  Common-tongue *no.* The first fragment's softness is preserved here.

- **Foxthaw** *(`world.md`, Calendar)* — confirmed again as the

## Hooks for future runs

- **A third fragment** is now structurally allowed but should not be
  written reflexively. Two leaves are a pair; three is a series, and a
  series implies a system. If a future Lorekeeper writes a third, the
  hand should be from a third direction *not yet covered* — a southern
  scribe who came north on a magistrate's quiet errand, perhaps, or a
  bard of Erris who walked the line for a song. The fragment must not
  resolve either of the first two; it must add a third refusal, or a
  *fourth* shape (a *yes*?). Withhold "yes" canon.
- **Roan's optional dialogue line.** If a player has read both fragments
  and speaks to **Stablemaster Roan** during a Foxthaw evening, Roan has
  one new optional line: *"I have read what the cave keeps, traveler.
  Korr."* The single Stone-Tongue word *korr* is said in Common
  pronunciation, with no translation. This is the only place Roan ever
  uses Stone-Tongue aloud in the village. Builder note: gate on
  `codex_steppe_riders_refusal_read` AND `codex_stag_courts_courtesy_read`
  AND current month == Foxthaw. No quest payload; the line stands alone.
- **Maeve's silent reaction extended.** Per the first fragment, Maeve's
  reaction to a player presenting the Stag-Court codex is silence and a
  slow nod. If a player presents *both* fragments to Maeve in sequence,
  Maeve's reaction extends: silence, slow nod, and one inline gesture —
  she lays the flat of her hand briefly on the table. The gesture is
  *korthain* without the saying. Maeve will not say *korthain.* She has
  no Stone-Tongue. She has only the gesture, learned from somewhere she
  has never explained. Builder: please use `anim_hand_lay_flat` (new) or
  `anim_palm_down_table` (existing if available) and no spoken line. If
  the animation does not exist, do not ship the gesture; ship the slow
  nod alone and queue the animation for a future Builder run.
- **Cailen's Horseshoe** *(`elder_maeve.md` hook)* — the Steppe-iron
  horseshoe over Maeve's hearth is now mythically positioned to be
  carried east, not south. The Steppe-rider's leaf says: *"Tend your
  cairns."* If a future Lorekeeper wires a Horseshoe quest, the natural
  endpoint is a *thirre*-stone on the High Steppe, not anywhere in
  Whisperwood. The Court has already declined to keep that name.
- **A muted ambient cue** when this fragment drops: a single low Steppe
  drum-beat (one strike, deep, not menacing — a heartbeat from far away),
  paired with the same Celtic flute note that paired the first fragment.
  Audio agent may pick this up. The two fragments share a *unison* —
  the flute note of the first, plus the drum of the second, plays on
  the rare moment a player has both unlocked and re-enters the Caves.
  Quiet enough that a player might not notice; just enough that the
  Caves feel *kept.*
- **Codex panel pairing.** UI agent: when both fragments are unlocked,
  the Codex panel's **Fragments** section should display them as a
  bound pair — perhaps two leaf-icons overlapping, with a short
  italicized header above the pair: *"What the cave keeps."* The pair
  treatment scales to three, four, etc., as future fragments arrive.
- **Festival timing.** The Foxthaw line *"Mind the forest-line tonight,
  traveler"* (queued in the first fragment for Maeve, Lyra, Roan) gains
  a Stone-Tongue variant only Roan would ever say, and only *if both*
  fragments have been read by the player: *"Mind the forest-line tonight.
  Korr."* The single word stands at the end. No translation. No
  follow-up.
- **Renown threshold.** If the player-renown system (per `WORLD_STATE.md`
  run 11) ever surfaces a "Caves-keeper" sub-track, reading both
  fragments is the natural first tick. Suggested label: *"You have
  read what the cave keeps."* No mechanical reward; the line is the
  reward.

## Author note for downstream agents

This fragment is a load-bearing piece of *what Stone-Tongue is.* Please do
not introduce a Stone-Tongue word, NPC, or item that violates the
binding-not-describing rule. Stone-Tongue is small because each word costs.
A Stone-Tongue glossary that reaches twenty words is wrong; ten is the
canonical ceiling for now. Add new Stone-Tongue sparingly, and only when
the saying of the word does work no Common phrase could do.

This fragment is also a load-bearing piece of *the Court's humility.* The
Antler-King declines to claim the High Steppe names. He does this softly,
without ceremony. If a future Lorekeeper writes a Court-versus-clans
conflict, the Court's voice in that conflict must remember it once said
*drevenn-i-haern* and meant it. The Court is not a power that wishes to
expand. Please do not write it as one.

The Steppe-rider's voice in this fragment — clipped, weather-knowing, plain
— is the canonical voice of any future Steppe-rider in any future writing.
A Steppe-rider who speaks in flourishes is a Steppe-rider gone south too
long. (Roan, after thirty years in Briarwood, is allowed *one* small
Steppe idiom freely, *"ride the leaves,"* and otherwise speaks Common like
a man who has chosen a hearth. That is the model.)

If a future Lorekeeper wishes to harden the Steppe — make it a
threat, a tempter, a villain — please remember the rule from the first
fragment's author note: write a *separate* power under the older-than-the-
Sundering layer, and let the Steppe remain what this fragment makes it.
A weather-knowing people who keep their dead in cairns and their oaths in
their bones, and who will refuse a Court's seat warmly when they have to.

*Vellum is patient.*
