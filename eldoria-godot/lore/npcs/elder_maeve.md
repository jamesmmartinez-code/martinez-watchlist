# Elder Maeve

*Eldest of Briarwood. Keeper of the Vigil candles, the village rolls, and the
walking stick on which the names of every Briarwood-born child for fifty
years are carved in a line of small ringed knots. Stooped, slow-spoken, and
not as frail as the stoop suggests.*

> "Ah, traveler. Trouble brews in the Whisperwood — seek out the Goblin Warlord."

---

## Where she grew up

Maeve was born in Briarwood in a hard winter of Lambmoon. Her mother
**Esca** was the village's potter and its quiet historian; her father
**Donn** was a charcoal-burner who walked the Whisperwood three days at a
time and came home smelling of pine-resin and old smoke. Maeve was the
youngest of four. Two of her brothers were taken by the winter cough
before she could remember their faces. The third, **Cailen**, taught her
to read at the kitchen hearth from a parchment-bound book of stag-prayers
that had belonged to their grandmother. Cailen left for the Stone Crown
at nineteen and did not come back. A horseshoe and a pressed sprig of
heather arrived two summers later with a Steppe-rider's compliments. That
is all Maeve has ever had of her brother. The horseshoe hangs above her
hearth. The heather she *kerritha-ed* into the meadow above the village
that same Honeysong, alone, and has not spoken Cailen's name aloud since.

She left Briarwood herself at nineteen, the same age Cailen had — to find
him, she told her mother, though she knew already that the High Steppe
swallows men, not gives them back. She walked for eleven years. She saw
the smoke-cities of the **Iron Crown** and did not love them. She rode a
season with a clan of the **Stone Crown** and kept their horses well. And
once, on a Foxthaw evening in the deep of Whisperwood-that-becomes-fey,
she heard the **Antler Crown** speak to her. They offered her a seat at
the Stag-Court for the price of one mortal year remembered backwards.

She did not take it. She had a letter in her pocket: her mother Esca was
dying. She turned south. She walked seventeen days in three pairs of
boots. She got home in time to wash her mother's hair one last time. She
has not left Briarwood since.

That was forty-two years ago. Maeve is sixty-eight. She has been Elder
twenty-two of those years.

## A formative loss — and a quieter wound

Maeve had a daughter once. **Aelis**. Aelis was born in a Sunpetal storm
to a father — a wandering scribe named **Oren** — who had stopped in
Briarwood for a winter and stayed for three. Oren left when Aelis was
still small; this is not the wound. Maeve does not speak ill of him. He
left because he had to, in the way some men do. Maeve raised Aelis at the
Elder's hearth, and the village raised her with Maeve. Aelis grew up
quick, bright, restless, and angry at the smallness of the valley.

At seventeen Aelis left for the Iron Crown's smoke-cities. She wrote
twice in the first year. She has not written in eleven.

Maeve writes, every Lambmoon, a single letter. She seals it in a brown
fold of parchment with a sprig of heather pressed inside, and she gives it
to **Mara the Merchant** to carry south on the year's trade route. She
does not know whether the letters arrive. Mara does not know whether to
tell her that one came back unopened three years ago, water-stained and
addressed in a hand not Aelis's. *(Mara has kept it. She has not decided
what to do with it. She will, eventually, give it to Edda to keep — the
forge holds things better than the market does.)*

This is the wound. It is the reason Maeve teaches the village's children
to write before they are six.

## A secret she keeps

Maeve still hears, on certain Foxthaw evenings, the Stag-Court's offer.

It is not a memory. It is a *current*. On rare nights, when fox-fire
kindles in the long grass between Briarwood and the forest-line, Maeve
can stand at her doorway and feel the offer renew itself, like a
half-remembered song the Whisperwood is humming back to her. She does not
go down to the forest. She has never gone. But she does not always sleep
on those nights, and the children in the cottages across the lane will
sometimes see her standing at her door with the candle un-lit, looking
out, very still, until the fox-fire fades.

She has told no one. Lyra suspects. Bram does not.

Maeve believes — she has not said this aloud either — that the Stag-Court
made the offer not to *take* her, but to *measure* her. She believes it
was a courtesy. She believes if she had said yes, they would have let her
go gently, and Cailen would have come home. She does not know whether
this belief is true. She does know that she would say no again.

## What she wants most

To outlive her usefulness by exactly one year. To see the next Elder
chosen before her hands give out. To, before she dies, know whether Aelis
is alive.

The first two she half-controls. The third she does not.

She has begun, this last winter, to teach **Lyra** the Vigil ritual — the
exact placement of the candles, the order of the names, which family's
candle stands closest to the well. She has not said why. Lyra has not
asked. Both of them know.

## The walking stick

The stick is hawthorn, cut by Maeve's own father in the year Maeve was
born and given to Maeve when Esca died. Sixty-eight years of weather have
made it black and oil-bright. Along its length, Maeve has carved a small
ringed knot for each child born in Briarwood since she became Elder. There
are one hundred and eleven knots. The thirty-seventh knot, lower than the
rest and nearly worn smooth from her thumb, is **Halsa**. The
sixty-second is **Edda**. Maeve named both. She will not say so. *(Edda
suspects. Edda has never asked.)*

When the stick fills, she will give it to whichever of the village's
children most needs holding to. She has not yet decided who. She suspects
she will know on the day.

## Relationships

- **Smith Edda.** Maeve was at Edda's birth. She washed Halsa's blood out
  of the linen and held the small new weight of Edda while Halsa slept.
  When Halsa died, Maeve carried Edda, then fifteen and half a smith
  already, home from the forge in her own arms because Edda would not
  walk. Maeve does not visit the forge. She brought Halsa's cradle to
  Edda the spring after, did not stay, and has not crossed the threshold
  since. Each thinks the other does not want her there. Both are wrong.
- **Innkeeper Bram.** Bram brings Maeve a bowl of stew on Longnight
  Vigil, after he has brought Edda hers. Maeve does not eat hers either.
  She sets it at the doorstep for the Hollow King, who is given his due
  in candles and in soup. The cat eats it before the Hollow King does.
  Bram knows. Bram has never said.
- **Herbalist Lyra.** Maeve's chosen successor, though neither of them
  has said the word *Elder* between them. Lyra brings Maeve heart's-ease
  and dogwort every Greenshield and stays to drink small-beer. Maeve
  teaches her the Vigil ritual one candle at a time. Lyra is the only
  villager who has ever asked Maeve, in plain words, *what did you see
  in the forest, that year you came back changed*. Maeve has not yet
  answered. She will. She has not yet decided how.
- **Stablemaster Roan.** Roan was Cailen's friend as a boy. Roan walked
  the High Steppe himself for two summers, half on Maeve's quiet asking,
  hunting for any word of her brother. He came back with none. Maeve
  paid him in a cradle she had carved with her own hands, a cradle Roan
  has never used. He keeps it in the loft above the stable. Both of them
  understand that the cradle is for him to give back to Maeve when she
  needs it again.
- **Mara the Merchant.** Maeve trusts Mara more than Mara realizes.
  Maeve gives Mara her annual letter to Aelis, and a small extra coin
  for "the road's wear on it." Mara takes both in good faith. The
  unopened letter Mara is keeping is the one debt between them that has
  not yet come due.
- **Trainer Hala.** Hala once asked Maeve to bless a sword-pupil of
  hers. Maeve said no — *Vellum keeps memory, not edges.* Hala nodded.
  They have respected each other entirely since. On Stag-night they
  walk together to Thiar's stone, neither of them hunters, and stand a
  while.

## How she sounds

Maeve speaks slowly. Each sentence is weighed twice — once in her mouth
and once after. She tends to address strangers by *traveler* and villagers
by their full given name. She quotes a proverb roughly every third
exchange — usually one she has invented and let the village remember as
old. She invokes **Vellum the Patient Stone** in moments of weight. Never
**Brigid** (that is Edda's god). Never **Thiar** (that is the hunters').
The **Hollow King** she names only in winter, and only with the reverence
one owes a guest.

Her Old Faerie is the cleanest in the village outside Lyra's — she lived
eleven years on the road and her ears were younger then. She uses it
exactly when nothing else will do, and she uses it in the older
constructions: *vael-tor-i* (the *we* form), not *vael-tor* (the *I*
form). Most villagers do not notice. Lyra does.

She laughs rarely and dryly. It is a *good* laugh when it comes — the
kind that makes a child decide to trust a grandmother.

## Old Faerie words seeded by this file

Two new Old Faerie words enter canon here. They sit alongside *thirre*,
*ai-velin*, *kerrithen* (`world.md`), and *haethe*, *unnen*
(`smith_edda.md`).

- ***vael-tor*** *(VAY-l tor)* — "the gathered hearth." The collective
  warmth that holds a village together when one of its number dies — the
  borrowed candles, the brought stew, the shared silence at the door.
  Treated grammatically as a singular thing the village does in unison;
  the *we*-form is *vael-tor-i*, and it is what Maeve actually uses when
  she speaks of Briarwood at the Vigil. The word is older than Common
  and older than the Sundering. The Stag-Court is said to have no word
  for it.
- ***thressa-mai*** *(THRESS-uh MY)* — "the unanswered letter." A debt
  of words owed to someone who has gone without farewell — a friend who
  left in a quarrel, a child who stopped writing, a brother who did not
  come back from the Steppe. To carry a *thressa-mai* is not shameful;
  to refuse to write it would be. Maeve has carried at least two her
  whole adult life. The word is used by herbalists when speaking of
  grief that has not yet found its shape.

## Cross-canon references

- **Vellum the Patient Stone** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon)* — Maeve's
  patron. *Endurance, oaths, the keeping of memory.* The walking-stick
  knots are a Vellum-rite of her own design.
- **The Hollow King** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon)* — Maeve sets out the
  Longnight stew for him; she names him only in his season.
- **The Stag-Court / Antler Crown** *(world.md, Three Crowns)* — Maeve
  was offered a seat once. She declined. The offer is, by her belief,
  still on the table. This is a major story hook for downstream agents
  and should be touched gently, never resolved cheaply.
- **The Stone Crown / High Steppe** *(world.md, Three Crowns)* — Maeve
  rode there a season; her brother Cailen was lost there. Roan rode
  there twice for her. Either thread is reusable for codex or quest.
- **The Iron Crown** *(world.md, Three Crowns)* — Maeve's daughter Aelis
  went there. Maeve does not love it. Future agents writing Iron Crown
  flavor should let Maeve's coolness toward it remain canonical.
- **Smith Edda's mother Halsa** *(smith_edda.md)* — Maeve was the
  midwife, named Edda, brought the cradle, has not crossed the forge
  threshold since. This is the canonical bridge between the two NPCs'
  backstories.
- **Longnight Vigil** *(world.md, Calendar)* — Maeve is the village's
  ritual-holder for it. The candle-order, the placement at the well,
  and the silence are all hers to keep. She is teaching Lyra the rite.
- **Honeysong Eve** *(world.md, Calendar)* — the lantern-on-the-pond
  offering to Erris. Maeve oversees it. This is also the night Maeve
  *kerritha-ed* her brother's pressed heather, and she is quieter that
  evening than any other.

## Hooks for future runs

- **A first quest from Maeve** that is *not* the Whisperwood Cleansing
  exists in latent form: *Cailen's Horseshoe.* The horseshoe above her
  hearth is iron. It is also, almost certainly, Steppe-iron with a
  Stone-Tongue oath worked into the curve. A future quest might ask the
  player to carry it to a Stone Crown rider passing through the valley,
  or to bury it on the High Road at Foxthaw. Reward: a small ringed knot
  carved into Maeve's stick *for the player*, which is the rarest thing
  the village offers. (No other in-game reward needed. The knot is the
  reward.)
- **The unopened letter** Mara is keeping is a slow-burn quest seed. If
  Builder ever wires a renown threshold or a *Mara trust* flag, the
  letter can surface as a turn-in to Maeve — and Maeve's reaction is
  *not* a quest-text moment. It is one of her dialogue lines, set
  carefully. Lyra should be present in the scene if possible.
- **The Stag-Court offer** is the deep story-fuel of Maeve's character.
  It should not be used until the player has reached the Crystal Caves
  (a *thirre*, per world.md), and even then it should arrive as a codex
  fragment, never as Maeve speaking it aloud. Maeve dies before she
  speaks it. That is the *point* of it.
- **Aelis** is reusable in two directions: a returning-daughter arc
  (rare, expensive, late-game), or a confirmed-dead arc (a Steppe-rider
  brings a second pressed heather from far south). Both are valid. Lore
  Keeper has not chosen which. Future writers may.
- The walking stick is a discoverable codex object in itself. *"Maeve's
  Knot-Stick"* — a one-page codex that lists the knots Maeve has carved
  for Briarwood-born children and the order they were born in — is a
  natural codex entry to pair with any future *Briarwood census* feature.
- A Longnight-only line for Maeve about *vael-tor-i* (the *we*-form)
  could be wired by Builder against `time_of_year == longnight` and
  `late_hour == true`. Maeve's `longnight_vigil` line in the dialogue
  tree is pre-tuned for this.

## Author note for downstream agents

Maeve's default `lines[]` array in `WorldBuilder.NPCS` is preserved —
those are the lines a stranger should still receive. The richer mood +
flag tree lives in `eldoria-godot/data/dialogue/elder_maeve.json`. As
with Smith Edda, this `.md` is canonical *intent*; the `.json` is the
canonical *surface* the Builder/Polisher will wire NPC.gd against.

Maeve's voice is the slowest in the village. She is a *small* presence
on a fast scene and a *large* presence on a slow one. Builder should not
shorten her pauses; the pauses are the character. Polisher should not
modernize her cadence; the cadence is the village's keel.

She will not, ever, tell the player about the Stag-Court. If a future
writer is tempted to make her say it — please do not. The withholding
*is* the lore.
