# Innkeeper Bram

*Keeper of **The Long Lantern**, Briarwood's only inn. Round, jolly, ruddy in
the cheek, white apron, mug always half-polished. He has not stepped past the
Briarwood signpost in nine years, and there is a reason.*

> "Welcome to the Long Lantern, friend. The stew's warm, the bench's open,
> and the road can wait outside. *polishes mug.*"

---

## Where he came from (and didn't go back)

Bram was not born in Briarwood. He came from somewhere south — he won't say
where, and the village has stopped asking. He arrived eleven years ago, on a
cart he did not own, with a lute he could play tolerably and a husband who
could play it astonishingly. The husband's name was **Caedr**, and Caedr was
a road-singer in the old sense — the kind Erris of the Two Roads still
listens for at crossroads.

For three years they walked the long path together, inn to inn, market to
market, debt-of-song to debt-of-song. Bram poured. Caedr sang. They were not
rich and they were not in a hurry.

On a midsummer **Honeysong Eve**, eight years ago now, Caedr followed a song
deeper into the **Whisperwood** than two hands of sense should have allowed.
A song-debt, Caedr called it — *unfinished business with the Antler Crown.*
Bram waited at the forest's edge for two seasons. Caedr did not come back.

Bram did not weep on the road. He waited until he reached Briarwood, set
down the lute, opened the empty house at the corner of the cobbled square,
and lit the first lantern. He has lit one in the front window every night
since. That candle has a name in the older tongue, and Bram will tell you
the name if you ask him kindly. He will not tell you whose it is.

## A kindness he tells as a lie

Bram brings Smith Edda a bowl of stew on every Longnight Vigil. He has done
this for nine years. He says, every time, that it is *"what your mother used
to make."*

It is not. Halsa never made this stew. The recipe is from a roadside inn
two weeks south of Briarwood, kept by a woman named Onna who let two young
travelers eat for free one winter when their cart-axle broke. Bram learned
it watching her stir.

Edda has known this since the third Longnight. They have never spoken of it.
This is, in the older smiths' phrase from `lore/world.md`, a *kerrithen*
between them — a thing laid down so the land may hold it. Both of them
prefer the lie. Both of them know.

The stew round does not stop at the forge. Every Longnight Vigil, Bram
walks from Edda's anvil to **Elder Maeve's** hearth and leaves a second
covered bowl on the doorstep — Maeve sets it out for the Hollow King, and
the cat eats it before dawn. Bram knows. He brings the bowl anyway.

Bram knows two other things about people in this village that he has not
spoken of and will not. He saw the saber Smith Edda forged badly the year
after Halsa died, and he saw who came to buy it. And he saw, two springs
ago, a single water-stained letter slip from **Mara the Merchant's** coat
pocket onto the bar — the address written in a hand not Aelis's, the seal
unbroken — and Mara pick it back up, and her face, and the way she did
not look up from her ale for the rest of that evening. Bram refilled her
cup without comment. The bar got a long polish that night.

## What Bram listens for

A village of seven NPCs has only one inn, and the innkeeper is the place
where stories pool. Bram has, over nine years, built a quiet ledger of
what he hears.

- **Maeve** does not drink, but she sits at the corner table on the third
  night of every moon and watches the door. Bram puts down a mug of warm
  apple-water without being asked, and Maeve nods. They are watching the
  same things, mostly. On Longnight she gets the second bowl of stew at
  her hearth-step, even though the cat is the one who eats it. Bram has
  never asked Maeve about the hawthorn walking stick or the knot she has
  carved into it for him. He suspects there is one. He will not look.
- **Mara** books a room every spring on her northbound circuit. Bram
  waters her ale a hair after the third — Mara prefers to wake clear, and
  Mara knows. Mara has never said. Both of them call this *good
  innkeeping.* Both of them are right.
- **Lyra** leaves a bundle of dreamleaf at his back door every Longnight
  Vigil. Bram does not sleep on Longnight. He gives the dreamleaf to the
  eldest traveler in the common room that night, every year. Lyra knows.
  Lyra has never said.
- **Roan** is the only person in Briarwood who calls Bram by his road-name,
  *"Bron."* Roan was Bram's horse-boy on a single shared route nine years
  ago, before either of them came to this valley. Roan does not use the
  name in front of strangers, and Bram is grateful in the way of people
  who do not need to say so.
- **Hala** argues with Bram, gently, about whether a blade is a tool or an
  oath. They have argued the same argument for nine years and neither has
  moved an inch. Bram thinks this is a kind of friendship. Hala does too.

## What he wants most

A last verse for **Caedr's** unfinished song.

Bram has tried to write one on most quiet evenings of the past eight
years. He has filled three small leather notebooks with attempts. None
of them rings. The day he writes a true last verse will be the day he
believes Caedr is gone — and so he will not. He has *chosen* this.

If a traveler hums a tune Bram does not recognize, he listens harder than
his face suggests. Once or twice a year, a passing bard sings something
that catches the shape of Caedr's old line, and Bram pours their cup
heavier than the coin paid for. He never asks where they learned it.

## How he sounds

Bram is louder in greeting than in conversation. The first three
sentences with him are warmth and welcome — *"Welcome to the Long Lantern,
friend"* — and after that, if you stay, he becomes quiet by degrees. By
the third mug he is mostly listening. He polishes the same mug for half
an evening when he is listening hard.

He hums old road-songs while he works. The same five or six of them, on
rotation. **Caedr taught him every one.** He hums them in Common — but
twice in a season, when the inn is empty and the snow is heavy, he
catches himself humming one of them in the older tongue, and he does not
remember learning the words.

He invokes **Erris of the Two Roads** more than any other god — at the
opening of a barrel, at the closing of the door for the night, at the
arrival of any stranger. He keeps a copper coin balanced on the lintel
above the front door for her, and he replaces it whenever he sweeps. He
does not invoke Brigid (that is Edda's god) and he does not invoke the
Hollow King by name during Longnight (Bram is, in his quiet way, defying
him).

## Old Faerie words seeded by this file

Three new words enter canon, joining *thirre*, *ai-velin*, *kerrithen* from
`lore/world.md` and *haethe*, *unnen* from `lore/npcs/smith_edda.md`.
Future writers may use them in codex entries, dialogue, or item flavor
without re-defining them.

- ***vethar*** *(VEH-thar)* — "the candle in the window." A small light kept
  burning for someone whose road has not ended. Erris-keyed. The lantern
  Bram has lit in the front window of the Long Lantern every night for
  nine years is *vethar.* The word is older than any inn.
- ***haisten*** *(HAY-stuhn)* — "the song with no last verse." A story whose
  teller stopped before the end — by death, by distance, by grief, or by
  *kerrithen.* Bards know the word. Innkeepers learn it. Caedr's last song
  is *haisten,* and Bram has chosen to keep it that way.
- ***breos*** *(BREE-oss)* — "what the bowl remembers." Old kitchens where
  many travelers have eaten are believed to hold the savor of every meal
  cooked in them. Used of any place a great many lives have passed through
  and been fed. The Long Lantern is *breos.* So is the road.

## Cross-canon references

- **Erris of the Two Roads** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon)* — Bram's patron.
  Coin on the lintel; lantern in the window; songs at every barrel-opening.
- **The Antler Crown / Stag-Court** *(world.md, Three Crowns)* — where Bram
  believes Caedr is. Per herbalist canon, *"the ones who don't return are
  not, the herbalists insist, gone."* Lyra is the herbalist who has told
  Bram this in those exact words. Both of them are lying to each other.
  Both of them are right.
- **Honeysong Eve** *(world.md, Calendar)* — the festival on which Caedr
  walked into the Whisperwood. Most loaded day in Bram's year. He sets a
  paper lantern on the pond for Erris, and a second one — silently — for
  *vethar.*
- **Longnight Vigil** *(world.md, Calendar)* — the night Bram brings stew
  to Smith Edda's forge. He does not sleep this night. He stays awake with
  whoever in the inn cannot.
- **Smith Edda's saber** *(npcs/smith_edda.md)* — Bram saw it when it was
  bought. He has not spoken of it. If/when Builder wires the Warlord's
  drop, Bram has a single non-confirming line cued up for that day.
- **Elder Maeve / the Vigil round** *(npcs/elder_maeve.md)* — Bram's
  Longnight Vigil stew-round is canonically Edda → Maeve. The bowl at
  Maeve's hearth is set out for the Hollow King; the cat eats it. Bram
  knows. The round is now a *village ritual*, not a one-NPC kindness.
- **Mara's water-stained letter** *(elder_maeve.md, withholding ledger)*
  — Bram is the second person in Briarwood (after Mara) who knows the
  letter exists. He has not spoken of it. Future writers MUST NOT have
  Bram surface it in dialogue; he is its keeper, not its caller. If Mara
  ever gives the letter on (canonically expected: to Edda for keeping),
  Bram has a one-line acknowledgement seedable on that day, but it must
  not name Aelis.
- **Whisperwood** *(world.md)* — Bram has not crossed the Briarwood signpost
  in nine years. He will not refuse a guest who has just come *out* of the
  Whisperwood. He will not ask why the guest looks shaken. The mug is
  always full first.

## Hooks for future runs

- **Honeysong Eve quest** *(seasonal, gentle):* Bram asks the player to set
  a paper lantern on the pond for "someone whose road has not ended." The
  quest is `kind: "fetch"` of one `paper_lantern` (a craftable or vendor
  item), `consequence: { npc_flag: ["Innkeeper Bram", "lantern_set"] }`.
  Reward: Bram's old road-knife (a `bram_road_knife` item — the only blade
  Bram still owns from his bard days, dulled, sentimental, +1 luck flavor
  not stat). NEVER reveals Caedr by name in the quest text. The player
  finds out only by reading this file or the codex.
- **The Last Verse** *(unresolvable, by design):* a `World.world_flag`
  named `bram_last_verse_offered` may be set by a future side-quest where
  a traveling NPC sings a tune the player can carry to Bram. Bram refuses
  to use it. The quest *completes* but Bram never confirms a last verse
  exists. This is intentional — Eldoria's tone is hopeful-not-tidy, and
  some songs are *haisten* on purpose. Future writers MUST NOT close this
  loop without consulting `THEME.md §7` first.
- **Caedr as codex narrator** *(downstream):* Caedr's voice is seedable for
  bardic codex pages — songs about Erris, the road, the Antler Court, the
  shape of an unfinished tune. Use the same convention as Halsa-as-narrator
  in Edda's hooks: present-tense voice, no body, no death confirmed.
- **The Long Lantern as building** *(Environment hook):* The inn's name is
  now canon. Environment may carve a hand-painted wood-and-iron sign with
  a small lantern motif over the front door. The lit candle in the front
  window is a per-night prop (Motion §12 — flicker, not static).
- **Bram's road-name "Bron"** *(downstream voice):* if Roan ever gets a
  warm dialogue variant addressed *to* Bram, Roan calls him *Bron.* No
  other NPC does. This is the only handle on Bram's pre-Briarwood life
  that any other NPC has. Use it sparingly.
- **Bram's dreamleaf gift** *(unset world flag):* Lyra's Longnight bundle
  is a candidate consequence for a future Lyra-Bram quest — possibly a
  `bram_holds_vigil` flag readable by Lyra's tier-3 dialogue.
- **Mara's letter — sealed-room pairing:** if/when Mara's unopened-letter
  arc ships (per `elder_maeve.md` Withholding Ledger), Bram is the second
  in-village witness. A reactive line on the day Mara turns the letter
  in is reservable as `bram_letter_acknowledged`, but it must not name
  Aelis or claim the letter's contents. The line should be *short, dry,
  and grateful that the matter has finally been laid down* — i.e., a
  *kerrithen* between Bram and Mara of the kind he and Edda already share.

## Author note for downstream agents

Bram's `default` line — *"Welcome to the Long Lantern, friend. The stew's
warm, the bench's open, and the road can wait outside."* — is the line he
would say to anyone, including a stranger walking out of the Whisperwood
two minutes after the worst hour of their life. It is *deliberately* the
warmest default in the village. Builder/Polisher should use it as the
fallback for any unhandled mood/flag combination.

The mood-keyed surface lives in
`eldoria-godot/data/dialogue/innkeeper_bram.json`. Tree-shape mirrors
`smith_edda.json` exactly — same time-of-day buckets, same contextual
keys, same `lore_notes` structure — so NPC.gd can read both NPCs through a
single code path once Builder wires it. The seasonal slot (Edda's
`spring_first_warm_day`) is replaced for Bram with `honeysong_eve`,
because Honeysong Eve is canonically the most loaded day in his year.
All three NPCs (Edda, Maeve, Bram) ship a `longnight_vigil` line; the
three lines are designed to read as a *triptych* — Edda doesn't look up
from the cooling blade, Bram brings the stew anyway, Maeve sets it out
for the Hollow King and the cat eats it. If Builder ever co-fires all
three on Longnight, the village's quietest scene plays itself across
three thresholds. Bram's line says *"Edda's forge first. Always Edda's
forge first."* on purpose — the *first* is the cue that the round
continues to Maeve.
