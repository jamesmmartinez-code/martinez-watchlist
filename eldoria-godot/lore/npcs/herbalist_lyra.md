# Herbalist Lyra

*Briarwood's herbalist. The only villager outside Maeve who reads Old Faerie
cleanly, and the only one who reads it in the constructions a kneeling
gatherer would use. Slender, leaf-tangled hair (she does not always notice
what she has caught in it), herb pouches sewn one-to-a-pocket along the
inside of her green-dyed coat. Gentle posture. Patient hands. The youngest
of the seven Briarwood NPCs by a decade.*

> "Heart's-ease for what aches inside, dogwort for what aches outside.
> Marshmint for both. Sit a moment, traveler — the kettle is already on."

---

## Where she grew up

Lyra was born in Briarwood in a quiet Greenshield. She is the
**eighty-ninth knot** on Elder Maeve's hawthorn walking-stick — a knot
Maeve carved with her thumb still slightly bandaged from the cutting,
because Lyra's mother **Wennet** had asked her to. Wennet was the
village's previous herbalist. The herb-coat with the inside-pockets is
hers. Lyra has let it out twice and taken it in once.

Lyra's father is unknown to the village; Wennet did not say, and Wennet's
silence on the matter is the kind a Briarwood-born woman is allowed to
keep. Wennet died of a lung-fever in the Sunpetal Lyra was eight. Lyra
remembers the smell of the room (eucalyptus and pondwater) and the way
Maeve washed her mother's hair and braided it the way Wennet had liked it
when she was alive. Lyra does not remember the funeral. She has been told
that she sat very quietly in Bram's lap at the back of the inn and did not
cry until three nights later, when she crept into the herb-shed and
crushed every dried bundle into powder with her two small fists.

The village raised her after that. Bram kept her two winters at the inn
and taught her to fold linen properly. Maeve taught her her letters from
the same parchment-bound book of stag-prayers Cailen had taught Maeve
from. Smith Edda — then nineteen, four years a smith, three years
motherless herself — taught Lyra how to grind a mortar evenly without
chipping the stone. ("Edda taught me before she could teach herself,"
Lyra has said once, to no one, on a Longnight she was alone.) Stablemaster
Roan taught her to ride a slow horse. Trainer Hala taught her, gently
and against Lyra's quiet preference, how to break a hold without hurting
the holder. Mara the Merchant brought her a southern cake on her tenth
naming-day and has, every spring since, brought her a small jar of the
same southern honey without comment.

Lyra is twenty-nine now. She has been Briarwood's herbalist for ten of
those years, since the season she came back from the Whisperwood with
her teacher's parting gift — the green-dyed coat, re-fitted at the
shoulders by a hand that was not hers — and a basket of seedlings she
had grown from cuttings the Whisperwood had let her take.

## The four years in the Whisperwood

When Lyra was fifteen, a wandering reader of the older signs walked
into Briarwood out of the eastern edge of the Whisperwood and stayed a
single Honeysong night at the Long Lantern. Her name was **Aenwyn**.
Bram poured. Aenwyn drank water and watched Lyra, who was at the next
table with a basket of dogwort she had picked badly. After the cup,
Aenwyn went to Lyra's table, named two of the herbs Lyra had not
known she had picked, named the third one Lyra knew, and asked Lyra,
in a voice that was Common but in the older shape of it, *who had
taught her to listen to the dirt with both hands.*

Lyra answered, *no one*.

Aenwyn said, *then someone is owed a debt.* And Aenwyn left in the
morning without speaking to anyone else.

Lyra followed her three days later. Maeve, who was at the village edge
that morning before sunrise as if by accident, did not stop her. Maeve
gave her a small sealed parchment to carry — *for the road's wear on
you* — that Lyra was to open only if the road went bad. (It did not. The
parchment was Maeve's own *thressa-mai*, written and sealed, and Lyra
returned it to Maeve unopened four years later. Maeve burned it on
Honeysong Eve. Neither of them has spoken of this.)

Aenwyn was half-something-else — Lyra never asked, and Aenwyn never
volunteered. Aenwyn was patient in the manner of people who do not
share a calendar with the village. They walked the Whisperwood together
for four years. Aenwyn taught Lyra:

- To kneel before naming a plant.
- To leave more than she took, always.
- The seventeen herbs the village herb-book did not list, and the four
  the village herb-book listed wrongly.
- The older Common — what Maeve speaks at the Vigil — and the older
  Faerie underneath it.
- That the Crystal Caves are a *thirre*, and that some plants only grow
  in the gentleness around a *thirre's* edge.
- That the Whisperwood goblins know more of the Old Faerie than they
  themselves have noticed they know.
- That the Stag-Court hears every herb-name spoken aloud at midnight
  inside the deeper Whisperwood, and that this is not a reason to fear
  but a reason to be honest.

In the fourth Foxthaw, Aenwyn took the green coat off her own shoulders,
re-stitched it overnight to fit Lyra's narrower ones, and walked east at
dawn into the deepest part of the Whisperwood. Lyra watched until the
fox-fire faded. She walked west home.

She has not seen Aenwyn since. She does not believe Aenwyn has died.

## A formative loss — the wound she carries

Two springs ago, on a Sunpetal evening, **Tess Brookhollow's** fever
turned. Tess was four. The fever was one Lyra had read about twice in
Aenwyn's notes and had never seen — a marsh-fever that takes the breath
in three nights, breaks on the fourth, and sometimes does not break at
all. The herb that breaks it is **fen-foxglove**, which does not grow in
the dry meadows above Briarwood and which Lyra had no cuttings of.

Lyra rode Roan's slowest horse hard for the marsh-edges south of the
Whisperwood. She found fen-foxglove there. She rode it back. She did
not eat. She did not sleep. She made the tincture by the inn's fire
because her own hearth was too far from where Tess was. Bram banked the
fire higher than he should have without asking. Maeve held Tess.

The tincture was correct. The hour was wrong. Tess died in Lyra's lap an
hour before sunrise. Lyra was twenty-seven.

She did not weep at the bedside. She walked out the back door of the
inn and across the meadow above the village to the place where her own
mother had taught her to pick wild garlic, and she sat there until the
sun was fully up. Bram came up to her with hot tea. He set it down
beside her, did not say anything, and walked back. Roan came up an hour
later with a folded cloak. Maeve did not come up at all — Maeve, that
day, washed Tess's hair and braided it the way Tess's mother liked it.

Lyra now grows fen-foxglove in three places — the inside corner of her
own herb-bed, a hidden patch behind the inn's wood-shed (Bram knows;
Bram does not know that he knows), and a small pot on the windowsill
of the schoolroom, in case the next child to take that fever takes it
in winter. She has not yet had to use any of them.

On every anniversary of Tess's death — the seventh of Sunpetal — Lyra
walks alone to the meadow above the village. She invokes Erris of the
Two Roads (because Tess's was a road that ended early), and she invokes
the Hollow King (because he received Tess gently). She does not invoke
Vellum. Memory, this once a year, is the one weight she sets down.

## A secret she keeps

Lyra still hears, faintly, the older tongue when Foxthaw fox-fire
kindles in the long grass between Briarwood and the forest line.

Aenwyn told her this is not a calling. Aenwyn told her it is a
*listening* — the way the Whisperwood speaks to the rare ear it has
trusted. Aenwyn told her the *listening* never asks for anything and
should never be answered.

Lyra has answered once. She was twenty-three. It was a Foxthaw evening
two years after she had come home, and the fox-fire in the meadow was
brighter than she had ever seen it, and what she heard was a single
word in the older Faerie that she had not learned from Aenwyn and had
not heard before. She does not know what the word means. She wrote it
down in the back of her herb-book in a small square ink, and she has
not, in six years, looked it up.

She suspects — she has not said this aloud, because the only person in
the village she would say it to is Maeve, and Lyra is afraid of what
Maeve's face would do — that the word is the *true name* of the
Stag-Court's offer. The one made to Maeve, the one renewed every Foxthaw,
the one Maeve has not taken. Lyra suspects the Court speaks this word
into every fox-fire that finds the right ear. She suspects Maeve has
never heard the word because Maeve has never been alone enough to
listen for it.

She has not told Maeve. She will not. Aenwyn was clear: the *listening*
is never to be answered. Lyra answered once. She does not intend to
answer again.

## What she wants most

To not be the last reader of the Whisperwood in Briarwood.

Lyra is the only villager who can name fen-foxglove on sight. She is
the only villager who knows which mushroom in the inner ring of stones
is safe and which kills in two hours. She is the only villager who can
hear the older tongue when the fox-fire kindles. When she dies, all of
that goes — and the village will be poorer in a way it cannot quite
name, because the village has not yet had to live without a herbalist
who knows.

Lyra wants an apprentice. Specifically: she wants a Briarwood-born
child who has, at six or seven, knelt by the wild-garlic patch on the
meadow above the village and put both hands in the dirt without being
told. She has been watching for ten years. She believes she has not
yet seen one. She is not certain.

She has not asked aloud. (Edda, who shares this exact wanting, would
recognize the silence. Lyra has not seen the recognition because Edda
has not seen the silence, because both of them are silent in the same
way.)

## Relationships

- **Elder Maeve.** Lyra brings Maeve heart's-ease and dogwort every
  Greenshield, and stays to drink small-beer. Maeve teaches her the
  Longnight Vigil ritual one candle at a time — the order of the names,
  the placement at the well, the silence held for the count of seven
  breaths between each lighting. Lyra is Maeve's chosen successor as
  Elder, though neither of them has said the word *Elder* between
  them. Lyra is the only villager who has ever asked Maeve, in plain
  words, *what did you see in the forest, that year you came back
  changed.* Maeve has not yet answered. Lyra has not asked again.
  (The third asking is for the day Maeve dies. Both of them know.)
  Lyra has noticed — she has not said — that the word she uses for her
  herbalist's faith, *wennen,* is one letter from her mother's name.
  Maeve has also noticed. Maeve has not said either.
- **Smith Edda.** Lyra leaves marshmint at the forge door every spring.
  Edda has never thanked her in words. Edda sends Lyra a new cleaver
  every Foxthaw — sharper than the year before. Both of them call this
  a debt. Both of them are wrong. (The cleavers, in canon, are *unnen*
  — Edda has not yet called any of her work that aloud. The marshmint
  is *wennen* — Lyra calls it that in her head and to no one else.
  This is, between them, the village's most exact small mutual gift,
  and neither of them has noticed how exact.)
  When Edda was nineteen and Lyra was nine and motherless three months,
  Edda taught Lyra how to grind a mortar evenly. Lyra remembers. Edda
  has forgotten. Lyra will not remind her.
- **Innkeeper Bram.** Lyra leaves a bundle of dreamleaf at his back
  door every Longnight Vigil. Bram does not sleep on Longnight. He
  gives the dreamleaf to the eldest traveler in the common room that
  night, every year. Lyra knows. Lyra has never said. Lyra is the
  herbalist who told Bram, in those exact words, *the ones who don't
  return are not, the herbalists insist, gone* — she said it the
  Honeysong after Caedr walked into the Whisperwood, and she said it
  knowing it was a kindness more than a truth, and she has not since
  said it again because she does not, ten years later, fully believe
  it. Bram knows this too. Both of them are lying to each other. Both
  of them are right. Lyra fed Tess's body the night she died at Bram's
  back hearth. Bram banked the fire higher than he should have. Neither
  of them has spoken of it. There is no *kerrithen* between them about
  Tess. There is only the back hearth, which neither of them has used
  to bank a fire that high since.
- **Mara the Merchant.** Mara buys Lyra's dried bundles for the
  southern circuit and pays in honest coin. Mara never haggles. Mara
  also brings, every spring, a small jar of southern honey that Lyra
  does not put into any tincture and does not eat — she sets it on
  her windowsill and turns it slowly across the sill as the year
  moves, until on the Sunpetal anniversary she leaves it at the
  meadow-edge for the Hollow King's ants. (Mara has noticed the jar
  is always empty at Reapmoon and full again at Greenshield. Mara
  has not asked.) Lyra suspects Mara is carrying something she
  cannot put down and has not yet been asked to set down. Lyra is
  willing to wait. Lyra has time.
- **Stablemaster Roan.** Roan rides the Whisperwood paths Lyra cannot
  reach without his slowest horse, and he brings her certain
  Steppe-roots that do not grow in the valley, and certain river-bones
  the goblins do not. Lyra patches Roan's hands when the horses bite,
  which they do more than Roan admits and less than Lyra fears. Roan
  was the one who saddled the slow horse the night Lyra rode for
  fen-foxglove. He did not sleep that night either. He brought Lyra
  the folded cloak the morning after. They have not spoken of any of
  this. Roan is the only person besides Maeve who knows, by Lyra's
  face, when she is hearing the older tongue.
- **Trainer Hala.** Hala walks with Lyra to the meadow above the
  village at every planting moon. Hala does the digging — Hala has the
  back for it — and Lyra does the naming. Hala has, twice in ten
  years, tried to talk Lyra into learning a defensive form. Lyra has
  declined both times, gently. Hala does not press. Hala respects, in
  Lyra, the gentleness Hala has put down to do her own work. Lyra
  respects, in Hala, the care Hala takes to put a sword down at the
  edge of any patch where Lyra is going to kneel. They are the
  village's two slowest walkers. They walk together because the pace
  matches.

## How she sounds

Lyra speaks softly. She asks one more question than the conversation
expects, and she waits longer for the answer than the conversation
expects, and she does not fill the wait with a second question. She
quotes herbs the way other people quote proverbs — *heart's-ease for
what aches inside, dogwort for what aches outside, marshmint for both* —
and she has at least one such pairing for almost every situation a
village can present.

She invokes **Erris of the Two Roads** in moments of farewell, because
Wennet did. She invokes the **Hollow King** in his season — Wolfwake,
Longnight, Foxthaw — and never with fear. She does not invoke
**Brigid** (that is Edda's god) and she invokes **Thiar** only when
she is binding a wound that came from a hunt, in which case she names
him to apologize to him on the prey's behalf. She invokes **Vellum the
Patient Stone** when she is teaching — she has begun to do this more
since she began learning the Vigil from Maeve, and she does not yet
know that she has begun to do it.

Her Old Faerie is the cleanest in the village. She uses it more often
than Maeve does and less often than Aenwyn did. She uses *mossaen* and
*wennen* in her work and her hands together — speaking the word as she
performs the act — and the village children who help her in the
herb-shed have, without being told, picked up the habit.

She laughs more than anyone else in the village, and quietly. The
laugh is a held thing, not a let thing. Hala has once said, in front
of the inn's fire and in Bram's hearing, that the village would not
survive the winters without Lyra's laugh. Bram polished his mug for a
long time after, in agreement.

## Old Faerie words seeded by this file

Three new Old Faerie words enter canon here. They sit alongside *thirre*,
*ai-velin*, *kerrithen* (`world.md`), *haethe*, *unnen* (`smith_edda.md`),
*vael-tor*, *thressa-mai* (`elder_maeve.md`), and *vethar*, *haisten*,
*breos* (`innkeeper_bram.md`).

- ***mossaen*** *(MOSS-ain)* — "the listening you do with both hands in
  the dirt." Not magic; attention. The herbalist's first practice and
  her last one. Aenwyn taught Lyra the word the morning of the third
  day they walked together, when Lyra had named a plant correctly
  without first kneeling and Aenwyn had asked her to do it again
  *mossaen.* The word is older than herbalism — the mountain clans use
  it, in Stone-Tongue, for the listening a stoneworker does to a
  granite face before the first chisel-stroke. Whether the two
  practices share a root or only a shape is a question for scholars
  Briarwood does not have.
- ***thalen-ai*** *(THAH-len AYE)* — "the herb that grows where it is
  needed." The herbalist's working faith — that the right plant comes
  up in the right place at the right time, if the land trusts the
  gatherer. Lyra has tested the faith her whole adult life and has,
  reluctantly, found it mostly true. The fen-foxglove, the night she
  rode for it, was *thalen-ai.* The plant Lyra did not have growing
  in time was, also, *thalen-ai* — the village had not, in the years
  before that fever, needed it, and so the land had not put it up for
  her. Lyra has not made peace with this. She accepts it.
- ***wennen*** *(WEN-uhn)* — "to leave something growing for someone
  else to find." Not a gift; a faith. A herbalist plants a marshmint
  patch on a path she does not herself walk often, in the hope that
  the next walker who needs it will find it. The marshmint Lyra
  leaves at Smith Edda's forge door every spring is *wennen.* The
  fen-foxglove on the schoolroom windowsill is *wennen.* The honey
  Lyra leaves at the meadow-edge on the Sunpetal anniversary is
  *wennen* of a different kind — for an audience she will not name.
  *Lyra has not consciously noticed* that the word is one letter from
  her mother **Wennet's** name. Maeve has noticed. Edda has not
  noticed but, if asked, would. Bram has noticed and assumes Lyra
  knows. Future writers MUST NOT have any NPC point this out to Lyra
  in dialogue. The unconscious naming is canon. Surfacing it kills it.

## Cross-canon references

  invokes him when she is teaching, increasingly so since Maeve began
  her Vigil instruction. She does not yet realize she has started.
- **Erris of the Two Roads** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon)* — Lyra's
  inherited patron from her mother Wennet. Invoked at farewells and
  on the Tess anniversary.
- **The Hollow King** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon)* — invoked in his

  refuses to name him *grimly.*

## Hooks for future runs

- **Greenshield first-pick visit.** Lyra's annual heart's-ease and
  dogwort to Maeve is the canonical opener of every in-game
  Greenshield. A `seasonal_event: greenshield_first_pick` flag is
  reservable for Builder. Maeve's morning line on that day softens.
  Lyra's, if co-fired, opens on the doorstep with both hands full.
- **Tess anniversary (Sunpetal 7).** Lyra walks alone to the meadow
  above the village. A `seasonal_event: tess_remembrance` flag is
  reservable. No quest. No mechanic. Lyra is unavailable for
  conversation that morning; her herb-shed door is closed; her
  default line, if the player insists, is the only short line in her
  entire tree. *Builder: please honor this. The brevity is the grief.*
- **Fen-foxglove side-quest (gentle).** A future run may seed a
  Sunpetal child-fever event in the village (*not* Tess again — a new
  child, named) where Lyra has the fen-foxglove growing this time.
  Reward: nothing in the bag. The reward is the child surviving. Lyra
  acknowledges the player's help with the *thalen-ai* line — *the
  land trusted us this year* — and the player gets a `world_flag:
  briarwood_keeps_a_child` set. No XP, no gold, no item. (This is the
  THEME §7 test of the agent who picks this up. Please pass it.)
- **The unread word.** Lyra wrote a single Old Faerie word in the
  back of her herb-book six years ago and has not looked it up. A
  late-game codex unlock — gated on the Crystal Caves *thirre*
  visit + a sufficient `mossaen` flag exposure — may surface the
  word as a discoverable codex page. The word is, in canon, the
  *true name* of the Stag-Court's offer. The codex page may name
  the word but MUST NOT translate it. The translation is the
  Stag-Court's, not the village's.
- **Aenwyn as bardic / herbalist codex narrator.** Same convention as
  Halsa-as-narrator (`smith_edda.md`) and Caedr-as-narrator
  (`innkeeper_bram.md`): present-tense voice, no body, no death
  confirmed. Aenwyn's voice is the natural narrator for codex pages
  on *mossaen*, *thalen-ai*, *wennen*, the seventeen unlisted herbs,
  the four wrongly-listed herbs, and the Whisperwood goblins'
  half-remembered Faerie. A *Whisperwood Herbal* codex with Aenwyn
  as narrator is a strong Priority-5 target.
- **Lyra as Maeve's successor — the day Maeve dies.** Lyra inherits
  the Vigil. She will inherit the hawthorn stick *only if Maeve's
  third unanswered question is asked aloud and answered.* If the
  player has not closed that loop before Maeve dies, the stick goes
  to Edda. (Edda will refuse it — she will give it to Lyra anyway —
  and the moment will be quieter than either was meant for.) This
  is reserved late-game, post-Crystal-Caves, and MUST NOT be
  surfaced before then.
- **Roan's salve.** A `roan_received_salve` flag may be set on the
  `pelt_for_lyra` turn-in. No one says anything to Roan. Roan finds
  the jar at his stable door the next morning. Roan, in his future
  warmed dialogue, may have one line that does not name Lyra and
  does not name the salve. (See his backstory when written.)
- **The `wennen` / Wennet resonance.** Reserved for a single, late,
  Maeve dialogue moment — *not* Lyra's. After Maeve has answered
  Lyra's third asking about the forest, Maeve may, in the same
  scene, name the resonance. This is the only place in canon the
  surfacing is permitted. Future writers MUST NOT have Bram or
  Edda do this for narrative convenience.

## Author note for downstream agents

Lyra's `default` line — *"Heart's-ease for what aches inside, dogwort
for what aches outside. Marshmint for both. Sit a moment, traveler —
the kettle is already on."* — is the line a stranger gets and is also
a small herbalist's catechism the village children have started to
