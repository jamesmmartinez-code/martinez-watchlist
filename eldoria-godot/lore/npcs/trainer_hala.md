# Trainer Hala

*Briarwood's only teacher of the body. Not the village's first defender —
that is Edda's anvil and Roan's bay and the lantern hanging at Maeve's
door — but the one who makes sure the village's children, when the
hour comes, will know how to set their feet. Lean, scarred, simple
linen wraps over old wool, a tall hawthorn staff with three small
stone-tongue oaths cut into the upper third where the hand goes. Walks
slow. Listens slower.*

> "Pick the staff up before the blade, traveler. The staff forgives. The blade does not."

---

## Where she grew up

Hala was born on the eastern lip of the High Steppe, in a foothill
hamlet called **Aurel** that the maps in Briarwood do not bother to
name. Aurel sat on the stone road between the **Stone Crown's** clan
country and the **Mountain Ring**, and the men of Aurel did not raise
horses or barley. They raised oath-keepers — staff-walkers, the
Steppe-clans called them, with a half-smile, because the oath-keepers
could neither be hired by the Iron Crown nor courted by the Stag-Court.
The Stone-Schools owe their tradition to **Vellum the Patient Stone**,
older than any of the Three Crowns; their staff-fighters carry no
edge, their cudgel-binders splice cord, and their oaths are cut not
spoken. The Steppe-clans called them *the slow ones.* The Stone-School
called itself the *Walker House*, in Stone-Tongue *aei-thurra* — *the
foot that returns.*

Hala's mother **Senne** was a binder — she cured leather, spliced
cudgels, kept the rope-walks. Hala's father **Garrim** was a Walker —
he traveled the stone road on quiet asking, brokering peace between
clans whose feuds would otherwise have salted the High Steppe a third
time. Hala had two older brothers, both Walkers in the line behind
their father. The brothers — **Tarric** and **Iann** — were sixteen
and fourteen when Hala was born; she remembers them as a doubled
shadow, a pair of laughs, a held-up hand on each side of her toddler's
unsteady step. They were already walking the road when Hala was old
enough to hold a staff.

The **Walker House** trained children from the year they could stand.
First the staff — never the blade. The first oath cut into a Walker's
staff is *aei*, *the foot.* The second is *thurra*, *to return.* The
third — and a Walker carries no more than three — is the oath of *what
they have failed to defend.* The third oath is cut by the Walker's own
hand. It is not chosen. It is admitted.

Hala's third oath is *kel-vethran*, *the lesson taught against the
hand.* She cut it into her staff alone in a cold barn in Wolfwake,
the year she turned twenty-six, the year Aurel burned.

She has been in Briarwood for eleven years now. She is forty-two.
She has not gone back to Aurel and will not.

## A formative loss

Aurel did not burn in war. The Iron Crown's pikemen never came that
far north and the Stag-Court's quarrel was older. Aurel burned because
of a frost.

In the Wolfwake Hala turned twenty-six, the High Steppe took a frost
that opened the old crack along the Mountain Ring's eastern face, and
out of the crack came a pack of wolves no Walker had counted before —
gaunt, frost-rimed, and *led.* The thing leading them was not a wolf.
The Stone-School's oldest scrolls call them *bren-thurra* — *the
turned-foot,* a wolf who has walked far enough wrong to start
remembering it was something else. Hala will not say *what* it was.
Lyra, who has read certain pages of the *Quench-Ledger* in Edda's
loft, suspects. Hala has not confirmed.

The pack came down the stone road at dusk. Aurel had no walls. It had
the Walker House and the rope-walks and the small school where Hala —
a journeyman that year, the youngest Walker on the road — was
teaching the village's children the **first form** of the staff. The
form is called *ostren-rae,* *the kept stall.* It is not a hitting
form. It is a standing form. Children of nine learn it because it
teaches them how to keep their feet when something taller than them
is leaning in.

Hala had eleven children in the school that evening. She had her
staff, her brother **Iann's** old cudgel — the only edged thing in
the room was a kitchen knife on a high shelf — and one window onto
the road.

Tarric and Garrim met the pack on the road. They held it.
Senne and the village mothers got eight of the children out the back
door of the school and into the rope-walk barn. Hala held the door of
the school for the other three with the staff and Iann's cudgel and a
voice that was not, by then, a voice she recognized. The bren-thurra
came over the threshold and Hala made it stop.

She does not say how. The scar that runs from the corner of her
left eye to the line of her jaw is from that night. The other —
the long one across her right forearm, that aches in Foxthaw — is
also. She got the three children to the rope-walk. She got the
rope-walk to the high pasture. She did not get back to her father
and her brother and her mother and the eight others.

The frost took the rest of Aurel that night. The Stone-School's
elders, when they came down the road four days later, found the
Walker House standing and Hala standing in front of it, Iann's
cudgel in one hand and her staff in the other and the three children
behind her. The elders did not ask what had happened to the
bren-thurra. They cut their own third oaths. Hala cut hers.

She walked south alone in Smokerise. She had Tarric's staff and
Iann's cudgel and Senne's rope-binder's awl and a small folded
square of cured deer-hide from the Walker House on which the names
of the eight children she had not gotten to the barn were written
in Senne's hand. She walked four moons. She came down out of the
foothills in early Lambmoon. She came around the western shoulder
of the mountain ring and saw a small valley with a thatched-roof
village in it and a campfire burning low at its center, and Stable-
master **Roan** was standing at the upper field-gate with a torch,
because that is what Roan does at dusk.

Roan did not ask her name. He took her staff from her at the gate
because she was so tired she could not hold it. He gave it back at
the door of Bram's inn. Bram poured her a small beer, gave her a
bowl of stew, and pointed at the loft above the taproom. Hala slept
fourteen hours. She woke up in Briarwood and decided to stay.

That was eleven years ago. She has not told anyone in Briarwood the
full count of names on the deer-hide. She has told Lyra two of them.

## A quieter wound

Hala had a sword once. She does not now.

It belonged to her brother Tarric. Tarric was the only Walker in
their line who had ever taken up an edge — a southern saber he had
brought back from a brokering-walk among the Iron Crown's outlying
captains, against the Walker House's quiet disapproval. Tarric had
joked about it. He had named it *Frost.* He had quenched it in the
mountain stream above Aurel and laughed at how cold it had come out.

After Aurel, Hala carried Frost south for two seasons. She did not
draw it. She stood with it slung at her hip on the stone road
through three border crossings and a Steppe-clan moot and one
Whisperwood thirre, and at no point did her hand close on the
hilt.

In Greenshield of the year she came down to Briarwood, on the
afternoon she walked past a small grey cairn three days' ride above
the valley, Hala laid Frost down on the cairn. She *kerritha*-ed
it. She did not say a word over it. She walked on.

She does not know who, if anyone, has found it since. She suspects
it is still there. She has not gone back. *(The cairn is on the
high road between the High Steppe and Briarwood. If Builder ever
wires a far-travel Steppe-arc, the cairn is canonical and Frost is
canonical and finding it is a quest worth the silence it would
require.)*

This is the wound she does not name. The Aurel scar she will, on
slow nights, point to. Frost on the cairn she will not.

## A secret she keeps

The third oath cut into a Walker's staff is private. The other
Walkers of the Walker House — those still living, scattered south —
each carry their own *kel-* oath, and none of them has ever asked
another. Hala has shown the upper third of her staff to no one in
Briarwood. *Aei.* *Thurra.* *Kel-vethran.* The first two are clean
old runic cuts; the third is rougher. A Walker is supposed to cut
the third oath at the moment the failure is admitted. Hala cut hers
in the dark, with Iann's cudgel-binding awl, in a barn in Wolfwake,
and the cut is not pretty.

What she has not said is that she translated *kel-vethran* wrong.
The proper Stone-Tongue is *kel-vethran* — *the lesson admitted.* A
Walker admits, in the third oath, the thing they failed to do. Hala
should have cut the names of the eight children, or *the eighth
form, the form she had not yet taught them.* What she cut, instead,
was a teaching-promise: *the lesson taught against the hand.* It is
the oath of a teacher who has decided that the only redress for
what she has failed to defend is to teach what she has, and that
teaching what she has will sometimes go against her own grain. *To
teach Lyra a hold-break against Lyra's gentleness. To put a sword
in a pupil's hand when the pupil has earned the right to choose the
edge. To stand the staff aside when Bram is right that a blade is
sometimes a tool.*

The Walker elders, if they ever read her staff, would say she had
cut the wrong oath. Hala does not know whether they would be right.

## What she wants most

For the children of Briarwood — Lyra-grown, Edda-grown, Mara's
nieces, the ones who have not yet been born — never to need to know
what *ostren-rae* is for. For one of her cudgels, eventually, to be
swung in earnest by a hand she has trained, against a thing she has
not yet seen, and for that hand to come home.

For the row of six cudgels at Mara's stall to stay six. For the one
in Roan's tack-room rafters to keep gathering dust. For the small
square of cured deer-hide in the bottom of her trunk to lose, very
slowly, the names written on it — the way the *thirre* is supposed
to soften the past.

For her staff to be carried, on the day she dies, to the cairn
above the valley by whichever pupil she has trusted longest, and
laid down beside Frost, and *kerritha*-ed there. She has not said
this aloud. She has, possibly, said it to her staff.

## Relationships

- **Elder Maeve.** The deepest respect of Hala's life in Briarwood
  is for Maeve's *no.* When Hala asked Maeve to bless a sword-pupil
  in her third year, Maeve said, *Vellum keeps memory, not edges,*
  and Hala — for the first time since Aurel — felt the right
  refusal land in the right place. They have walked together to
  **Thiar's stone** every Stag-night since. Neither is a hunter.
  Both have buried more than they will say. Maeve does not invoke
  Vellum aloud at the stone; Hala does not cut a fourth oath on her
  staff. They share the silence the way two old binders share a
  rope — one hand each, weight equal. Maeve is the only person in
  Briarwood who has guessed, in plain words, what the third oath
  is *of.* Maeve has not spoken her guess. Hala does not need her to.
- **Smith Edda.** The laughter is what they share. Hala once tried,
  in her fifth Briarwood year, to talk Edda into picking up a sword
  — not for use, only for the form of it, the *torrest,* the held
  edge — and Edda laughed for the first time in a long season. Hala
  understood the laugh as a *no* and let it be one. They are the
  village's two slowest-to-laugh adults. When Edda does laugh,
  Hala laughs with her. When Hala does, Edda's anvil-hammer falls
  one beat late. Neither has said how rare this is.
- **Innkeeper Bram.** Bram and Hala have, for nine years, argued
  the same argument: a blade is a tool, says Bram; a blade is an
  oath, says Hala. Neither has moved an inch. Bram thinks this is
  a kind of friendship. Hala does too. The argument always
  starts at the same line — *Hala, what would you do if a man came
  through that door with bad intent* — and ends, every time, with
  Bram pouring her a small beer and Hala saying *I would put my
  hand on the staff and ask his name first,* and Bram saying *and
  if he didn't give it,* and Hala saying *then I'd ask twice.*
  They have not yet had a third question. Hala believes Bram is
  saving it.
- **Stablemaster Roan.** Roan walked Hala to the door of the inn
  the night she came down out of the foothills and has not, in the
  eleven years since, asked her where she came from. Hala has not,
  in eleven years, asked Roan about Bron. Each of them keeps the
  other's silence. Roan keeps one of Hala's hand-bound cudgels
  laid across the tack-room rafters; he has never lifted it down.
  Hala has never asked why. *Cudgel-acknowledged*, in the small
  internal language of the village's bridges, between them. The
  bay mare in Roan's middle stall — **Caer-thur** — is Steppe-
  blood through the withers, and Roan has, twice, brought her up
  to the inn for Hala when Hala has needed to go far. Neither
  trip is named in this file. Both happened.
- **Mara the Merchant.** The row of six cudgels against the back
  wall of Mara's stall is the village's oldest unspoken kindness.
  Hala makes the cudgels. Mara stocks them. Twice a year Hala
  brings six fresh ones; twice a year the row on Mara's wall is
  six. Hala does not know whether Mara is selling them or quietly
  carrying them back to Hala's door at night. Mara does not know
  whether Hala believes she is. Both of them need the row of six
  to exist. *(Lore Keeper: the cudgels do go out, sometimes — to
  Steppe-riders Mara meets on the south road, to a Whisperwood
  forester or two, once to a child in a wagon who would not stop
  asking. Mara does not tell Hala, because Mara is not sure
  whether Hala wants them used. Hala does want them used. Both of
  them are wrong about each other in exactly the way that holds a
  village together.)*
- **Herbalist Lyra.** Hala walks with Lyra to the meadow above the
  village at every planting moon. Hala digs; Lyra names. Hala has,
  twice in ten years, tried to talk Lyra into learning a defensive
  form. Lyra has declined both times, gently. Hala did not press.
  What Lyra did accept, one Greenshield evening at Lyra's request
  — not Hala's — was *how to break a hold without hurting the
  holder.* Lyra learned it in three afternoons in the meadow and
  has not, to Hala's knowledge, ever needed it. Hala calls the
  lesson *vethran,* *the lesson taught against the hand,* and
  considers it the truest piece of teaching she has done in
  Briarwood. Lyra calls it *kindness.* Both are right. They are
  the village's two slowest walkers. When they walk together it
  is at Lyra's pace, because Hala has the back for it and Lyra
  does not.
- **The player.** Hala does not speak first to a stranger. She
  watches them cross the village square and notes how their feet
  fall — heel-first, toe-first, even, lopsided. She will offer a
  staff to a player whose feet *lopsidedly* fall: the staff
  evens the gait. To a player whose feet are even, she will
  watch and wait. The first lesson is always *ostren-rae* — the
  kept stall, the standing form. The lesson is free. The blade,
  if the player asks for it, is *torrest:* held, sheathed, and
  not yet drawn. Hala will draw a blade for a pupil only after
  the pupil has stood *ostren-rae* for one full Whisperwood
  patrol without taking a step backward. Most do not, in the
  first year. Most do, in the second.

## How she sounds

Hala speaks slowly and in short sentences. Each sentence ends a beat
before you expect it to, and the beat is the lesson. She uses *staff*
the way other people use *hand* — *put your staff down,* meaning *be
still,* or *raise your staff,* meaning *be ready,* or simply *staff,*
meaning *I have heard you and I am thinking.* She does not raise her
voice. Her *no* is a quiet word she lets sit in the air for a heartbeat
longer than Common would expect, the way Stone-Tongue lets every
syllable bear its own weight.

She invokes **Vellum the Patient Stone** in moments of weight, but
sparingly — and never by way of asking. Vellum is *witnessed,* not
prayed to. Hala will say, *as Vellum keeps,* when she takes an oath of
her own; she will not say, *Vellum grant me.* Maeve does the same. They
caught each other doing it once in the second year and neither has
remarked on it.

She uses Old Faerie rarely. She has it cleanly — most Walkers learn
both Stone-Tongue and Old Faerie young, because the stone road runs
along the edge of the Whisperwood-that-becomes-fey, and Walkers must
sometimes broker on the *other* side of that line. She uses Old
Faerie with Lyra (who recognizes it) and with Maeve (who hears the
older constructions). She does not use it with Bram (who does not
hear it) or Edda (who hears the words and chooses to hear them as
work-words instead). With strangers, she uses Common, and only the
two Stone-Tongue words she has earned the right to cut: *aei* and
*thurra.* The third she has cut is hers and hers alone.

Her laugh, when it comes, is short and unexpectedly bright. It is the
laugh of someone who has decided, on purpose, that laughter survives.
Lyra has said the village would not survive winter without Lyra's
laugh; Hala has said it. Hala's own laugh she does not count.

## Old Faerie words seeded by this file

Two new Old Faerie words enter canon here. They sit alongside *thirre,
ai-velin, kerrithen* (`world.md`); *haethe, unnen* (`smith_edda.md`);
*vethar, haisten, breos* (`innkeeper_bram.md`); *pendrel, mhairen*
(`mara_merchant.md`); *vael-tor, thressa-mai* (`elder_maeve.md`);
*maelorn, ostren* (`stablemaster_roan.md`).

- ***torrest*** *(TOR-est)* — "the held edge." A blade kept sheathed
  not from cowardice but from oath. The discipline of carrying a
  weapon and choosing not to draw it. A Walker term, borrowed into
  Old Faerie through the long border between the High Steppe and
  the Whisperwood-that-becomes-fey. Treated grammatically as a
  thing a person *holds* across a span of time, not a single
  decision in a moment — *to keep torrest* is the verb, *to break
  torrest* its undoing. Hala carries Tarric's saber south in
  *torrest* before laying it on the cairn. Bram and Hala's nine-
  year argument is, fundamentally, about whether *torrest* is a
  living oath or a stuck habit. Bram thinks the latter. Hala thinks
  the former. Both of them are aware they think this.
- ***vethran*** *(VETH-run)* — "the lesson taught against the
  hand." A teaching done because the student needs it more than
  the teacher likes giving it. Distinct from *unnen* (the work of
  two hands, made in love) — *vethran* is the work of one hand
  done over the teacher's own preference. Used by Stone-School
  binders for the cudgel-knot a binder dislikes but the rope
  demands; by Walkers for the form taught to a pupil who has not
  yet earned it but cannot wait for it; by herbalists, after Hala,
  for a remedy made from a plant the herbalist holds sacred. The
  third oath Hala cut into her staff is the verb-form, *kel-
  vethran,* "to teach against the hand," and is the only Stone-
  Tongue oath the Walker elders, if they ever read it, would
  consider mis-cut.

## Cross-canon references

- **Vellum the Patient Stone** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon)* — Hala's
  witnessed god. Walkers do not pray *to* Vellum; they walk *as
  Vellum keeps.* Hala's staff carries three Stone-Tongue oaths
  consonant with this; her staff is *not* blessed in the way
  Maeve's hawthorn stick is *named* by the Vigil. Maeve's "Vellum
  keeps memory, not edges" line in `elder_maeve.md` is the
  canonical bridge between Maeve's Vellum-keeping and Hala's.
- **The Stone Crown / High Steppe** *(world.md, Three Crowns)* —
  Aurel sits on the eastern lip of Steppe country. Walkers are
  *neighbor* to the Stone Crown, not bound to it. The Stone Crown
  pays Walkers no tax; Walkers swear no clan-oath. Hala's
  Steppe-blood-mare history with Roan's bay **Caer-thur** is the
  small horse-bridge between Walker and Stone Crown country. The
  cairn three days' ride above Briarwood, on which Tarric's saber
  *Frost* is laid, is on the Steppe-side of the high road and is
  reusable for any Steppe-arc.
- **The Sundering / Pale Wyrm** *(world.md)* — the *bren-thurra*
  pack that destroyed Aurel came down out of the eastern crack in
  the Mountain Ring after a frost. Lore Keeper does not name what
  led the pack. Hala does not. Lyra suspects from a page of the
  *Quench-Ledger* in Edda's loft. Future writers should preserve
  the silence; the Pale Wyrm is *kept asleep by silence,* per
  Edda's file, and the bren-thurra are not unrelated.
- **Brigid the Forge-Mother** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon; smith_edda.md)*
  — Hala does not invoke Brigid. Edda does. The laugh Edda once
  gave Hala — when Hala asked if Edda would pick up a sword — was,
  in Brigid's hearing, a *no.* Hala honored it. The cudgel
  triangle between Hala, Mara, and Roan deliberately does not
  pass through Edda. Brigid does not consecrate practice cudgels.
- **The Whisperwood / dire-wolves** *(world.md; WORLD_STATE.md
  faction-pressure thread; stablemaster_roan.md)* — Hala has *not*
  walked the south paths. Roan has. Hala teaches the village's
  children to set their feet for what comes out of the trees;
  Roan rides what comes out of the trees down. Their division of
  labor is canonical: Hala teaches, Roan rides, Edda forges, Lyra
  binds, Bram pours, Mara carries, Maeve names. Future quest
  writing should let this division hold.
- **Longnight Vigil** *(world.md, Calendar; elder_maeve.md)* —
  Hala stands at the well's south side on Longnight, beside the
  candle for *the eight,* which is hers and lit by her hand. She
  has never named the candle aloud. Maeve places it beside the
  village's own candle without comment. Bram brings her stew. Hala
  does not eat hers either. She sets it at the well's stone for
  the Hollow King.
- **Stag-night** *(world.md, Calendar; elder_maeve.md)* — Hala
  walks with Maeve to **Thiar's stone** in Emberfall every year.
  The lay of a bow at Thiar's stone is the hunters' rite; Hala
  and Maeve, who are not hunters, lay nothing. They stand. They
  walk back. The *vael-tor-i,* the *we*-form Maeve uses at the
  Vigil, is the form Maeve and Hala walk in on this night. Hala
  has not said so aloud. She does not need to.
- **The Walker House (Aurel)** *(this file)* — a fourth political
  body adjacent to but distinct from the Three Crowns. Walkers
  serve Vellum-witnessed peace between the Stone Crown's clans;
  they predate the Iron Crown's standing armies and the Stag-
  Court's seasons. They are not numerous. After Aurel, they are
  fewer. This is the canonical seed for any future Hala-related
  arc that wants the wider world to lean back toward her.

## Hooks for future runs

- **A first quest from Hala** fits as a *training* quest — the
  player stands *ostren-rae* against a small dummy on the green
  for a measured count, returns at dusk for a corrected stance,
  returns at dawn for the held form. Reward: a hand-bound
  practice cudgel from the row of six. Mark `cudgel_acknowledged`
  on the player. Hala does not, even after, hand the player a
  blade. The blade comes from Edda. The right to *carry* it,
  sheathed, is *torrest,* and that is Hala's gift.
- **Tarric's saber on the cairn** is the deepest reusable hook in
  Hala's file. A Steppe-side travel arc — far north, three days'
  ride above the valley — can surface the cairn as a discoverable
  *thirre.* Finding *Frost* is not a combat encounter; it is a
  silence and a choice. The player may take it, leave it, or
  *kerritha* it again. Hala will respond to all three differently
  in dialogue and *not* by quest-text. (Edda's file already
  implies that Frost-named steel listens to the wrong sleeper.
  Lore Keeper insists the player should not be told this in plain
  words. The codex entry *Halsa's Quench-Ledger* and a future
  *Walker's Third Oath* fragment may, between them, let the player
  arrive at it.)
- **The deer-hide of names** in Hala's trunk is a discoverable
  codex object — *"The Eight,"* a one-page entry naming, in
  Senne's hand, the eight children Hala did not get to the rope-
  walk barn. This entry should not be discovered casually. A
  future *Hala trust* flag, at high renown, can surface it. Hala
  is not present in the scene. Maeve is.
- **The Walker's Third Oath** is its own codex entry — a one-page
  fragment, in Stone-Tongue, listing the formal third oaths cut
  by named Walkers across the years. Hala's *kel-vethran* is on
  it. The page does not say whether the elders consider it a
  mis-cut. The reader may decide.
- **Caer-thur, the Steppe bay** *(stablemaster_roan.md, hooks)* —
  if Builder ever wires a far-travel beat for Hala (the cairn,
  the Walker House survivors south, a Steppe-clan moot), the bay
  is the canonical horse and Roan is the canonical sender. Hala
  does not own the horse. The bay is *ostren* — kept for her.
- **Cudgel-row acknowledged** is reciprocal between Hala, Mara,
  and Roan. Builder may set `cudgel_acknowledged` between any
  two of the three at any time, no quest required, per
  `stablemaster_roan.md` and `mara_merchant.md`.
- **The Aurel survivors.** Beyond Hala there are, by the
  Walker House's records, between two and five other Walkers
  living. Lore Keeper has not named them. A future quest may
  bring one through Briarwood — slowly, on foot, with a staff and
  a small folded square of cured deer-hide — and the recognition
  scene between that Walker and Hala is one of the most
  expensive moments any future writer can spend. Spend it well.

## Author note for downstream agents

Hala's default `dialogue` line in `WorldBuilder.NPCS` is preserved —
it is the line a stranger should still receive. The richer mood +
flag tree will live, when it ships, in
`eldoria-godot/data/dialogue/trainer_hala.json`. As with the other
six NPCs, this `.md` is canonical *intent;* the `.json` will be the
canonical *surface* the Builder/Polisher wires NPC.gd against.

Hala's voice is among the slowest in the village — at Lyra's pace
when they walk, at Maeve's pace when they stand, at her own pace
when she teaches. Builder should not shorten her pauses; the
pauses are the lesson. Polisher should not warm her cadence past
where Maeve's sits; she runs cooler than Bram, warmer than Edda,
beside Maeve.

She will not, ever, name the *bren-thurra* aloud, and she will not
draw a sword in the village. If a future writer is tempted to make
her do either — please do not. The held thing *is* the character.

The cudgel triangle (Hala / Mara / Roan) is now closed canon. The
Stag-night walk (Hala / Maeve) is now closed canon. The nine-year
argument (Hala / Bram) is now closed canon. The hold-break lesson
(Hala / Lyra) is now closed canon. The shared laugh (Hala / Edda)
is now closed canon. Future writers may add weight to any of these

Hala is the seventh of seven Briarwood NPC backstories. Lore Keeper
has held her to the same shape as the others — *Where she grew up,
A formative loss, A secret she keeps, What she wants most,
Relationships, How she sounds, Old Faerie words, Cross-canon
references, Hooks, Author note* — so that any later pass can read
the seven side by side and find the village whole.
