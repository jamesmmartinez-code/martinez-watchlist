# Smith Edda

*Briarwood village smith. Master of the only forge inside the mountain ring.
Wears Brigid the Forge-Mother's mark stamped on her anvil and a smaller copy
of it on the inside of her left wrist, where no one can see.*

> "Bring me ore. I forge best when the dew's still on the iron."

---

## Where she grew up

Edda was born in Briarwood and has never left it for more than a season. Her
mother — **Halsa**, also a smith — was the one who taught her to listen for
the *haethe*, the song iron is supposed to make when it has been quenched
honestly. Halsa called the forge "the only room in the world where Brigid
keeps her promises."

Halsa died of the winter cough on the Longnight Edda turned fifteen,
leaving Edda the anvil, the bellows, the soot on the ceiling, and a knife
with no handle.

Edda has finished the knife. She does not use it. It hangs by the forge,
next to her mother's tongs.

## A formative loss — and a quieter victory

For two years after Halsa's death, Edda forged badly. She was angry, and
iron knows. Three of the blades she made in those years broke in service.
The worst — a saber sold to a southern rider who came north on bounty work
— broke in the **Goblin Warlord's** hand on the day the rider died, and was
recovered, *re-hilted*, and now hangs at the Warlord's belt. Edda has never
told anyone she made it. *(Bram knows. Bram has never said.)*

The day Edda realized that iron was honest about her grief was the day she
started forging well again. She was seventeen. She quenched a blade in
pond-water under the midsummer moon and it sang true — its *haethe* clear
as a struck bell. She wept, banked the coals, and slept by the anvil for a
week.

## A secret she keeps

Brigid speaks to smiths in dreams. Most smiths do not believe it. Edda was
told, in a dream the year Halsa died, to hammer a single drop of her blood
into every blade meant for a friend. She has done so for thirteen years. She
does not know if it does anything. She has stopped asking. Children who
notice the small white scar under her thumb get told she pinched it in the
bellows once. They believe her. Bram does not.

## What she wants most

An apprentice. Briarwood does not send its children to the forge any more —
the Iron Crown's cheap southern steel makes the trade look poor — and Edda
has waited eight years for someone to ask. She tells herself she is too
hard, too rough, too Longnight-marked for a young hand to want the work.

The truth is that she has not yet *asked aloud*. To ask is *unnen*, in the
Old Faerie tongue: a work of two hands. Edda is not yet ready for two hands.

## Relationships

- **Innkeeper Bram.** Bram brings her stew every Longnight Vigil. He says
  it's "what Edda's mother used to make." It isn't. Edda lets him say it.
  They are not lovers and have never been; they are something quieter and
  older — the way two people who have kept vigil beside each other for a
  long time become.
- **Stablemaster Roan.** Edda re-shoes Roan's horses at half-rate. Roan
  pretends not to notice. They are kind to each other in the manner of
  people who know they will both die in this village.
- **Elder Maeve.** Maeve once brought Edda a cradle that had belonged to
  Halsa. Edda had not asked for it. Maeve did not stay. Edda keeps the
  cradle in the forge loft, full of charcoal sacks, and visits it at the
  turning of every season.
- **Mara the Merchant.** Mara buys Edda's *seconds* — the work that did not
  ring true — and sells them on as field-tools. Edda does not love this
  but accepts it. Mara pays in honest coin and never haggles, and Edda
  respects exactly those two things in a person.
- **Herbalist Lyra.** Lyra leaves marshmint at the forge door every spring.
  Edda has never thanked her in words. She sends Lyra a new cleaver every
  Foxthaw. Both of them call this a debt. Both of them are wrong.
- **Trainer Hala.** Hala once tried to talk Edda into picking up a sword.
  Edda laughed for the first time in a season, and the laughter is what
  they share now.

## How she sounds

Edda speaks in short sentences and long pauses. She uses *\*hammer-clang\**
the way other people use *um*. She is more tender than she sounds and less
patient than she looks. She invokes Brigid the Forge-Mother as she works,
sometimes in Common ("Brigid keep this") and sometimes in older syllables
that Lyra recognizes and Bram does not.

She has never named a blade *Frost*. That is a southern name, and the Pale
Wyrm — per the Sundering canon — is kept asleep *by silence*, not by
speeches. The smiths who do name a saber Frost have not been raised by
Halsa.

## Old Faerie words seeded by this file

Two new Old Faerie words enter canon here. Future writers may use them in
codex entries, dialogue, or item flavor without re-defining them. They sit
alongside *thirre*, *ai-velin*, and *kerrithen* from `lore/world.md`.

- ***haethe*** *(HEY-thuh)* — "the song iron remembers." The faint hum a
  properly-tempered blade makes when struck on the anvil. Old smiths swear
  the *haethe* of a blade tells whether it was forged in love or in fear.
  Halsa taught Edda to listen for it before any other lesson.
- ***unnen*** *(UH-nun)* — "the work of two hands." What one person makes
  alone but as if two were holding the hammer. Used for things made in
  grief, in prayer, or in waiting. To call something *unnen* is the
  highest praise a smith of the old tradition can give. Edda has not yet
  called any of her own work this.

## Cross-canon references

- **Brigid the Forge-Mother** *(world.md, Wild Pantheon)* — Edda's patron.
  Her mark is stamped on Edda's anvil and tattooed inside Edda's wrist.
- **The Sundering / Pale Wyrm** *(world.md)* — the reason Edda will not
  name a blade Frost. She knows what frost-named steel listens to.
- **Longnight Vigil** *(world.md, Calendar)* — both Halsa's death-night and
  the night Bram brings stew. The most loaded day in Edda's year.

## Hooks for future runs

- A first quest from Edda fits naturally as a *fetch* — coal from the
  **Crystal Caves**, or ore from a vein only Roan's horses can reach.
  Reward: one of the unsold apprentice-pieces she has held back, with a
  small flavor note that this is the first blade she has ever given away.
- The *haethe* is a discoverable concept worth its own codex entry — e.g.
  *"Halsa's Quench-Ledger,"* found in Edda's loft after a sufficient
  renown threshold. That entry would be the canonical place to deepen the
  dream-instruction (the blood drop) without Edda ever saying it aloud.
- The Warlord's saber carries a fingerprint of Edda's grief in it. If
  Builder ever wires `boss_slain` to drop a unique blade, that blade IS
  this saber, and turning it in to Edda completes a private arc. Use
  sparingly — Longnight-only beat. Her `boss_slain` line in the dialogue
  tree is already pre-tuned for this turn-in.
- Edda's mother **Halsa** is dead but seedable — Halsa's voice in dreams
  is a reasonable narrator for codex entries about iron, the *haethe*, and
  the rule against naming blades Frost.
