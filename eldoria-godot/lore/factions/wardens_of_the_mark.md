# The Wardens of the Mark

*The first faction-politics canon written from inside Briarwood. Companion
to `lore/factions/three_crowns.md`, which establishes the political frame
from outside (the three Crowns and their postures toward the valley);
this document establishes how the valley names itself when it remembers
it has been left to itself.*

*Tone: warm gravitas, child-safe (THEME §7). The Wardens are not an army.
They are not a guild. They are a posture the village wears without
saying so aloud. This file does not turn them into anything louder.*

---

## What the Wardens are

The Wardens of the Mark are **the four people in Briarwood who, between
them, hold what the Crowns have not.** They do not call themselves the
Wardens. The village does not call them the Wardens. The phrase exists in
exactly one written place — see *Bram's grandmother's ledger*, below —
and is otherwise an old breath that moves under the village's days.

They are not appointed. They are not elected. They are not paid by any
purse. They have, between the four of them, eight hands and four old
tools, and the eight hands are the village's whole standing answer to
the question *what is between the Whisperwood and the children at the
well?*

The four are:

- **Smith Edda** — *the keeping-warm.* Her anvil, her hammer, the
  small white scar under her thumb. *(NPC bible:
  `lore/npcs/smith_edda.md`.)*
- **Stablemaster Roan** — *the keeping-watch.* The bay in the front
  stall who sees the south road, his hand on her halter at first
  light, his nine years of courier-eyes. *(NPC bible:
  `lore/npcs/stablemaster_roan.md`.)*
- **Elder Maeve** — *the keeping-vigil.* The lantern at her door, the
  Vigil candles she lights every Longnight, the eighty-ninth knot in
  the Elder's rope. *(NPC bible: `lore/npcs/elder_maeve.md`.)*
- **Trainer Hala** — *the keeping-still.* The hawthorn staff with the
  Stone-Tongue oaths cut into it, the first form she teaches every
  child, the long pause she holds when the village asks her a hard
  thing. *(NPC bible: `lore/npcs/trainer_hala.md`.)*

These four names are the canon of the Wardens. They are not a roster
that grows on a normal year. **They grow only when one of the four can
no longer carry their tool.** That is the whole rule of the bench.

## *Aen-velin-corr,* looked at from the inside

`lore/factions/three_crowns.md` establishes that the Antler-King's
phrase for the valley is **aen-velin-corr** — *the small-long-path that
has not been bought.* The Court's word. From the Court's side. From
*outside.*

From inside, the same condition has a Common-tongue name. The village
does not, of itself, recognize that it is named. *The Wardens of the
Mark* is what an old hand will call the four when an even older hand
asks. Maeve, asked, would say: *"It is not a title. It is what we
notice."* Hala, asked, would not answer in words; she would set the
staff down and look at where the staff fell. Edda, asked, would
*hammer-clang* and say: *"Names cost coal. Ask Bram."* Roan would say:
*"There's no Wardens. There's only the bay. Ask the bay."*

This is the shape. The Wardens do not believe in themselves as
Wardens. The condition believes in them.

## The Mark

The Mark is **what they watch, not where.** It is not a line on the
ground. It is not painted on a tree. It is not a banner-pole in the
village square. (There are three banner-poles in the village square —
see `lore/world.md` §Briarwood Village. None of them is a Warden's
flag. None of them is going to be.)

The Mark, in canon, is the village's *attention,* held in four
directions at once:

- **South** — Roan's direction. The road from the smoke-cities and the
  signpost twenty leagues out. The bay's ears.
- **North** — Hala's direction. The mountain ring, the high passes, and
  whatever weather the Stone Crown rides through on. The staff-side.
- **West** — Maeve's direction. The Whisperwood and the forest-line
  the children are told never to follow on a Foxthaw night. The
  Vigil-side.
- **East** — Edda's direction. The Crystal Caves under the hill and
  the slow under-stream that smells of cold iron. The anvil-side.

Four directions, four hands, four tools. Each Warden, when standing in
the village, is *standing toward their direction without thinking about
it.* This is canon. Future Builder runs may, on a future seasonal-flavor
tier, gate one quiet idle-orientation rule on this: each Warden's
default facing should, on a clean day, line up with their direction.
*(Hooks below.)*

## The four civic unwritten oaths

The village keeps four short sentences that are not, by themselves,
oaths. They are *what is said* when something has gone wrong enough that
the next thing has to be done. They are not chanted. They are not sworn.
An old villager will say one — quietly, almost to themselves — and the
village will know what to do.

The four:

- ***"The candle waits."*** — Maeve's. Said when something is owed to
  someone who is not in the village to receive it. Said most often on
  Longnight Vigil, but said also on smaller nights — when a courier
  has not come north in four Foxthaws, when a child has not come home
  before the second bell. The village answers by lighting one extra
  lantern at the well. No one has named the lantern after anything.
  No one needs to.
- ***"The bay sees."*** — Roan's. Said when news has reached the
  stable yard before it has reached the inn — a Stone-rider on the
  pass, a southern cart on the signpost, a stranger at the field-gate.
  The village answers by sending the youngest available pair of feet
  to the well to draw fresh water (which is the village's old signal
  for *prepare to receive a guest, kindly*). The bay does not, in
  canon, see anything magical. She is just where she is, and Roan
  trusts what she pricks her ears at.
- ***"The hammer answers."*** — Edda's. Said when a thing has broken
  that the village did not yet know was load-bearing. A latch, a
  hinge, a wheel-rim, a roof-beam, a child's first-and-only knife.
  The village answers by carrying the broken thing to the forge. Edda
  works late that night and Bram brings her stew and she does not, by
  the village's polite rule, charge for the work until the next quench
  has rung true. *(Cross-ref: `npcs/smith_edda.md`, *the haethe.*)*
- ***"The staff keeps."*** — Hala's. Said when nothing should be done
  and someone with their hand on a sword has not yet understood that
  yet. Said most often by Hala herself, said sometimes by Maeve, said
  rarely by anyone else. The village answers by *not answering* — by
  staying where they are, doing what they were doing, letting the
  half-second of stillness widen. Hala holds the staff. The blade, on
  the wrong day, *does not* come out. This is the oath.

These are not sworn. They are inherited the way bread-recipes are
inherited. They are what the village means by *being a village.* Old
Briarwood hands know all four. Children learn them by hearing them said
into older shoulders. Strangers do not, unless they stay a season.

## Bram's grandmother's ledger

The phrase *"Wardens of the Mark"* is written in the village exactly
once, in a small red kid-leather ledger that Bram keeps under the bar
at the **Singing Hearth** *(the inn)*. The ledger is not an inn ledger.
It is older. Bram inherited it from his grandmother **Halsa** *(no
relation to Edda's mother of the same name; Common is a small tongue
and the name was popular three generations ago)*, who inherited it from
her grandmother before her.

The ledger holds one entry per generation, written in the elder hand
of that generation's village. Bram's grandmother's entry, in a slow
country script, reads — and this is the only direct quote of the
phrase admissible in canon:

> *Wardens of the Mark, this year: Edda's mother Halsa at the forge;
> Roan's father's father Bren at the stable; Maeve's mother Aelis the
> Elder at the lantern; the Walker-house woman of Aurel at the staff.
> The valley is unbought. The Mark is held.*

Four lines, four names, no flourishes. (The Walker-house woman is not
named because Bram's grandmother did not know her name; this is the
canonical seed for Hala's predecessor and a hook the
`trainer_hala.md` tradition can grow into without contradiction.)

The ledger is one page per generation. Bram has not written this
generation's entry. He will, when the village's elders meet next on
the eighty-ninth knot of the Elder's rope, ask Maeve to write it.
Maeve will say no. Maeve will say *"The candle waits."* Bram will,
that night, write it himself in his own slower hand. He has not yet.

This is the ledger. It is canon. The hook below names the line Bram
will, when he writes, write.

## Who the Wardens are not

To prevent canon-drift in future runs, the following are explicitly
*not* Wardens:

- **Bram is not a Warden.** Bram is the *hearth.* Bram cooks the stew
  Edda eats on Longnight Vigil. Bram is the village's *welcome,* not
  the village's *posture.* He carries the ledger; he does not bear the
  Mark. *(Cross-ref: `npcs/innkeeper_bram.md`.)*
- **Mara is not a Warden.** Mara is the *eyes the Wardens trust.*
  Mara has the southern history; she has the unopened letter and the
  unspoken slate-mark *(canon: `factions/three_crowns.md` — Mara saw
  the *point* mark go down four Foxthaws ago)*. She serves the Mark.
  She does not bear it. The four directions do not include the road
  she came from; the road she came from is *Mara's,* and the village
  honors that by not asking her to face south on its behalf.
- **Lyra is not a Warden.** Lyra is the *forest's listener.* She
  knows two of the three codex fragments by their Old Faerie roots.
  She translates *thrian-mor* without saying it aloud. The forest-line
  is not Lyra's to *keep;* the forest-line is Maeve's. Lyra walks past
  it, gentle, with empty hands. *(Cross-ref:
  `npcs/herbalist_lyra.md`.)*
- **The brothers are not yet Wardens.** *Alden* (girl, 9, the
  Pathfinder) and *Owen* (boy, 11, the Vanguard) — see
  `LOREKEEPER_AGENT.md` — are the village's *apprentices to the
  Mark.* Each of the four old hands has, in their NPC bibles, a
  natural teaching role: Edda has been waiting eight years for an
  apprentice (`smith_edda.md`); Roan would teach a child the bay's
  ear-rules without naming them as a teaching; Maeve teaches the
  village's children the eighty-nine knots (`elder_maeve.md`); Hala
  teaches the *first form* of the staff to every child old enough
  (`trainer_hala.md`). None of the four would call this making a
  Warden. All four know it is.

The brothers will, in the long arc, *bear the Mark.* They will not, in
this run or any reasonable next run, be *named* Wardens. The phrase is
held back. (See the Listening-clock in `factions/three_crowns.md` for
why this matters: the slate goes back to the magistrate in three
Lambmoons. The brothers' apprenticing has to feel earned by then.)

## What the Wardens do not do

Hard rules for downstream agents — please do not invent:

1. **No Warden's banner.** No flag, no crest, no painted shield, no
   embroidered tabard. The Mark is not heraldic. *(THEME §5: hand-
   painted banners exist in the village, but none is a Wardens'
   banner; there is no Wardens' banner.)*
2. **No Warden's hall.** The four meet in the village's normal
   meeting-places — the inn, the forge, the Elder's hearth, the
   training-ring under the old yew. There is no Hall of Wardens.
   There is no chamber. There is no door.
3. **No swearing-in.** Children are not, on coming of age, asked to
   swear anything. The Mark is borne, not sworn. The four civic
   unwritten oaths *are* the closest thing — and even they are not
   said *to* anyone, only said *aloud.*
4. **No Warden's call.** No horn, no drum, no chant, no rallying-cry.
   When the village must answer, it answers by what is said *(the
   four civic oaths above)* and by what is done *(the lantern at the
   well, the broken thing carried to the forge, the half-second
   stillness)*.
5. **No Warden's enemy.** The Wardens have no opposite faction. The
   Whisperwood goblins are not the Wardens' enemy; they are *the wood
   the Wardens watch.* The Iron Crown's clerks are not the Wardens'
   enemy; they are *the slate the Wardens are listened on.* The
   Stag-Court is not the Wardens' enemy; the Court is *what the
   Wardens have, on Maeve's lantern-side, learned to be quiet next
   to.* The Stone Crown is not the Wardens' enemy; the Stone Crown is
   *the weather Roan's bay was born in.* This is the whole shape.
   Briarwood does not have an enemy. Briarwood has a Mark.
6. **No four-Wardens-against-the-Warlord scene.** The Goblin Warlord
   is *Edda's old saber* and *Maeve's first quest* and *the player's
   threshold* — see existing canon. He is not a Warden-killer.
   Warden-deaths are not a thing canon supports without far more
   weight than this run, or any next run, can put under them.

## *Aen-velin-corr* held by four hands

The Antler-King's *aen-velin-corr* is the unbought road *seen from the
forest-line.* The Wardens of the Mark is the unbought road *held from
the village square.* They are the same condition under two different
weights of attention.

The Court watches. The Wardens *carry.* That is the difference.

This is the canonical reason the Court does not enter the valley *(see
`factions/three_crowns.md` §The Antler Crown)*. Not because the Court
cannot — the Court could. Because the Court does not need to. The four
hands are enough. *Vael-i-thirren, aen.*

It is also the canonical reason the Iron Crown's *Listening* has, three
re-listenings running, gone down as *the point.* The valley is held.
The valley does not need to be entered. The slate-mark is small because
the carrying is small enough — four hands, four tools, four directions —
that the Crown's larger machinery is not required.

Three years from now the magistrate will ride out and walk Briarwood.
He will not be told the village calls itself anything. He will not be
told about the ledger under the bar. He will see four old people doing
ordinary work — a smith at her anvil, a stablemaster at his stall, an
Elder at her hearth, a trainer at her staff — and he will, if he is the
kind of magistrate the Code makes, write *the point* a fourth time and
ride home.

If he is not — if he is a different kind of magistrate — that is a
future Lorekeeper's run, not this one.

## Hooks for future runs

- **Bram writing the next ledger entry.** When Bram writes his
  generation's entry — and he has not yet — the canonical line, in
  his slower hand, is reserved here for a future Builder run that
  surfaces the ledger as a discoverable inn-prop:

  > *Wardens of the Mark, this year: Edda at the forge; Roan at the
  > stable; Maeve at the lantern; Hala at the staff. The valley is
  > unbought. The Mark is held.*

  Four lines, four names, no flourishes. *(Mirrors the
  grandmother's entry exactly. The Mark is the same.)* Please do
  not ship the prop until a Builder run wants it; please do not
  ship the line in a different hand or a different rhythm.
- **Each Warden's default facing.** A future Builder/Polisher run
  may, on a clean-day idle pass, gate each Warden's default
  facing: Edda east *(toward the Crystal Caves)*, Roan south
  *(toward the road)*, Maeve west *(toward the Whisperwood)*, Hala
  north *(toward the mountain ring)*. The village square's
  geometry should support this without re-staging. This is a
  small motion-feel pass, not a story beat. THEME §12 honor.
- **The brothers as apprentices to the Mark.** A long-arc story
  beat, not a single quest. Each Warden has a natural teaching
  hand-off in their NPC bible. The phrasing the village would use
  is *learning the Mark,* not *becoming a Warden.* The phrase
  *Warden* is not, in this lifetime of the brothers, said to them
  by any of the four. They will hear it for the first time when —
  and this is a withheld future-run hook — Bram writes the next
  ledger entry, and one of the brothers, helping clean the bar
  late one Longnight, sees it in his slow hand. That is the whole
  beat. It is not shipped here.
- **The fifth posture.** A future Lorekeeper, eventually, may need
  to introduce a fifth Warden — when one of the four can no longer
  carry their tool. The natural rule, by the bench, is that the
  fifth Warden is *named by the four.* The named-on candidate is
  *not* one of the brothers (they are too young; the Mark is borne
  by elders, not by children). The named-on candidate is, by the
  ledger's pattern of one-per-generation, *a child of one of the
  four,* trained for a decade. There is, in current canon, exactly
  one such child the village knows by sight: Maeve's daughter
  **Aelis,** in the smoke-cities, eleven Lambmoons silent. *(See
  `npcs/elder_maeve.md`, `factions/three_crowns.md`.)* Whether
  Aelis is the fifth posture is the largest single hook in this
  document. **Please do not resolve it.** It is held for a future
  Lorekeeper's whole run.
- **The Walker-house woman in Halsa-the-grandmother's ledger.** The
  unnamed staff-bearer in Bram's grandmother's entry is a hook for
  Hala's predecessor in the Walker tradition. The
  `trainer_hala.md` Aurel material gives one canonical Walker
  House. A future writer may name the predecessor; please do so
  cautiously and do not contradict Hala's bible.
- **An Edda-side Listening beat.** *(Carried hook from
  `factions/three_crowns.md`.)* When the Listening-month is the
  live month, a magistrate's clerk arrives, Edda feeds the clerk
  soup, Edda forges nothing different. The Wardens-frame for this
  beat is: *the keeping-warm answers,* without naming itself, and
  the clerk leaves with a soup-warmed cloak and no fact for the
  slate. This is a one-scene beat, not a quest.

## Author note for downstream agents

This file is the **inside-out twin** of `factions/three_crowns.md` and
should be read alongside it. Together they are the political frame of
Eldoria-realm: three weather-Crowns watching from outside, four old
hands carrying from inside.

Three rules for future writers:

1. **The Wardens never become loud.** No banner, no hall, no oath-
   ceremony, no enemy. The whole point of the Mark is that the
   village does not have to *announce* itself to be held.
2. **The four are the four.** Edda, Roan, Maeve, Hala. Do not add a
   fifth without the bench's rule (the four name the next; one of
   them must lay down their tool first). Do not subtract one
   without far more story-weight than a single run can carry. NPC
   deaths in this set are not a thing this canon supports.
3. **The brothers are *apprentices to the Mark,* never named
   Wardens.** Until — see hooks — the ledger is written and the
   moment is earned, the phrase *Warden* belongs to the four old
   hands and to no one else.

The Wardens are what Briarwood has instead of an army, instead of a
banner, instead of a Crown. They are an old quiet sentence the
village says into its own shoulder when something is owed. They are
what the Court is *quiet next to* and what the Iron Crown sees and
mistakes for *small*. They are the keeping-warm, the keeping-watch,
the keeping-vigil, the keeping-still — and the four civic unwritten
oaths that pass between them when the village's ordinary quiet is
not, on a given evening, going to be enough.

The Mark is held.

*Vellum is patient.*
