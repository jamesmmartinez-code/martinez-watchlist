# The Bandits — A Faction Reading

*The second faction-politics canon of Eldoria-realm, sister-document to
`lore/factions/three_crowns.md` (the older quiet powers) and
`lore/factions/wardens_of_the_mark.md` (the village's own posture). Where
the Three Crowns hold the valley between three different *weathers,* the
bandits are the **first mortal hand** that tried to put a hand on the
unbought road. They are small. They will be cleared. The reading below
explains, in full canon, why they could rise, why they cannot last, and
what they leave behind on the leaning plank when they are gone.*

This document does **not** make the bandits a hero faction or a tragic
faction. It makes them a *human-scale* faction — opportunists, deserters,
a captain in a sodden cloak who once carried a Halevant pike and now
chalks a poor man's slate-mark on a roadside plank because he saw nobody
was watching. They are the kind of trouble the village can clear in an
afternoon, and the kind of trouble that *means* something all the same.

*Tone: warm gravitas, child-safe (THEME §7). The bandits are not
monsters; they are mortals with a small wrong idea. Roan's verb is
**clearing,** not **slaying.** Future writers who make the bandits
grimdark-cruel break the canon. The dignity is in the human smallness.*

---

## What the bandits are, in this world

A **bandit camp** in Eldoria is not an army. It is a **cold-ash
freebooter's camp** — five to nine former pikemen who deserted a
Halevant company before reaching the smoke-cities (or were paid off and
not paid honestly, which is the usual way a Halevant company dissolves
its own discipline) and who walked north through the Reapmoon-passes
following an old courier-rumour that *the valley below the mountain ring
has no garrison.* The rumour is true. The valley below the mountain ring
has no garrison. The bandits walked into the mouth of the **unbought
road** *(`three_crowns.md`,* ***aen-velin-corr****)* and read the held
quiet as **opportunity.**

They were wrong about what the held quiet is. The held quiet is a
*posture.* The Iron Crown's *thrian-mor* — the long listening — is
keeping a slate on the valley. The Antler Crown's *aen-thirre* is
watching it. The Stone Crown's *korr* is riding through it. The valley
is *not* unwatched. It is unbought, which is a different word, and the
bandits do not know the difference because Halevant pike-companies are
not taught the difference. They are taught to march in straight lines
and to count.

The bandits, in practice, attempt to **count** the road. They impose a
toll. They mark the leaning plank south of the village with the
**toll-mark** (described below), which is a broken Halevant
pike-company chalk-sigil. They wait at the camp for travellers to come
through, and when travellers do not come through (because the
courier-line south goes around the camp the moment the runner-talk
reaches Briar's Run), they grow restive, and they grow restive in the
small ways desperate small men grow restive: stolen sheep, smashed
sheep-fence, an evening's argument over the captain's share. The camp
is a held breath that knows it is going to be held until somebody
clears it. Pikemen *know* when a camp will not last. That knowing is
the canonical reason **Vrith of the Sodden Cloak** does not, in the
end, run.

## Origin — the Halevant route, broken

The canonical origin of the present camp is **the Halevant Reap-line
mutiny of three Foxthaws ago.** The Iron Crown's Code requires a
pike-company on the Reap-line — the long granary corridor between
**Halevant-on-the-Reap** *(`three_crowns.md`)* and the southern
smoke-cities — to be paid in standard copper at the close of every
Lambmoon and again at Reapmoon. Three Foxthaws ago, the Reap-line
company under **Captain-of-Pikes Hervel** received Lambmoon pay in
clipped silver instead, in a year the Halevant magistrate's clerk
later admitted (privately, to a colleague, never on the slate) had
been *miscounted at the seal-house.* Hervel's company waited for the
Reapmoon to be paid in honest copper. The Reapmoon arrived. The
Reapmoon was paid in clipped silver again.

The company broke. Hervel led the loyalist half south to the
smoke-cities and the slow legal redress of the Code. The
**company-broken** half — the Code's Common term for a pikeman whose
company has dissolved its own discipline before the year was out, and
who is *technically* still on the rolls but, in practice, no longer
expected back — went **north** instead, on the rumour of the unbought
valley. There were eleven of them. There are six in the present camp.
The other five did what *company-broken* pikemen usually do: drifted
into smaller hamlets along the Reap-line, took up smaller crafts,
became (on the Iron Crown's slate) a small line of unbought names that
are not, themselves, going to be Listened-to for a long time. Two of
those five sometimes walk through Briarwood without anyone in the
village knowing they were ever pikemen at all. They pay copper at
Bram's. They ride on. They are, by Iron-Crown reckoning, harmless.

The six in the present camp are not, on the same reckoning, harmless.
They are *company-broken in the second sense* — pikemen who, having
failed to find a small craft, have fallen into the only craft the pike
had ever taught them. They count. They draw a line on a plank. They
stand on it.

## Vrith of the Sodden Cloak — the captain

The canonical captain of the present camp is **Vrith of the Sodden
Cloak.** He is, by his own remembering, fifty-one. He was a
**pike-corporal** in Captain-of-Pikes Hervel's company for nineteen
Lambmoons, and he was the most senior *company-broken* man among the
eleven who walked north. The cloak was issued at the seal-house in
his fourth Lambmoon and has been on his back through three Reapmoon
floods since; it carries the mildew of every river-crossing on the
Reap-line and one chalk-stain in the small of the back where Vrith
once leaned against a freshly-marked plank he was, at the time, proud
of. The cloak is the canonical reason Roan calls him *the captain in
a sodden cloak* in `quest_text/bandit_road_for_roan.md`. The cloak is
not Vrith's vanity. It is the only Halevant thing he has left that
the seal-house ever gave him in honest copper-weight. He keeps it
because losing it would be the last *company-broken* thing.

Vrith's voice — for any Builder run that surfaces him in dialogue —
is **clipped, southern, dry as a Reap-line summer.** He does not say
much, and the little he says is the half-formal pikeman's Common of
a man who was never taught flourish. Two Halevant-pikeman idioms are
canonically his (and only his among the bandits, although other
*company-broken* men in future writing may use them):

- **"By the slate."** A pikeman's affirmation. Half-oath, half-shrug.
  When a pikeman commits to a thing — a count, a stand, a watch —
  he says *by the slate,* and the saying binds him to the slate's
  reckoning *(the Iron Crown's slate, in Code)*. Vrith uses it
  twice in the canonical encounter beat described below. It is not
  Stone-Tongue and does not bind the way Stone-Tongue binds; it is
  a Halevant *manner.* A *company-broken* pikeman who says *by the
  slate* is invoking a slate that has, as it happens, broken him.
  The line carries that grief without naming it.
- **"Stand the line."** A Halevant pikeman's address to his own
  company on the eve of a hard count. Vrith says it once when the
  player closes the camp. He is not addressing his men. He is
  addressing himself. The line is the canonical reason Vrith does
  not run.

Vrith's pommel-mark — the small steel plate Roan accepts at the
quest turn-in *(`quest_text/bandit_road_for_roan.md`)* — is a
Halevant pike-company issue, stamped with the Reap-line company's
older sigil. The sigil is **a horizontal stroke with a single
vertical bar struck cleanly through it** *(see "The toll-mark"
below)* — that is, an *intact* Reap-line stroke-and-bar, pre-mutiny.
Vrith carried the pommel-mark from the seal-house. He never
deserted *his own pommel.* This is the canonical detail Roan reads
when he weighs the mark on his palm and sets it on the stable rail
next to the wolf-fangs. Roan does not name it aloud. Roan does not
name Vrith aloud. *(`quest_text/bandit_road_for_roan.md`,* turn-in
line — *"You will speak to Maeve, traveler."*)

Vrith's **seal** — the canonical item Maeve will ask for in the
queued `captain_seal_for_maeve` chain *(`WORLD_STATE.md` run-23
hook)* — is a small wax-press kept on a leather thong inside the
sodden cloak, against his shirt. The press is older than Vrith. It
was the Reap-line *Captain-of-Pikes'* press, not a corporal's; how
Vrith came to carry it is a beat **withheld** for a future
Lorekeeper run *(see Withholding Ledger)*. The seal carries the same
stroke-and-bar sigil as the pommel-mark — but **intact** on the
press's metal face, and **broken** in the wax Vrith has chalked over
it on the leaning plank. *That is the bandits' whole self-reading
in one object: an intact tool used to print a broken mark.* Maeve
sees it the moment Roan hands it across. She does not say so. She
sets the seal in the small iron box on her hearth-shelf. She does
not break the seal. *Burning a Halevant magistrate's older sigil is
not Briarwood's right.*

### The captain name table — for Builder

The present camp is canonically Vrith's. **Future bandit camps**
*(currently un-shipped — `WorldBuilder._build_enemies` will, on
present canon, only spawn one camp at `Vector3(2, 0, -55)` per
`WORLD_STATE.md` run-22)* may, when the bandit-pressure track
matures, support multiple captains across multiple camps. For that
day, the canonical name table — pure Halevant pikeman hand-me-downs,
Reap-line-flavored, southern, dry — is:

- **Vrith of the Sodden Cloak** — *canonical, present camp.* Do not
  reuse for a second camp. Vrith is one man, in one cloak, in one
  ash-camp south of the village.
- **Hesto the Half-Pay** — a corporal who *did* receive his Lambmoon
  copper but only at half-weight. Bitter where Vrith is dry.
- **Olen of the Banked Fire** — a journeyman pikeman who tends the
  cold ash because he cannot abide a fire he did not light himself.
  The least dangerous of the captains; he has run from a fight
  before and would again.
- **Maerc the Plank** — by reputation only; the man who first
  chalked a toll-mark on a roadside plank, said to have done it on
  the Halevant-side of the Reap-line *before* the mutiny. *Nominal*
  ancestor of the toll-mark itself. Future Lorekeepers may write
  Maerc as a captain, an absent legend, or a name on a slate
  somewhere; the file leaves all three open.

Builder note: when the second-camp shipment arrives, the per-spawn
name dict in `WorldBuilder._build_enemies` should pull from this
table in **order of severity** (Vrith → Hesto → Olen → Maerc),
not at random. Pikemen camps get bigger and meaner as the
faction-pressure track stays high; the names track the same curve.
A camp on the *first* climb of pressure is *Olen's* camp. A camp
on a Reapmoon-into-Wolfwake spike is *Hesto's.* Vrith's camp is
the present one; Maerc's is the rumour the bandits tell *each
other* when they want to feel less small.

## The toll-mark — what is on the leaning plank

The leaning plank south of the village *(per `_make_bandit_camp`,
`WORLD_STATE.md` run-22)* carries the bandits' canonical sigil:

> **A horizontal stroke** *(the Halevant pike-company *patrol-passed-
> here* mark, lifted unchanged from the Reap-line slate-shorthand)*
> **with a single vertical bar struck through the middle** *(the
> *broken-company* mark, drawn by a *company-broken* pikeman to
> say "this stretch is ours")*.

The mark is **chalked.** Not burned (burning is Stone-clan custom and
the bandits do not have it), not carved (carving requires the kind of
patience a freebooter has lost), not painted (paint costs honest
copper). Chalk fades. Chalk runs in the rain. Chalk has, by the
present canonical run-day, faded twice on the plank and been
re-chalked twice. The third re-chalking is **the chalking the player
finds.** The fourth would have been the chalking after the next
Reapmoon flood. There is not going to be a fourth.

The mark has a name in pikeman Common: **the toll-mark.** It is not
the same word as the Iron Crown's slate-mark *(`three_crowns.md`,*
the *point* and the *stroke* and the *cross)*; the toll-mark is a
**bandit invention.** No magistrate would recognize it. No
Halevant clerk would ever record it. It is a *poor man's slate-
mark* — a sigil that wants to look like authority and only succeeds
in looking like a man who wishes he still had a company.

When the bandits are cleared, the chalk on the plank will run in the
next Reapmoon rain and be gone. The plank itself will remain. Roan
canonically *leaves* the leaning plank where it is — *"a village
should be able to look at what it almost lost."* — and the plank
becomes, by accidental kindness, **a village memory-prop** that
future Polisher runs may light, mossing, weather. *The toll-mark is
chalk; the plank is wood; the village keeps the wood.*

### Polisher / Builder hook — the decal

The current `_make_bandit_camp` prop has a `MAT_DARK_WOOD` plank
with no decal. When the rune-texture pipeline lands *(per
`WORLD_STATE.md` Hook for Lore Keeper)*, the toll-mark decal
should ship with these properties:

1. **Chalk-white,** not burnt-black, not red. Mid-value, slightly
   warm — the chalk is Halevant chalk, milled out of pond-bed
   limestone, not pure white.
2. **Two strokes only:** the horizontal stroke first, then the
   vertical bar through the middle. No flourishes. No date. No
   second decoration.
3. **Half-faded** by default. The third re-chalking is canonically
   the run-day chalking; weathering is the *previous* chalking
   showing through. The decal should look like a re-applied
   chalk on top of an older chalk-shadow.
4. **Visible from twelve metres at noon, invisible at thirty.** The
   plank is on the south-road approach; the silhouette of the camp
   reads *first,* the toll-mark *second,* the cold ash *third.* In
   that order is the canonical first-glance reading — the player
   sees a camp, then a sign on the camp, then a sign that the
   camp's fire is not warm.
5. **After Roan's `road_warden` flag fires,** the decal should
   *not* be repainted. Builder may, in the same run that ships
   the rune-texture pipeline, add a second decal-state
   `toll_mark_faded` that swaps in after the bandit-pressure
   track has been below `0.20` for two full Reapmoons. The
   faded state is the chalk run almost to nothing; the plank
   still stands. *The plank is the kindness.*

## The hierarchy in the camp

The present camp's six men, by canonical role:

1. **Vrith of the Sodden Cloak** — captain, former pike-corporal,
   carries the Reap-line *Captain-of-Pikes'* seal he should not
   have. *(One man.)*
2. **Two Brutes** — former pike-corporals or
   third-Lambmoon journeymen, the two heaviest of the
   *company-broken,* the canonical mapping for the existing
   `bandit` enemy kind at higher silhouette weight. *(Builder: this
   is **lore guidance, not a code change.** Run-22 ships a single
   `bandit` kind; future runs may, when the pressure-track
   matures, surface a `bandit_pikeman` mid-tier between the base
   `bandit` and the `bandit_captain` mini-boss already shipped in
   run-23. Lore here permits but does not require.)*
3. **Three Scouts** — former courier-guards or fresh
   first-Lambmoon pike-recruits, the lighter and warier of the
   six, the canonical mapping for the existing `bandit` enemy
   kind at base silhouette weight. They are the reason the camp
   *knows* travellers are coming through — the courier-string
   habit dies hard. They are also the canonical reason the camp's
   first reaction to a closing player is **to fall back on
   Vrith,** not to flee. Pike-recruits do not flee on their own;
   they flee on a captain's word, and Vrith — *standing the
   line* — does not give it.

The camp also keeps **one stolen sheep, tied at the cold-ash side
of the camp,** canonically a black-faced ewe out of Edda's
neighbour's flock. *(Edda's neighbour is the canonical owner; Edda
herself does not keep sheep. The pelt-and-fleece traffic between
the neighbour and the village is detailed in
`smith_edda.md`'s relationships.)* The sheep is **not a quest
item;** Roan, on the road-clearance, returns it to the neighbour
before riding back to the village. The neighbour gives Roan a
half-pound of cheese for the saddle and asks no questions. Roan
asks none either. *Unnen.* Future writers may, gently, surface a
small Reapmoon ledger entry on Bram's "ewe-back-from-the-south"
day; the line stands alone and need not be a quest payload.

## What the bandits do *not* do

These are the canon constraints on the bandit faction. They are
**load-bearing** — the political frame of `three_crowns.md` and
the village frame of `wardens_of_the_mark.md` rest on them.

- **The bandits do not herald the Iron Crown.** They are
  *company-broken,* which is to say *off the Iron Crown's slate
  while still owning a Halevant pommel-mark.* Their presence does
  not move the Listening's clock. *(`three_crowns.md`, Author
  note rule 1 — "Do not ship a magistrate-arrives quest before
  the Listening-month.")* The bandits' clearance does not delay
  the Listening either; the Listening is a separate clock, and a
  separate weather. The two should not be braided.
- **The bandits do not represent the Antler Crown.** They are
  Common-tongue mortals; they have no Old Faerie. They cannot be
  at the forest-line on Foxthaw evenings; the Court would not
  measure them, would not offer them the seat, would not
  speak of them. The Court does not name *company-broken* mortals;
  it does not need to. *(`stag_courts_courtesy.md`, the rule of
  measuring.)*
- **The bandits do not represent the Stone Crown.** They have
  ridden no Long Ride; they are not *kerritha-ed;* they are not
  on any cairn. A Stone-clan rider passing through the
  Whisperwood would, at the cold-ash camp, *not stop.* The
  bandits are a weather to the Stone Crown the way Briarwood is
  a weather to the Stone Crown — *open* or *closed* or *korr,*
  no fourth answer. *(`three_crowns.md`, the Stone Crown's three
  answers.)*
- **The bandits do not have a faith.** No Wild Pantheon
  invocations. They do not say *Brigid* on the steel; they do
  not lay a kill at *Thiar's* stone; they do not light *Hollow
  King* candles at Longnight. Halevant's pike-companies are
  taught a small *seal-house* devotion to *the Code,* which is
  not a god. Vrith's only religion is the slate. The slate is
  cold.
- **The bandits do not have a code of mercy that scales.** A
  single traveller surrendering coppers at the toll-mark walks
  through unharmed. A village walking through with arms is not a
  surrender; it is a *closing.* The bandits read the difference.
  Vrith does not ambush. *(He counts; he stands; he fights when
  the count goes the other way.)* This is the canonical reason
  the player can clear the camp **in daylight, with the village
  watching.** It is not a stealth quest. It is not a betrayal.
  It is a closing, said in pikeman Common.
- **The bandits do not enslave.** No captives. No prisoners. No
  human cargo. A Halevant pike-company of any vintage does not
  do this; *company-broken* pikemen carry the prohibition with
  them. Future writers MUST NOT introduce a captive-rescue beat
  to the bandit camp. The closing is a *count,* not a *rescue.*
- **The bandits do not have allies in the Whisperwood.** The
  goblins do not deal with them; the Stag-Court does not see
  them; the dire-wolves are *more* dangerous to the bandits
  than to the village, because the wolves do not respect the
  toll-mark. *(See cross-canon: the wolves' contempt for the
  toll-mark is the canonical reason Roan suspects the bandit
  camp is not going to last another Foxthaw.)*

## What the bandits *do* leave behind

When the camp is cleared, four things remain:

1. **The leaning plank** with the chalk toll-mark, faded by the
   next Reapmoon. Roan leaves the plank canonically *(see
   above)*.
2. **The cold ash** — never lit, never warm. *(`world.md`'s
   *no warm light* rule for the camp.)* The ash is a black
   small ring in dark soil; it weathers down within two
   Foxthaws to a dark patch indistinguishable from a wagon-
   stop.
3. **The captain's pommel-mark and seal.** Roan keeps the
   pommel-mark on the stable rail until the next Reapmoon's
   wolf-fang turn-in cycle; on that morning, he takes it down
   and hands it to Maeve with no comment. Maeve places it
   beside the seal in the small iron box on her hearth-shelf.
   The two pieces of Halevant steel sit together. Maeve does
   not break either.
4. **A small drift of clipped silver coins** — Halevant
   Lambmoon-pay, the wrong weight, three Foxthaws old — buried
   six inches under the cold ash, in a strip of oilcloth Vrith
   never told the others about. Roan finds it. Roan does *not*
   take it to Mara. Mara, who walked the Halevant route, would
   read the coins immediately, and the village does not need
   to read them. Roan re-buries the strip under the same six
   inches of cold ash and sets a flat stone over it. The stone
   is not a marker. It is a *covering.* *Kerrithen,* in the
   *covering* sense — laid down so the land holds it, *not* so
   the land remembers it. The clipped silver is a thing the
   land is to forget. *(Roan would not call this kerrithen
   aloud. Lyra, told the story across a salve-jar one
   Reapmoon, would. The withholding remains.)*

## Two new Common phrases (no new languages)

Old Faerie is not extended this run. Stone-Tongue is not extended.
Goblin Cant is not extended. The bandits are Common-tongue
*company-broken* pikemen; their canonical contributions to the
Common register are two **Halevant pikeman idioms,** which are
*Common dialect,* not a new language:

- **"By the slate."** A pikeman's affirmation. Half-oath, half-
  shrug. Binds the speaker to the slate's reckoning *(in
  context, the Iron Crown's Halevant slate)*. A *company-broken*
  pikeman who says *by the slate* is invoking a slate that has,
  as it happens, broken him. Vrith says it twice in the
  canonical encounter beat. The phrase is a Vrith-class
  utterance only; a non-pikeman saying it is canonically wrong.
  Future writers introducing a Halevant-side NPC (a magistrate's
  clerk, a courier, a seal-house apprentice) may use it
  carefully.
- **"Stand the line."** A Halevant pikeman's address to his own
  company on the eve of a hard count. Vrith says it once when
  the player closes the camp, and he is not addressing his men;
  he is addressing himself. The phrase is the canonical reason
  Vrith does not run. Future writers MUST NOT have a Briarwood
  NPC say *stand the line* in any context; the phrase is
  Halevant-pikeman, and the village does not borrow it. Roan,
  who has heard the phrase across nine years of courier-strings,
  has *never* said it aloud.

And one **Common noun**, already used above:

- ***the toll-mark.*** The bandits' chalked sigil on the leaning
  plank — a horizontal stroke with a single vertical bar struck
  through it. A *poor man's slate-mark.* Future writers MUST
  NOT use *toll-mark* of any Iron Crown sigil; the toll-mark is
  a *bandit invention,* and the magistrate would not recognize
  it. The toll-mark is canonically chalked, not burned, not
  carved, not painted. Chalk fades. *That is the whole story.*

## Author note for downstream agents

This document is the **load-bearing faction frame** for the bandit
faction the run-22 Builder shipped and the run-23 Builder named.
Please do not introduce a quest, a codex page, or a Halevant-side
character that contradicts:

1. **The bandits do not herald the Iron Crown.** They are
   *company-broken.* The Listening's clock is separate. *(`three_crowns.md`,* the Listening, *thrian-mor*.)
2. **Vrith does not run, does not surrender, and does not give
   a deathbed speech.** *Stand the line* is the whole shape.
3. **The toll-mark is chalk.** Not burned, not carved, not
   painted. It fades. The plank stays.
4. **Roan does not name Vrith aloud.** Maeve does not break
   the seal. Mara does not buy the silver. Lyra does not learn
   of the buried coin until much later, across a salve-jar.
   The four withholdings are the village's whole posture toward
   the closing.
5. **The bandits do not have allies.** Not the goblins. Not the
   Stag-Court. Not the dire-wolves. Not the smoke-cities. They
   are alone, *company-broken,* and small.

The softness of this frame is the point. The bandits are not a
war. They are a small wrong shape on the unbought road, and the
village clears them, and the chalk runs in the next Reapmoon
rain, and the plank stays. The plank is the kindness. *Roan
leaves the plank where it stood.*

The valley has been listened-to, watched, and ridden-through. The
bandits are the first hand that *touched* it. The hand has been
lifted. The road is *aen-velin-corr* again — the small-long-path
that has not been bought — and Briarwood does not, yet, know it
came that close.

The valley does not need to know.

*Velhain-tor.*
