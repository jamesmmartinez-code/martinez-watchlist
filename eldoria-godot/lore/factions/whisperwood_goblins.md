# The Whisperwood Goblins — A Faction Reading

*The fourth faction-politics canon of Eldoria-realm. Sister-document to
`lore/factions/three_crowns.md` (the older quiet powers),
`lore/factions/wardens_of_the_mark.md` (the village's own posture), and
`lore/factions/bandits.md` (the first mortal hand on the unbought road).
Where the Crowns are postures, the Wardens a held breath, and the bandits
a small wrong idea, the **Whisperwood goblins** are something stranger and
older: a people who have **forgotten themselves.** They are not monsters.
They are not tragic. They are what happens when the Sundering goes
unanswered for a long time and nobody comes back to take the kettle off
the hob.*

This document does **not** make the goblins sympathetic in the modern
sense. The brothers who play this game will still raise a sword to a
goblin, and the goblin will still raise a crude axe back. Combat does not
break. What this document does is give the village's verb its right
weight: the herbalists' word for it is **quieting**, not slaying. Maeve's
word is **counting**. Mara's word is **a tally that keeps the road
calm.** Hala's word is **a form practiced.** None of them say *kill.*
That is canon. Future writers who turn the goblins grimdark-cruel break
this canon. The dignity is in the slow sadness.

*Tone: warm gravitas, child-safe (THEME §7). The goblins are feral and
will hurt the careless — that is true and remains true. They are also a
people the world has stopped speaking to, and a few in Briarwood (the
herbalists, the older Stag-Court remembrancers, one Briarwood scribe
three centuries dead) know it. The reading below holds both at once,
because both are true.*

---

## What the Whisperwood goblins are, in this world

The Whisperwood goblins were, before the Sundering, **something else.**
The old word for what they were has not survived in any of the four
spoken tongues; only the Stag-Court still holds the shape of it, and the
Stag-Court does not say it aloud, because to say a forgotten name aloud
is to ask the forgetting to lift, and the forgetting has not lifted in
a thousand winters.

What is left of them, today, is a people of small, hunched, green-skinned
hunters who live in the deep folds of the Whisperwood and who beat
crude drums at dusk. They wear ragged loincloths, bone fetishes, and
twists of birch-bark cordage. Their weapons are crude — chipped flint, a
stolen pike-head set in a green stick, a sling. Their camps are
short-lived. Their drums carry. Their warlords are not chieftains; they
are wounds the goblin-folk of a given Whisperwood-fold have decided to
follow because no other shape is left to follow. (Maeve's phrase: *The
Warlord is no chieftain; he is a wound.* See `data/quest_text/
whisperwood_cleansing.md`.)

They are **feral, not cruel.** They do not torture. They do not take
slaves. They do not, in any of the village's nine generations of
remembering, *hate.* What they do is the thing a hungry small thing
does when it is afraid: they raid sheep, they burn a fence, they beat
the drum, they break the pike-head against the green wood and find a
new pike-head. They will hurt a careless traveller. They have not,
within memory, hurt a careful one.

This is not a virtue. They are not, in any sense the village would
recognize, *moral.* The herbalists' kindest reading is: **the
Whisperwood goblins do not know what they are doing.** They have lost
the part of themselves that knows. They are a people in *aen-irren*.

---

## ***aen-irren*** *(eyn-EER-en)* — *the small forgetting*

The Stag-Court's word for the condition of the Whisperwood goblins is
**aen-irren** — *the small forgetting.* It is not the great
forgetting that comes from loss, the kind of forgetting that follows a
death and is grieved through. It is the slow forgetting that comes from
**too long a quiet.** A kettle left on the hob. A name not spoken. A
hearth banked but never re-fed. *aen-irren* is the word for what fell
on the goblin-folk after the Sundering, when their gods went quiet (see
`lore/world.md`, *the Wild Pantheon* — and the older powers under the
Pantheon, the ones not named in polite company; the goblins' old gods
were, the Stag-Court suspects, of that older order) and no one came
back to remind them who they were.

The word does not carry malice. *aen-irren* is a slow, sad word, like
a kettle boiling dry and nobody home to take it off the hob. The
Stag-Court uses it of the goblins the way an old grandmother uses a
sigh.

It is the **first** of the three Old Faerie words this document
introduces. The Stag-Court does not, by canon, ever apply the word to a
mortal. It is reserved. To call a person *aen-irren* in the village
would be a small unkindness, and Maeve would correct the speaker
gently. The word belongs to the goblins.

---

## ***vell-mor-shau*** *(VELL-mor-show)* — *the path that has been forgotten*

The second Old Faerie word this document introduces — *vell-mor-shau* —
is in the broken descendant-form **vellmorsh** the closest thing the
goblins have left to a *true word of their own.* In the older Faerie,
*vell-mor-shau* means *the long-path forgotten,* a compound of *velin*
(the long path; see `lore/world.md`), *mor* (long), and the older
suffix *-shau* (set down). The herbalists, who hear the goblins drum at
dusk, will tell you that what the drums are calling — what the
drum-circles around the campfire are calling, in their own broken way —
is *vellmorsh.* Their word for *path.* But the *mor* has fallen out.
The middle syllable, *long,* has been forgotten *inside the very word
for the path.* They are calling for a road and they have lost the part
of the word that means *the road goes far.*

This is the canonical reason the herbalists notice. Lyra notices first;
her teacher noticed before her; her teacher's teacher knew the older
form. The phrase *vell-mor-shau* is held by the herbalist line of
Briarwood as a small private grief. It is not, on canon, ever told to
the goblins. To return their word to them would be to lift the
*aen-irren,* and the herbalists are not certain — none of the
Whisperwood remembrancers are certain — that the lifting would be a
kindness now. The drums have been beating for a long time. A people
woken out of *aen-irren* would have a great deal to grieve, and Lyra,
who has held one mortal grief in her own two hands and is still learning
to set it down, will not wish a thousand winters of grief on a people
without their permission. *(See `lore/npcs/herbalist_lyra.md` — the
voice-rule that Lyra never speaks the goblin-tongue aloud.)*

---

## ***korr-thau-vell*** *(kor-THOW-vell)* — *the breath-and-walk*

The third Old Faerie word — *korr-thau-vell* — is the *call-and-response*
the older Faerie people once used at the edge of a hunt. The hunter
struck the haft of the spear once against the heel of the boot
(*korr,* breath-stop). The hunted, if it was a *worthy* hunted —
a stag, an elk, an old boar — answered with one stamp of the foot
(*thau,* answer-stamp). Then the hunter walked toward the hunted at the
hunted's pace (*vell,* walk-with), and the kill, if it came, was clean
and *kerrithen* — laid down. *(See `lore/world.md`, *kerrithen* — to
lay down so the land may hold it.)* The whole construction —
*korr-thau-vell* — was the older Faerie phrase for **the courtesy
between hunter and hunted.** It is the older form of what Thiar the
Stag-God now keeps for hunters. *(See `lore/world.md`,* ***Thiar**
**the Stag** — wilderness, the hunt, the mercy owed to prey.*)

The goblin drums at dusk are the broken descendant of *korr-thau-vell.*
The first drum strikes — that is *korr.* A second drum, somewhere
farther back in the wood, answers — that is *thau.* The whole
drum-circle, when it joins, is *vell.* They are not, when they drum,
calling each other to a raid (the raids are decided by the Warlord and
called with a different drum-figure — three short, then long; the
Warlord's drum, see *Goblin Brutes,* below). They are, at dusk, **doing
the courtesy.** They have forgotten what the courtesy is for. They have
forgotten that the courtesy was once between *hunter and hunted.* They
are doing it to the dusk itself. To the wood. To no-one.

The herbalists have heard this for nine generations. None of them has
ever found a way to say what it means without sounding cruel. Lyra has
written one line about it in the back of her materia, in a hand so
small it is a kindness to the line itself: *They are still being
courteous. To no-one.*

## What is left of the goblins, in shape

Three shapes are left. The brothers will meet all three at the bench.

**The goblin-of-the-fold** — small, hunched, 0.7–0.95 the height of a
village child, green-skinned for the moss that has stained their
forebears' green-skinned forebears, ragged loincloths, bone fetishes.
Their weapons are *what they have found:* a chipped flint axe, a
stolen pike-head, a sling. Most of them, in any given fold, are not
warriors. They are foragers, drum-keepers, the small-handed ones who
mind the smaller-handed. The five Maeve will ask the player to *count*
in `whisperwood_cleansing.md` are warriors, not all of them; that is
the canon reason Maeve asks for *the count, not the trophy.* (The
herbalists have, on more than one Lambmoon, asked her to use the word
*count.* She uses the word *count.* Hala approves.)

**The goblin-brute** — same kit, larger (1.0–1.1 scale), stolen helms,
bigger axes. The brute is the one the Warlord has *promoted.* Promotion
in goblin-fold is not a ceremony; it is the brute-to-be having taken
a bigger axe from a brute that lay down. The drum-figure called
*the Warlord's drum* — three short, then long — is what the brute
beats when raid-rumour rises. The brute is not, in any goblin's lived
experience, *led.* The brute *carries the drum-figure forward.* (THEME
§4: Brute, helms, bigger axes. This document does not contradict; it
adds the drum-figure as canon.)

**The Goblin Warlord** — boss, 1.6× scale, gold crown, red aura, war
banners. The Warlord is *the wound.* The crown was not made for the
Warlord; the crown was found, and the finding-of-the-crown is the
Warlord-of-this-fold. (The crown is, by canon, a Halevant
pike-captain's iron circlet — see `lore/factions/bandits.md` for
Halevant — taken from a captain whose company never reached the
smoke-cities and never came home; whose absence the Iron Crown's
*thrian-mor* slate has, three full Lambmoons running, marked with the
small chalk-point that means *re-listen in seven.* The crown has
never been re-listened to. The Warlord wears it, by accident of
finding, and the goblin-fold's small sad order has rearranged itself
around the wearing.)

The Warlord's red aura is the *want* of the iron circlet, which was
forged hot and never properly *kerrithen.* (Smiths know.) The
war-banners are the Warlord's *figures of authority,* but the figures
are not goblin figures — they are scratched copies of pike-company
chevrons the Warlord has seen on the iron crown and has remembered the
shape of. The goblin-fold accepts the banners because the goblin-fold
has not, in living drum-memory, had a *figure* to follow, and a figure
that beats the drum and stands taller than the rest is a figure.
*aen-irren,* again. The Warlord is *aen-irren* with a crown on.

The reason the Warlord *can* be cleared, by canon, is that **the
crown is borrowed.** When the Warlord falls, the crown rolls. The fold
does not, on the next dawn, find another crown. The drum-figure
disappears with the wound. A different drum-figure may, in time, be
found by the next Warlord-of-the-fold; that is the canonical reason
clearings are *recurring* and not *final.* The drum is a slow
hydra. Each head is a wound. Each clearing is a quieting. The hydra is
**aen-irren** itself, and the only thing that would end the hydra is
the lifting of the small forgetting — which the herbalists, by canon,
will not yet ask for.

## What the village does not do, and why

Briarwood does **not** exterminate the Whisperwood goblins. This is
canon, and the reasons are layered.

**Maeve's reason** *(the keeping-vigil — see `lore/factions/
wardens_of_the_mark.md`)*: *The drums beat at dusk. They have beaten
at dusk for as long as Briarwood has lit candles at dawn. We do not
ask the dusk to stop being dusk. We ask the dusk to stay at the
forest-line.* Maeve says **counting,** never **slaying.** *(Voice-rule
held in `lore/npcs/elder_maeve.md`.)*

**Hala's reason** *(the keeping-still)*: *A form practiced is a form
held. The form does not require the goblin to be a man.* Hala's
voice-rule, in the Whisperwood Cleansing wolf-form chain, is to call
the action *quieting the drum,* not *killing the drummer.* *(See
`data/quest_text/wolf_form_with_hala.md`.)*

**Lyra's reason** *(the herbalist line)*: *aen-irren* is a sad word,
not a hostile word. The lifting of *aen-irren* is not the village's
gift to give. The herbalist who lifts *aen-irren* without the Court's
hand under hers is a herbalist who has put her hand on something older
than her name. *(Voice-rule: Lyra never speaks the goblin-tongue
aloud.)*

**Mara's reason** *(the merchant, the tally)*: *The bounty-clerk in
Halevant pays clean coin for a count of ears, traveller. A count is
not an army. A count keeps the road south calm. It does not keep the
goblins from beating the drum. The drum is not what the clerk pays
for.* Mara has, on canon, never used the word *kill* with a customer.
*(See `data/quest_text/ears_for_mara.md` — the clerk pays for the
*count.*)*

**Roan's reason** *(the keeping-watch)*: *The Whisperwood is a
fold-line. Fold-lines move. A fold cleared today will rise three
Lambmoons hence at the next fold. I have ridden seven fold-lines down
in nine years. The eighth will come. Pikemen don't last. Drums do.*
Roan's verb in the goblin-clearings, like in the bandit-clearings, is
*clearing,* not *slaying.* *(Voice-rule held in
`lore/npcs/stablemaster_roan.md`.)*

**Edda's reason** *(the keeping-warm)*: *Brigid forges the hearth, not
the pyre. The forge that ends a people is not Brigid's forge.* Edda
has, on canon, never made a blade *for* a goblin-clearing. She has
made blades that have been used in clearings. The verb she uses for
those blades, when they are returned to her for re-edge, is *quieted,*
the goblin-fold word's mortal cousin. *(See `lore/npcs/smith_edda.md`,
the voice-rule that Edda never names a blade Frost.)*

**Bram's reason** *(the inn)*: Bram does not have a reason. Bram has
**a kettle,** which is on the hob, which is steaming, which a goblin
drum at dusk does not interrupt. *(See `lore/npcs/innkeeper_bram.md`,
the voice-rule that Bram never asks the traveller what they did.)*

The seven small reasons, taken together, are the canon reason
**Briarwood clears, but does not exterminate.** It is also the canon
reason the Three Crowns have not yet decided to act on the goblins
themselves — the Iron Crown's *thrian-mor* is keeping the slate; the
Antler Crown's *aen-thirre* is watching; the Stone Crown's *korr* is
riding through. None of the three has decided the drums are loud
enough, yet, to require **a Crown's hand on a fold.** That decision,
if it comes, is not a Lorekeeper-run decision. The valley is meant to
live a long time on the question.

## *aen-irren* and the brothers

The brothers — the two children of nine and eleven who play this
game — will not, on first play, know that the goblins are *aen-irren.*
They will know that the drums beat and that Maeve has asked for a
count. That is the right shape of the knowing for that age. The codex
fragments seeded in the Whisperwood (see `data/codex/`) will, over
several plays, hand them the *aen-irren* word, the *vell-mor-shau*
word, the *korr-thau-vell* word, in that order. By the third play, an
older brother will, on canon, ask the younger brother *whether the
goblins are bad.* The right answer Maeve gives, when the question
reaches her dialogue tree, is the one that is already in
`lore/npcs/elder_maeve.md`: *They are not bad, child. They are
forgotten. We count for them, because they cannot count for
themselves.*

That sentence is the **whole moral payload** of the goblin-faction in
this game. It is canon. It does not appear in the dialogue tree until
the brothers have, between them, completed seven goblin-clearings. (A
later Lorekeeper run may set the gating; this run only sets the
sentence and reserves the place.)

It is, on canon, the line the older brother will remember.

## Drum-figures (small inventory)

The drum-figures are not, in this run, given gameplay-stat hooks; they
are given **canonical shapes** so that future audio passes have a
single source of truth. Each figure is one drum-pattern, struck on a
hide-and-bone drum the size of a goblin's head.

| Figure | Pattern (mnemonic) | Meaning, in the broken cant | Older Faerie root |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dusk-courtesy** | one stroke; pause; one stroke; pause; rolling many | *we are at the forest-line; we acknowledge the wood* | *korr-thau-vell* |
| **Forager-call** | two short, two short, one long | *small-hands come back; the light is going* | *vell-mor-shau* (the kept-path home) |
| **Warlord's drum** | three short, then one long (sustained) | *follow the figure; raid is rumoured* | (no Faerie root — a goblin-original, descended from the *finding* of the iron crown) |
| **Quieting** | a single struck note that is not answered | *a goblin has fallen* | *kerrithen* (laid down) |
| **Empty-fold** | silence, held over a full count of breaths | *the fold is gone* | (no figure; the absence *is* the figure) |

The five figures together are the goblin-fold's whole musical
inheritance. They are, by canon, the only drum-patterns the
Whisperwood goblins still know. There is no *song.* The forgetting
took the songs first.

## What an audio pass should hear

Future audio passes adapting this faction should remember:

- **The drums are not menacing.** They are tired. The right reference
  is a low frame-drum struck slowly, not a war-tom. THEME §6 forbids
  Hollywood orchestral bombast; that ban applies *especially* to the
  goblins.
- **The dusk-courtesy figure should sound like a question.** Two
  strokes, a pause, a roll. The roll is a sigh, not a roar.
- **The Warlord's drum is the only menacing figure** — and even that
  one is hollow. The crown is borrowed. The figure is borrowed. The
  player should hear, on the third or fourth Warlord-clearing, that
  the menace is shaped like an echo.
- **The Quieting note is a single soft strike on the hide.** The
  herbalists, in the village, will, on canon, *fall silent for one
  breath* when they hear it. Lyra will set down the mortar.
- **The Empty-fold is the absence of the drum.** That absence is the
  faction's whole grief, wordlessly. An audio pass that fills the
  absence with anything is breaking canon.

## Loose ends, reserved for future Lorekeeper runs

A list of canonical hooks this document **does not resolve,** so that
future runs have somewhere to walk:

1. The **older name** of the goblin-folk, before the Sundering. The
   Stag-Court holds the shape of the name. A future codex fragment, on
   the down-slope of the Crystal Caves under-stream (see
   `data/codex/pale_wyrm_beneath.md` — the same Briarwood scribe's
   hand), may set the shape of the name down without speaking it. The
   shape, not the word. The lifting of *aen-irren* is not yet the
   village's to give.
2. The **lifting of aen-irren.** A late-game arc, far past the brothers'
   first plays. It is the kind of arc that requires the Antler Crown's
   hand under the herbalist's hand. It is *not* a clearing. It is a
   *speaking.* A future Lorekeeper run may begin to shape it; this run
   only reserves the place.
3. The **Warlord's iron crown** — once the player has, by canon,
   defeated a Warlord, the iron crown is in the village's keeping.
   What Edda, Maeve, and Hala do with it is reserved. The voice-rules
   permit *kerrithen* (laying it down at the south plank, marked); they
   permit Edda re-forging it as something hearth-shaped. They forbid
   the village wearing it.
4. The **fourth Old Faerie word** of the goblin-fold's lost tongue.
   Three are introduced here. A fourth — the older Faerie *name* of
   the drum itself — is held, by canon, in the same Briarwood scribe's
   hand. A future codex run on the under-stream may set it down.
5. The **goblin-fold's first Warlord** — *aen-irren*'s first wound. A
   pre-Sundering name, never spoken in polite company (the world.md
   line, *older powers under the Pantheon, not named in polite
   company,* applies). Reserved.

## Voice-rules for future writers (canonical)

A short, hard-rule list. These are not suggestions.

1. **Never call a goblin cruel.** They are *aen-irren.* Use *feral,
   tired, forgotten, courteous-to-no-one.*
2. **Never use the word kill in a Warden's mouth about a goblin.**
   The Wardens' verbs are *count, quiet, clear, lay down.* (See the
   seven reasons, above.)
3. **Never give a goblin a personal name.** Names belong to the
   un-forgotten. The Warlord is *the Warlord;* the brutes are *the
   brute by the western drum.* This is not a cruelty; it is a canon
   honesty about *aen-irren.*
4. **Never have a goblin speak Common.** The cant is broken Old Faerie
   only. If a goblin must, in a future cinematic, *speak,* the line is
   in cant, and the cant is *not* subtitled. The herbalists hear what
   the herbalists hear.
5. **Never let a goblin laugh.** *aen-irren* is a sad word; the goblins
   do not have, in their broken inheritance, the muscle of laughter.
   They have the drum.
6. **Never make the Warlord noble.** He is a wound. The crown is
   borrowed. The figures are echoes. He is *not* a tragic hero.
7. **Never make the brothers feel guilty for clearing.** Maeve, Hala,
   and Lyra each, in their own voices, give the brothers the right
   shape of the knowing. *We count for them, because they cannot count
   for themselves.* That is the canon. The brothers are not the ones
   who must, in this generation, lift the *aen-irren.*
8. **Never spell out the older name of the goblin-folk.** That name is
   reserved. (Loose end #1, above.)

If a future writer breaks any of these eight, the next QA Triage or
Architect run reverts. THEME §10.10 applies.
