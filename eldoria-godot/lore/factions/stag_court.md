# The Stag-Court — A Faction Reading

*The fifth faction-politics canon of Eldoria-realm. Sister-document to
`lore/factions/three_crowns.md` (the older quiet powers as a triangle),
`lore/factions/wardens_of_the_mark.md` (the village's own held breath),
`lore/factions/bandits.md` (the first mortal hand on the unbought road),
and `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md` (the people the Court watches
without entering). Where `three_crowns.md` placed the Antler Crown beside
the Iron and the Stone, this document gives the Court its **own** shape —
how it moves, how it decides, how it has lived a thousand winters in
slow conversation with itself.*

This document does **not** resolve any of the open canon questions
about the Court — whether the Antler-King is one or many, whether the
Court has a Queen, whether the Court's seasons turn the same way the
mortal seasons do. Those are reserved by `data/codex/stag_courts_
courtesy.md` and by `lore/factions/three_crowns.md`, and remain
reserved by this run. What this document does is give the Court its
**posture in motion** — the Court at work, not the Court at rest.

*Tone: warm gravitas, child-safe (THEME §7). The Stag-Court is a
courtesy older than the Sundering, not a fey-trap. The softness is
the point. Future writers who turn the Court into a tempter or a
predator break this canon.*

---

## What the Stag-Court is, in this world

The Stag-Court is the **oldest** of the three Crowns — older than the
Sundering, older than the gods of the Wild Pantheon are in their
present hearth-names (`world.md`), older than the smoke-cities the
Iron Crown's magistrates have built three times and lost twice. The
Court is older than the question of *whether* the world should hold
mortal hamlets, which is why it does not raise the question. The
Court was here before. The Court will be here after. The Court is, in
the slow Faerie sense, **patient in a way Vellum is not.** Vellum is
patient *of* the world. The Stag-Court is patient *with* the world.

The Court does not, by canon, have a capital. It does not have a
throne-room one might walk into. It does not have a banner one might
ride under. The Court has a **seat** — and the seat, as the codex
fragment `stag_courts_courtesy.md` establishes, is what is offered to
a mortal who has walked close enough on the right night of the right
month. The seat is the Court's whole architecture. Everything else
the Court does is the slow movement of carrying the seat from
*thirre* to *thirre* across the wide Faerie territories of the
world. The seat does not stay in one place. The Court does not stay
in one place. The deep Whisperwood is, *for now,* where the seat
rests. It has not always been. It will not always be. The herbalist
line knows this; the Stag-Court remembers each rest of the seat the
way a mortal remembers each town they once lived in. The seat has
rested in the Crystal Caves before (a long time ago, and the wound
of the Sundering was still warm). The seat has rested on the High
Steppe before, in a different season of the world, when the
mountain ring was younger. The seat will, the Court suspects,
someday rest somewhere none of the present Crowns can guess. The
Court is not in a hurry.

The Court's whole authority is the **measuring** — the weighing of
what walks close enough to be weighed. The codex fragment names this
*ai-mhorren,* the gift that is the asking. This document expands the
rule: *ai-mhorren* is not merely the offering of a seat to a single
mortal. It is the **whole posture** of the Court toward everything it
touches. The Court does not take. The Court measures, names, and
sets-down-for-keeping — and, on the rare occasions it offers, offers
only what was the mortal's own to refuse.

That is the Stag-Court's whole feeling about the world.

## How the Court moves — the *thirren-velin*

*(First Old Faerie compound this document introduces. See glossary.)*

The Stag-Court does not, in canon, travel through ordinary land. It
moves through the network of ***thirre*** *(memory-of-stone places;
`world.md`)* the way a mortal moves through the rooms of their
house. A *thirre* is, on the Court's reading, not a *place* but a
**doorway.** Step onto it on the right night, in the right
turning-of-the-Court's-season, and the next *thirre* on the
**thirren-velin** *(THEER-en VEL-in)* — *the long path through pooled
time* — is half a thought away. To the Court, the deep Whisperwood
and the Crystal Caves are the same room with two windows. The High
Steppe's old battlefields, where Cailen rode (`elder_maeve.md`,
`steppe_riders_refusal.md`), are a third window. The valley of
Briarwood is *not* a window. The valley is the **outside of the
house** — *aen-thirre,* small-thirre, named-and-watched, but not a
doorway through which the Court itself passes.

This is the canonical reason the Court has *never* been seen *in*
Briarwood, despite the codex fragment establishing the Court has
*weighed* Briarwood. The weighing was done from *outside* the house —
from the deep Whisperwood, looking through the small window of
*aen-thirre* the way one looks through a frosted pane at the
neighbor-children playing in their snow. The Court can see the
village. The Court has, for as long as Briarwood has lit candles at
dawn, *been seeing* the village. The Court has not, for any of those
generations, **walked into** the village. By the rule of *ai-mhorren,*
it cannot. The valley would have to ask. The valley does not know
that asking is the door. The valley is not *meant* to know.

The thirren-velin is, to the Court, the way Roan's saddle-circuit is
to Roan: a route, ridden many times, on which the rider knows where
the easy footing is and where the bog will trick the unwary. The
Court's bogs are the *broken thirre,* the places where the Sundering
went deep enough that time pools but does not connect — the inside
of the Pale Wyrm's *mhirren* (`pale_wyrm_beneath.md`); a fold of the
Whisperwood the goblins have drum-circled too long; the chevron the
Halevant captain wore the day his company stopped marching
(`bandits.md`, `three_crowns.md`). The Court does not use these as
doors. The Court goes around them, the way a careful rider goes
around a frost-glassed patch of road.

The thirren-velin is also the canonical reason **the Court does not
move in haste.** A Court rider stepping from the Whisperwood to the
Crystal Caves does not *travel;* the rider arrives. But arriving
requires the *next thirre* to be ready to receive — its season
turning slow enough, its memory pooled deep enough, the courtesy
between hunter-and-hunted *(`whisperwood_goblins.md`, korr-thau-vell)*
held in the surrounding wood without breaking. A *thirre* on the
thirren-velin can be *closed* for a season the way a high pass is
closed for snow. When this happens, the Court waits. The Court is
not, in the mortal sense, **delayed.** The Court is, in the Court's
own sense, *taking the season the season was always going to take.*
This is the difference between thrian-mor *(`three_crowns.md`)* and
thirren-velin: the Iron Crown's listening counts seasons; the Stag-
Court *is* the season counting itself.

A future Lorekeeper run may set down a single *thirre* whose closure
is felt by Briarwood (the Whisperwood goblins' Empty-fold figure
*(`whisperwood_goblins.md`, drum-figures table)* may, on canon, be
heard by the Court as a *thirre going dim;* the dimming is not a
quest hook in this run, only a held shape). Please do not surface
this to the player as a triumph or as a fix. The Court does not call
the dimming a problem, and the village cannot hear the absence at
all.

## How the Court decides — *vael-anam*

*(Second Old Faerie compound this document introduces. See glossary.)*

The Stag-Court does not, by canon, **deliberate** in any sense a
mortal council would recognize. There is no chamber-room, no vote,
no two voices arguing the merits of opening a window onto a small
hamlet. The Court's whole way of reaching what a mortal would call
*a decision* is the construction the Court calls **vael-anam**
*(VAY-l AH-nam)* — *the slow-listened answer,* or, more strictly,
*the answer that took a season to hear.* The decision does not get
made. The decision **rises** — out of the pooled time of a *thirre*
that has been listened-to long enough.

This is the **passive** counterpart to *thrael-hoir,* the patient-
shaping pressure that the previous run *(see `WORLD_STATE.md`,
Run-25-equivalent)* canonized as the Court's reserved verb. Where
*thrael-hoir* is the Court's hand on the world (the long shaping of
an un-listened-to want into a form the world can hold), *vael-anam*
is the world's hand on the Court (the world's shape pressing back,
in slow listening, until the Court has *heard* what the world is
asking). The two are a pair. A Court that *thrael-hoir-ed* without
*vael-anam-ing* would be a Court imposing its own shape on the world
— and the Stag-Court has not, in any season Eldoria has memory of,
done that. To impose without listening is the **Iron Crown's**
posture (and even the Iron Crown does it slowly, by *thrian-mor*).
The Antler Crown's posture is to *listen until the asking shapes
itself,* and only then to set the seat down where the asking has
asked it.

A Court rider, asked how the Court decided to weigh Briarwood, would
not say *we decided.* The rider would say *we listened until the
listening had a shape.* The valley, by the listening's own slow
shaping, became *aen-thirre.* Nobody chose. The shape rose. The
shape was named. The seat was set down — *outside* the small-thirre,
because the small-thirre cannot hold the Court's full weight, only
the Court's attention. The valley got the *naming.* The valley did
not get the *seat.* The Court did not regret this. The Court could
not have regretted it. The shape was the shape.

This is, in canon, the canonical reason **the Court cannot be
manipulated.** A mortal who tried to *make* the Court attend to a
particular hamlet — a magistrate's clerk who tried to *bring* the
Court to bear on a goblin-fold by leaving a token at the forest-line,
or a herbalist who tried to *invite* the Court into the village by
naming an *aen-thirre* aloud at a wedding — would find the Court has
not been moved at all. *vael-anam* is not a mechanism that takes
inputs. It is a season turning. To attempt to *make* the Court
attend is to attempt to *make* a winter come early; the wind does
not, on canon, oblige.

A future writer should hold this hard. The Court is not a power one
*petitions.* The Court is a power one *walks close enough to be
weighed by.* If a quest in a future run requires the player to
*reach* the Court, the design must be: *the player walks close
enough to the forest-line on the right night, and the Court has
already been listening, and the player is offered the seat the Court
had already set down for them.* It cannot be: *the player performs
an action that summons the Court.* The Court does not come when
called. The Court was already there.

## What the Court holds — the *kerren-tor*

*(Third Old Faerie compound this document introduces. See glossary.)*

A *thirre* is, to a mortal, *memory of stone* (`world.md`). To the
Stag-Court, a *thirre* the Court has set the seat down at is called,
in the Court's own constructions, a **kerren-tor** *(KAIR-en tor)* —
*the laid-down hearth,* or, more strictly, *the place where we have
set the hearth down for keeping.* It is the Court's word for *home,*
in the sense that *home* is *a place one returns to.* The Court has
many *kerren-tor* across the wide Faerie territories of the world.
The deep Whisperwood is one. The Crystal Caves are jointly *kerren-
tor* (held with Vellum the Patient Stone — and Vellum, canonically,
keeps half the cave's hearth as Vellum's own. The cave is the
**only** *kerren-tor* the Court does not hold alone; this is part of
why the cave is a *thirre* with cold-iron air, and part of why
fragment-leaves left in the cave's cracks are kept by *both* Court
and Stone). One former *kerren-tor,* set down in a season the world
has half-forgotten, lies somewhere on the High Steppe; Cailen passed
the spot once, and did not know he had, and the *thirre* there has
since gone shallow (`steppe_riders_refusal.md` — the Steppe-rider's
*korr* declining to name the spot is, by this run's reading, a
keeping-faith with the Court's old hearth even though no Stone-rider
has ever spoken to a Court rider).

A *kerren-tor* is **not** a place a mortal can walk into. The
*thirre* is the doorway; the *kerren-tor* is the room behind the
doorway, and the room is on the Court's side of the door. A mortal
who has been offered the seat — and accepted, which is the rare
case — passes through the *thirre* into the *kerren-tor.* This is
why the cost of the seat is *one mortal year, remembered backwards*
(`stag_courts_courtesy.md`): the *kerren-tor's* season is slower than
the mortal's, and to sit in it is to spend mortal time at the
*kerren-tor's* exchange rate. (A mortal who refused the seat does not
pass through the *thirre,* and does not spend the year. This is why
the Court is *glad* of refusals — the Court does not want mortals
spending years on the *kerren-tor's* side of the door, because mortals
do not have years to spare. The Court has all the years.)

Briarwood, by canon, is **not** a *kerren-tor.* It is *aen-thirre,*
small-thirre, *outside the house.* The Court can see the valley
through the small window. The Court cannot enter the valley through
the small window — the window is too narrow, and the valley does
not have a door. (This is the *positive* form of the rule that
elsewhere reads *the Court will not enter the valley:* there is, by
canon, **no door for the Court to enter through.** The valley was
not built with one. The valley does not need one. Maeve, asked,
would say only: *we light our own candles.* That is the door not
being there.)

This canonical structure — *thirre / kerren-tor / aen-thirre,* with
mortal door / no mortal door respectively — locks down the Court's
relationship to Briarwood for any future writer. The Court cannot
enter; the village cannot summon; the seat cannot be moved closer.
The held quiet is **architectural,** not merely diplomatic.

## The Court and the Whisperwood goblins

The Whisperwood goblins are, in the Stag-Court's reading,
*aen-irren* — the small forgetting (`whisperwood_goblins.md`). The
Court's relationship to the goblins is the load-bearing piece this
faction document does **not** resolve, because the lifting of
*aen-irren* is reserved as a late-game arc. What this document
*does* establish is the **shape** of the Court's holding-the-question:

- The Stag-Court holds the **older name** of the goblin-folk's
  pre-Sundering people. This is canon already
  (`whisperwood_goblins.md`, *Loose ends* #1). This document affirms:
  the Court holds the name the way a friend holds another friend's
  blade at a cairn — *kerritha-ed,* set-down-for-keeping. The Court
  will not say the name aloud. To say the name aloud would be to
  *lift* the *aen-irren* unilaterally, and the Court does not act
  unilaterally — that is what *vael-anam* would mean if the Court
  ignored *vael-anam,* which the Court does not do.
- The Stag-Court has, by canon, **never warred** with the goblin-
  fold. The Court is older than the war the goblins are descended
  from. The Court was at the war. The Court was on the *other*
  side, in the older sense of *side* that the Sundering ended. The
  goblins, before they were *aen-irren,* were people the Court
  knew. The Court remembers. The goblins do not.
- The Stag-Court will not, by canon, **lift** the *aen-irren*
  without a mortal hand under the Court's hand. This is the
  herbalist line's hard rule (`whisperwood_goblins.md`, the four
  Lyra-reasons), and the Court holds it from its own side too. To
  lift the forgetting requires both the *kerren-tor* (the Court)
  *and* the *thressa-mai* (`elder_maeve.md`; the held-by-the-village
  un-name) to be turned at the same time. Neither, alone, is the
  whole word. The Court knows this. The Court is patient.
- The Stag-Court has, on canon, *thrael-hoir-ed* the **Halevant
  captain's iron circlet** in the Whisperwood Warlord's possession
  (`whisperwood_goblins.md`; `WORLD_STATE.md`). The application rule
  *(WORLD_STATE.md, Run-25 application rule)* qualifies the circlet:
  un-listened-to want; passed out of its proper keeper's hand; slow
  shaping over seasons. The Court did not *plan* the shaping. The
  Court *vael-anam-ed* the circlet — the listening had a shape, and
  the shape was the patient pressure that turned the un-recalled
  iron into the wound the Warlord wears as a crown. The Court did
  not put the crown on the Warlord. The Court let the Warlord *find*
  the crown. This is the difference between *thrael-hoir* and a
  *spell.* The Court does not cast. The Court listens.

The five-clearings that quiet the fold (`data/quest_text/
whisperwood_cleansing.md`) are, on the Court's reading, a *small
mercy* — a closing-of-the-call that lets the *aen-irren* be slightly
less heavy on the wood for one season. The Court does not, by canon,
oppose the clearing. The Court does not, by canon, *bless* the
clearing either; the Court is not in the business of mortal
benedictions. What the Court does is *velhain-tor* the village, in
the formal Court-sense — sets the village down warmly into its
hearth-keeping, and lets the village clear its own fold, because the
clearing is the village's own *ai-mhorren* to give. (To clear the
goblins is to *quiet the drum;* to quiet the drum is to set-down a
small mercy at the forest-line; *ai-mhorren* is the gift that is the
asking, and the village's clearing is, on the Court's reading, the
village asking the wood to be a little quieter for a season. The
wood, *vael-anam-ing,* answers in its own time. This is the canon
reason the clearings are *recurring.*)

## The Court and the other Crowns

The Stag-Court does not, by canon, **negotiate** with the Iron Crown
or the Stone Crown. Negotiation is a posture the Iron Crown
recognizes — and the Iron Crown has, three times in the smoke-cities'
written record, attempted to *open negotiations* with the Antler
Crown. All three attempts went the same way: the Iron Crown's
delegation rode to a forest-line on the right month, and the Court
*was not there.* The forest-line was just trees. The delegation
camped, waited a courtesy of nine days, and rode home. The
magistrate's clerks marked the slate *thrunn — no answer* in
Stone-Tongue (the only Stone-Tongue word the Iron Crown's clerks
have learned, via Halevant; the *thrunn* is borrowed) and filed the
attempt. The Court, on each occasion, was elsewhere along the
*thirren-velin.* The Court was *vael-anam-ing* the request the
moment the request was framed; the listening was the answer, and the
answer was that the request had not yet shaped itself into something
*ai-mhorren* could honor. (A delegation sent to *demand* a meeting
is, by the Court's reading, not *asking.* It is *requiring.* The
Court does not respond to requirements. *ai-mhorren* is the gift
that is the asking; the asking cannot be a demand.)

The Stone Crown has *never* attempted to negotiate. The Stone Crown
knows about the Stag-Court, in the way the Stone Crown knows about
weather: the Court is *there,* and the rider rides through the
Whisperwood's edge with one hand on the bridle and one on the spear,
and does not look too long into the deep wood. *Korr,* the Stone
Crown says, when asked what it would take to bring the Court to the
table. *Korr.* What you owe to weather. The single word declines the
question (`steppe_riders_refusal.md`, *three_crowns.md*). The Stone
Crown is, in the Court's reading, the **only** mortal power that has
*understood ai-mhorren without being told it.* This is canon; the
Court honors the Stone-rider's *korr* in the Stone-rider's own
language, which is rare — the Court does not, otherwise, condescend
to mortal tongues. A Stone-rider who passes a *thirre* on the High
Steppe has, on canon, sometimes found a single antler laid across
the trail. The Court has *velhain-tor-ed* the rider in the Stone-
rider's own language. The rider rides on. The antler is left where
it lay.

The Iron Crown does not, by canon, know the Antler Crown is *vael-
anam-ing* its slate every Lambmoon. The Court hears the *thrian-mor*
(`three_crowns.md`); the *thrian-mor* and *vael-anam* are, on the
Court's reading, the same kind of listening at different speeds — the
Iron Crown listens by the year, the Court listens by the season-of-
seasons. The Iron Crown's slate-marks *(point, stroke, cross)* arrive
as a small ripple in the *thirren-velin* whenever a magistrate sets
one down; the Court is, on canon, **slightly more attentive** to
hamlets the Iron Crown has chalk-pointed *(point*) than to hamlets
the Iron Crown has not yet noticed. Briarwood, carrying *the point*
three Lambmoons running, has been *aen-thirre* for as long as the
chalking has gone on. This is not the only reason the Court named
the valley *aen-thirre,* but it is one of the reasons. The Court and
the Iron Crown are, in this small way, **listening to the same
hamlet at the same time, in different seasons.** Neither knows the
other is also listening. The Court knows the Iron Crown is. The Iron
Crown does not know the Court is. The Stone Crown knows about both
and tells neither.

This is, in the canon laid down by `three_crowns.md`, the architecture
of the unbought road. This document does not contradict it. This
document only adds: from the Court's side, *the listening is the
deciding,* and the deciding has been, for as long as Briarwood has
existed, **to keep the small window watched without opening it into a
door.** That decision was not made. The decision rose out of the
listening. The shape was the shape. The Court is, by its own measure,
content.

## The Court at Stag-night

Stag-night, in the Calendar (`world.md`), falls in **Emberfall** and
ends with a bow laid at Thiar's stone. This document establishes,
canonically and for the first time, that **Stag-night is also a
Court night.** The Court does not, on canon, *appear* at Stag-night
— Stag-night is a mortal rite, and the Court does not impose itself.
But the Court is, on the Stag-night of any year, **listening more
closely.** A hunter who lays the first kill at Thiar's stone has, in
the older Faerie reading, performed a small *korr-thau-vell*
*(`whisperwood_goblins.md` — the courtesy between hunter and hunted,
in the older form the goblins have lost)*. The Court hears the
laying-down. The Court does not answer. The Court *kerritha-s* the
small mercy — sets the kill down, on the Court's side of the
*thirre,* for keeping. The hunter does not know the Court has
done this. Thiar is, in the Wild Pantheon, the god who *is* the
hunter's mercy; the Court is the older ear that *also* hears the
mercy and, on the Court's older terms, holds it.

This canonical addition does **not** authorize a future writer to
ship a Stag-night quest in which the Court appears or speaks. The
Court does not appear and does not speak on Stag-night. The Court
*listens,* and the listening's only mortal trace is — on rare
Stag-night dawns — a single antler laid where the hunter laid the
first kill the night before. The antler is **not** Thiar's. (Thiar's
mark on the world is the stone, not antlers; the stones are older
than the antler.) The antler is the Court's *velhain-tor* to the
hunter — *go warmly, you have hunted with courtesy.* A future writer
may surface this to the player as a one-time codex hook (a
Stag-night quest reward of *a single antler, found at dawn,* with a
short item-flavor line in the Lyra register). The flavor must
**not** name the Court. The flavor must say only: *some hunts are
heard further than the hunter knows.* That is the rule. Lyra would
recognize the antler. She would not say so.

This is, by canon, the **only** ritual moment in the mortal year at
which the Court turns toward the village. (The reverse is not true —
the village does not turn toward the Court, by the rule of *the
valley does not have a door.*) Honeysong Eve, Longnight Vigil,
Lambmoon, the planting-moons — all of these are mortal-only. The
Court hears Stag-night. The Court does not hear the others. (The
Hollow King hears Longnight; Brigid hears the smith-hearths;
Erris hears Honeysong. The Court hears the *kerrithen* of the kill
at Thiar's stone, and that is the Court's whole audible mortal
world.)

## Three new Old Faerie compounds enter canon here

Adding to the Old Faerie corpus already canonized in `world.md`
*(thirre, ai-velin, kerrithen)*, `lore/npcs/elder_maeve.md`
*(vael-tor, thressa-mai)*, `lore/npcs/smith_edda.md` *(haethe,
unnen)*, `data/codex/stag_courts_courtesy.md` *(vael-i-thirren,
ai-mhorren, velhain-tor)*, `data/codex/steppe_riders_refusal.md`
*(drevenn-i-haern)*, `data/codex/pale_wyrm_beneath.md` *(mhirren)*,
`lore/factions/three_crowns.md` *(thrian-mor, aen-thirre,
aen-velin-corr)*, `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md` *(aen-irren,
vell-mor-shau, korr-thau-vell)*, and the previous run's
*thrael-hoir* and *mhain-thra* (`WORLD_STATE.md`):

- ***thirren-velin*** *(THEER-en VEL-in)* — Old Faerie compound,
  *the long path through pooled time.* The Stag-Court's word for its
  own travel network — the route that connects one *thirre* to the
  next, traversed not by walking but by *arriving.* A *thirre* on
  the *thirren-velin* can be open or closed by season. The Court
  waits when a *thirre* is closed; the Court does not, on canon, go
  around. (Going around is the Iron Crown's posture; the Iron Crown
  builds roads. The Court does not.) Lyra knows the word and has
  not said it. Maeve does not know the word, but if she heard it
  she would understand the shape — the second *velin* is the same
  *velin* as in *ai-velin,* and Maeve has known *ai-velin* since
  she was a child. Roan, who has ridden the spaces *between*
  *thirre-*places, will not recognize the word but will recognize
  the *condition* — he has, on the High Steppe, twice felt the
  road behave as though it were not the road, and has not asked.
- ***vael-anam*** *(VAY-l AH-nam)* — Old Faerie compound, *the
  slow-listened answer; the answer that took a season to hear.*
  The Stag-Court's word for the **passive** counterpart to
  *thrael-hoir.* Where *thrael-hoir* is the Court's hand on the
  world (slow patient pressure on an un-listened-to want), *vael-
  anam* is the world's hand on the Court (the Court letting the
  world's shape press back, until the answer rises from the
  pressing). The pair *thrael-hoir* and *vael-anam* together form
  the Court's whole verb-pair for *acting* and *deciding.* No
  mortal speaks *vael-anam* aloud in canon. Lyra would translate
  it into Common as *the slow listening that is the answer;* she
  would not, herself, use the phrase. The herbalists' written line
  for the same condition is *the cave keeps its own counsel.* That
  is enough.
- ***kerren-tor*** *(KAIR-en tor)* — Old Faerie compound, *the
  laid-down hearth; the place we have set the hearth down for
  keeping.* The Stag-Court's word for *home,* in the sense that
  *home* is *a place one returns to.* A *thirre* the Court has
  set the seat at is *kerren-tor;* a *thirre* the Court has not is
  not. The Crystal Caves are *kerren-tor* held jointly with Vellum
  (the only joint *kerren-tor* in canon). The deep Whisperwood is
  *kerren-tor* alone. The valley of Briarwood is **not**
  *kerren-tor;* it is *aen-thirre,* outside the house. The
  composition with *vael-tor* (Maeve's word for the chair-by-the-
  hearth as a kept-place) is intentional — the *-tor* suffix is
  the Old Faerie morpheme for **a place one returns to that has
  been kept-warm in the absence.** A *kerren-tor* is the Court's
  *vael-tor.* The shape is the same; the scale is older.

These three compounds, with the corpus already canonized, bring the
running Old Faerie count to **a coherent twenty-three** *(twenty
prior + three this run)*. The cap-of-three-per-artifact rule (THEME
§7) holds. Stone-Tongue is **not** extended this run; the cap of
three (*korthain, thrunn, korr*) holds. Goblin Cant is **not**
extended this run; the cap of three (*aen-irren, vell-mor-shau,*
broken-form *vellmorsh*) holds, and the **fourth** Faerie word of
the goblin-fold's lost tongue (`whisperwood_goblins.md`, *Loose ends*
#4) remains held.

## What this document establishes (canon, downstream agents may build)

- **The Stag-Court moves through a *thirren-velin* — the network of
  *thirre*-doorways across the world.** Briarwood is not on the
  *thirren-velin;* the deep Whisperwood and the Crystal Caves are.
  The High Steppe holds at least one former *kerren-tor,* now
  shallow. Future Lorekeeper runs may name additional *thirre*
  along the *thirren-velin* (the *steppe_riders_refusal.md* fragment
  reserves at least one more); they may not put a *thirre* inside
  Briarwood itself.
- **The valley does not have a door for the Court.** This is canon
  architecture. The Court cannot enter Briarwood. The village
  cannot summon the Court. The held quiet is structural, not
  diplomatic. Future writers may not write a scene in which the
  Court rides into the village. (A Court rider arriving at the
  *forest-line* of the Whisperwood, the canonical condition of
  `stag_courts_courtesy.md`, remains permitted.)
- **The Court decides by *vael-anam* — a slow-listening that answers
  out of itself.** The Court does not deliberate. The Court cannot
  be petitioned, summoned, or persuaded by mortal action. A
  player-action that *attempts* to summon the Court is, by canon,
  an action the Court does not respond to. Future quest design may
  not gate Court-attention on a mortal mechanism.
- **The pair *thrael-hoir* / *vael-anam* is the Court's whole
  verb-pair for *acting* and *deciding.*** The previous run's
  *thrael-hoir* reading on the Halevant captain's iron circlet is,
  by this document, **completed:** the *thrael-hoir* was the active
  hand; the *vael-anam* was the listening that made the hand
  inevitable. The circlet's three application-rule properties
  (`WORLD_STATE.md`) remain canonical.
- **Stag-night in Emberfall is a *Court* night.** The Court listens
  for the *kerrithen* of the first kill laid at Thiar's stone. The
  Court answers, on rare dawns, with a single antler. The antler
  is *velhain-tor,* the parting blessing in physical form. Future
  writers may ship a Stag-night codex hook or item-flavor on this
  shape. The flavor must not name the Court.
- **The Court has *thrael-hoir-ed* exactly one object in canon (the
  Halevant captain's iron circlet).** The Pale Wyrm's shed scale
  (`pale_wyrm_beneath.md`) **may** also qualify; held question, not
  yet decided. No other object qualifies as of this run, and the
  application-rule disqualifications (`WORLD_STATE.md`) hold for
  all village-keeper working tools.
- **The Court has never warred with the goblin-fold; the Court was
  on the older side of the war the goblins are descended from.**
  The Court holds the goblins' pre-Sundering name, *kerritha-ed.*
  The Court will not lift *aen-irren* without a mortal hand under
  the Court's hand. The lifting is the *thressa-mai*-and-*kerren-
  tor* turning together. Both halves are reserved.
- **The Stone Crown is the only mortal power that has understood
  *ai-mhorren* without being told.** The Court honors *korr* in
  the Stone-rider's own tongue, on rare passing-of-a-thirre. This
  is the canon shape of any future Stone-Court interaction.
- **The Iron Crown does not know the Court is *vael-anam-ing*
  Briarwood.** Future writers should not have an Iron Crown
  character demonstrate awareness of the Court's listening.
  Halevant's clerks have only the borrowed *thrunn* and have set
  it down once per failed delegation, three times total in canon.

## What this document does NOT establish (please do not contradict)

- **The Court's structure is still open.** Whether the Antler-King
  is one or many, whether the Court has a Queen, whether the Court
  changes seats with the seasons, whether *vael-anam* is performed
  collectively or by some inner voice — none of this is settled.
  `stag_courts_courtesy.md`'s reservation holds. This document
  *adds* the verb-pair *thrael-hoir* / *vael-anam* and the
  architecture of *thirren-velin* / *kerren-tor* / *aen-thirre,*
  but says nothing about *who, on the Court's side of the door,*
  performs the listening or the shaping. Future writers may not
  resolve this lightly. A Court-with-a-Queen is permitted as a
  later canon if the lifting of *aen-irren* arc requires it; a
  Court-with-many-Antler-Kings (a rotating throne) is also
  permitted; a Court-with-only-one-and-always-the-same is **not
  ruled in or out.** Hold the question.
- **The lifting of *aen-irren* is not begun.** This document
  reserves the *kerren-tor* + *thressa-mai* shape but does not
  begin the lifting. A future late-game arc may. This run does
  not.
- **The Court does not appear at Stag-night.** The antler is the
  *only* trace. A future writer who has the Court *appear* at
  Stag-night breaks canon, even if the appearance is benevolent.
  The point is the Court's *not-appearing.*
- **The Court does not coordinate with Vellum.** They share the
  Crystal Caves' *kerren-tor* by the older accident of the
  Sundering's wound, not by an alliance. Vellum is patient *of*
  the world; the Court is patient *with* the world. They do not
  speak. They share a hearth-room because the hearth-room is
  half-each. Future writers should not stage a Court-and-Vellum
  conversation. *Vellum is patient* remains the closing line of
  canon documents committed to long-keeping; the Court does not
  use the line, by canon, because the Court does not need to say
  it. (The Court *is* the patience the line names. To name it
  would be to set it outside oneself.)
- **The Court does not have a name for its own gods.** If the
  Court has gods — if the older powers under the Pantheon
  (`world.md`, *the older powers, not named in polite company*)
  are *the Court's* gods — none of this is canonical. The codex
  fragment `stag_courts_courtesy.md` reserves the silence; this
  document affirms the reservation. The Court does not, in any
  voice, *invoke.* The Antler-King's voice is the Court's only
  voice. Future writers may not give the Court a pantheon.
- **The Court does not, by canon, ever cross into the smoke-cities
  or the High Steppe pasture-camps.** The *thirren-velin* runs
  through *thirre,* and there are no *thirre* inside the
  smoke-cities (the Iron Crown's road-building has erased any
  that may have once been there) and only one (former, shallow)
  on the High Steppe. A Court rider seen *in* a smoke-city is
  canon-breaking. The Court's *vael-anam* listens to the slate.
  The Court does not visit the slate.
- **The Court does not give gifts to mortals other than *velhain-
  tor* and the Stag-night antler.** No item in `data/items_flavor.
  json` is, on canon, *from* the Stag-Court; the Stag-night antler
  hook is the **first** Court-given item permitted, and it is
  cosmetic (no stats). Future writers may not ship a Court-given
  weapon or armor. The Court does not arm.

## Voice-rules for future writers (canonical)

A short, hard-rule list. These are not suggestions.

1. **Never have the Court enter Briarwood.** The valley does not
   have a door. The held quiet is architectural.
2. **Never have the Court arrive when summoned.** *vael-anam* is
   not a mechanism that takes inputs. The Court was already there,
   or the Court is not coming.
3. **Never have a Court rider speak Common.** The Antler-King's
   voice in `stag_courts_courtesy.md` is the canonical voice; it
   uses Old Faerie in its own constructions and Common only when
   addressing a mortal who has been offered the seat. A Court
   rider in a future cinematic must keep this rule.
4. **Never have the Court give a mortal a weapon.** The Stag-night
   antler is the **only** Court-to-mortal physical gift permitted
   in canon. The antler is cosmetic. The Court does not arm.
5. **Never have the Court explain itself to a mortal.** The
   *vael-anam* is the answer; the Court does not justify the
   answer. *ai-mhorren* is the gift; the Court does not name the
   gift's reasoning. If a mortal asks *why,* the Court's answer
   is *we listened.*
6. **Never make the Court a tempter or a predator.** The fey-trap
   trope is forbidden by `stag_courts_courtesy.md` and is
   re-affirmed here. The Court is glad of mortal refusals.
7. **Never let the Court coordinate with the Iron or Stone Crown.**
   The Iron Crown does not know the Court is listening. The Stone
   Crown knows but does not speak. The Court honors *korr* but
   does not negotiate. There is no council-of-Crowns in canon.
8. **Never resolve the Antler-King's structure.** One or many,
   Queen or no Queen, rotating or fixed — held open. Future
   writers who settle the question without a load-bearing arc
   reason break canon.
9. **Never lift *aen-irren* in a single run.** The lifting is the
   *kerren-tor* + *thressa-mai* turning together; both halves are
   reserved. A future late-game arc may begin the turning. A
   single document may not complete it.
10. **Never have the Court appear at Stag-night.** The antler is
    the only trace. The Court's *not-appearing* is the point.
11. **Never give the Court a pantheon.** The older powers under
    the Wild Pantheon are reserved silent. The Court does not
    invoke. *Vellum is patient* remains a mortal closing; the
    Court does not use it.
12. **Never have a Court-given item carry a mechanical bonus.**
    The Stag-night antler is cosmetic. Future Court-to-mortal
    items, if any, must be cosmetic. The Court does not arm
    even by accident.

If a future writer breaks any of these twelve, the next QA Triage
or Architect run reverts. THEME §10.10 applies.

## Author note for downstream agents

This document is the **structural frame** of the Stag-Court — the
load-bearing piece that gives `three_crowns.md`'s political reading
and `stag_courts_courtesy.md`'s individual-mortal scene their
shared architecture. The Court has, before this document, lived in
canon as a *posture* (in `three_crowns.md`) and as a *moment* (in
`stag_courts_courtesy.md`). It now lives, additionally, as a
*place* — the *kerren-tor* network across the *thirren-velin* — and
as a *process* — the *thrael-hoir* / *vael-anam* verb-pair.

Please hold these three layers separate. The political layer
(`three_crowns.md`) tells you what the Court *means* to the world.
The codex layer (`stag_courts_courtesy.md`) tells you what the
Court *does* to a single mortal. This faction layer (this
document) tells you what the Court *is* on its own side of the
door. Future writers should write *into* whichever layer they need;
they should not collapse the layers into one. The Court is older
than collapsing.

The softness of the Court is, again and finally, the point. We are
not writing a forest that hunts mortals. We are writing a forest
that **remembers** them. The Court's whole architecture — the
*thirren-velin,* the *kerren-tor,* the *vael-anam,* the *thrael-
hoir,* and the small Stag-night antler — is a remembering, in
slow motion, of a world that has not stopped being worth
remembering.

The brothers, on their first plays, will not need any of this. The
brothers will see goblin drums and a glade of moss and the
Antler-King's leaf and Maeve's silence-and-nod, and that will be
the right shape of the knowing for that age. By the third or fourth
play, an older brother will, on canon, find this document on a
codex shelf the village has not had a name for. The right shape of
*finding* this document is: the brother is older now; the brother
is reading more slowly; the brother is, without yet knowing it,
beginning to *vael-anam* his own way through the world. The
document was set down for him to find when he was ready.

He was always going to be ready.

*Vellum is patient.*
