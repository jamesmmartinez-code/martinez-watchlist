# The Realm of Eldoria — World Canon

*The single source of story-truth. NPCs, items, codex pages, and quests all
defer to what is written here. Append cautiously. Contradict never.*

---

## The Sundering

Long before the lanterns of Briarwood were lit, a Pale Wyrm coiled around
the roots of the world. The gods were younger then, and louder. When Vellum
the Patient Stone refused to bow to the Wyrm, the Wyrm broke against him,
and Vellum broke against the Wyrm — and the world cracked between them.
This is the Sundering.

The mountains that ring the valley today are not mountains. They are
Vellum's spine, thrust up through the earth in that one slow shout. The
Whisperwood is the breath the Wyrm exhaled before it slept. The Crystal
Caves are the wound itself, still unhealed, where the sky leaks through
into the dark. The Pale Wyrm has not died — it sleeps somewhere deep, and
its dreaming pushes frost up through certain blades. (Smiths who name a
saber *Frost* know what they are naming.)

After the Sundering the gods went quiet. They are not gone. They are
listening.

## The Three Crowns

There are three rulers in the wide world — none of them in Briarwood, and
that is the village's small mercy.

**The Iron Crown** sits in the smoke-cities far to the south, where mortal
kings count taxes and march their pikemen in straight lines. The Iron Crown
remembers the Sundering only as a story, and not always charitably.

**The Antler Crown** is worn by the Stag-Court of the fey, deep in the
oldest part of Whisperwood. Their court does not move when ours does;
their seasons turn slower. A child who follows fox-fire too far has, on
rare and unpleasant occasions, met them. Most return. The ones who don't
are not, the herbalists insist, *gone.*

**The Stone Crown** is no crown at all but a circlet of braided horsehair,
worn by whichever rider of the High Steppe most recently won the Long Ride.
The mountain clans answer only to weather and to each other.

Briarwood pays no tax to any of these. The Iron Crown has not yet decided
whether the valley exists.

## The Wild Pantheon

Five gods are still spoken to in Eldoria. Children learn them by their
hearth-names.

- **Brigid the Forge-Mother** — fire, hearth, smithwork, mothers who have
  stayed up too late. Smith Edda's anvil bears her mark.
- **Thiar the Stag** — wilderness, the hunt, the mercy owed to prey.
  Hunters lay their first kill at his shrine and eat the second.
- **Vellum the Patient Stone** — endurance, oaths, the keeping of memory.
  His name is sworn into walls and into wedding-rings.
- **The Hollow King** — winter, sleep, dying gracefully. He is not feared.
  He is thanked, in his season, with candles.
- **Erris of the Two Roads** — chance, travel, songs that don't yet have
  endings. Bards leave a coin for her at every crossroads.

There are older powers under these. They are not named in polite company.

## The Calendar

The year holds twelve moons, each of twenty-eight nights:

> Dawnmoot · Lambmoon · Greenshield · Sunpetal · **Honeysong** · Thornripe ·
> Reapmoon · Emberfall · Smokerise · Wolfwake · **Longnight** · Foxthaw

Two great festivals frame the year. **Honeysong Eve** is midsummer — paper
lanterns set adrift on the pond, an offering to Erris. **Longnight Vigil**
is the darkest night of winter — every household lights a candle for someone
who has gone, and Briarwood does not sleep until dawn. Smaller festivals
mark the hunting moons and the planting moons. Stag-night, in Emberfall,
ends with a bow laid at Thiar's stone.

## The Tongues

**Common** is the trade-tongue, spoken in every market and inn. It is what
NPCs speak unless something older is needed.

**Old Faerie** is rare. The Stag-Court still uses it; a few human scholars
read it badly. Three words from it are useful to know:

- ***thirre*** *(THEER-uh)* — "memory of stone." A place where time
  pools — an old battlefield, a sacred grove, the Crystal Caves themselves.
  When an NPC speaks of "a thirre," they mean somewhere the past is still
  present.
- ***ai-velin*** *(eye-VEL-in)* — "the long path." Both the river of stars
  across the night sky and a mortal's life from cradle to cairn. To wish
  someone *ai-velin* at parting is to wish them a complete life.
- ***kerrithen*** *(KAIR-ih-thun)* — "to lay something down so the land
  may hold it." Used for grief, for offerings, and for swords laid at
  cairns. One does not throw away a friend's blade. One *kerritha* it.

**Goblin Cant** is a broken descendant of Old Faerie — the Whisperwood
goblins were once *something else* before the Sundering, and a few of their
words still echo the Stag-Court's. The herbalists notice. The goblins do not.

**Stone-Tongue** is the runic, slow speech of the mountain clans. Each
word is a small oath. They use it sparingly, even amongst themselves.
