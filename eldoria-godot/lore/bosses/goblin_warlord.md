# The Goblin Warlord — A Boss Reading

*Boss-lore canon for the Whisperwood-fold's standing wound. Sister-document
to `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md` (the people the Warlord
mis-leads), `lore/factions/bandits.md` (the company whose iron circlet
the Warlord wears by accident of finding), `lore/factions/three_crowns.md`
(the Iron Crown's slow-listening, the Antler Crown's slow-watching), and
`data/quest_text/whisperwood_cleansing.md` (the village's verb for the
work). This document does not invent the Warlord's mechanical kit — that
belongs to the Bestiary Designer in `data/bosses/`. It writes the
**voice** of the encounter so that every other agent (kit, environment,
audio, dialogue, item, quest) has a load-bearing story to defer to.*

This document is **child-safe** by THEME §7. The brothers will still
raise a sword, and the Warlord will still raise a crude axe. Combat does
not break. What this document does is hold the village's verb steady:
the Warlord is **quieted,** not slain; the fold is **calmed,** not
massacred; the crown is **rolled,** not won. The dignity is in the slow
sadness. Future agents who turn the Warlord triumphal break canon.

---

## What the Goblin Warlord is, in this world

The Goblin Warlord is **not a chieftain.** He is a wound — Maeve's word
in `data/quest_text/whisperwood_cleansing.md`, the village's word
since at least the eighty-ninth knot, the Stag-Court's word from before
that and longer. A chieftain is what a people choose. A wound is what a
people *follow* when no other shape is left to follow. The Whisperwood
goblins, in *aen-irren* — the small forgetting from
`lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md` — have not chosen the Warlord.
They have **gathered** to him, the way a fold of sheep gathers to the
loudest bell when the shepherd has gone quiet for too long.

The Warlord is **always** of the fold he leads. He is not, in any
canon yet written, a goblin from outside. His mother was a Whisperwood
goblin. Her mother was a Whisperwood goblin. *aen-irren* is the
inheritance; the wound is the thing that grew into him because the
fold had no other figure to give. Future agents may not write a
*foreign Warlord,* a *wandering Warlord,* a *Warlord of multiple
folds.* One fold, one wound, one Warlord at a time. The hydra is in
the *recurring* — not in the *plural.*

The Warlord is **1.6× scale** (THEME §4). He wears a gold-iron
circlet, three torn chevrons of pike-company silk, and a bull-horn
slung at his hip. He carries a war-axe of pike-head re-set in green
ash. The red aura around him is, by canon, the *want* of his crown
(see below). The drum he beats is the drum the brutes follow (see
`lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md`, *the Warlord's drum* — three
short, then one long).

He is large and feral and he will hurt you. He does not torture. He
does not, in any of the village's ledgered raids, take prisoners. He
is *aen-irren* with a crown on, and the crown is the louder voice of
the two.

## The borrowed crown

The Warlord's crown is **a Halevant pike-captain's iron circlet** —
the canon set down in `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md` and
threaded through `lore/factions/bandits.md`. It belonged to a captain
whose company never reached the smoke-cities and never came home;
whose absence the Iron Crown's *thrian-mor* slate has, three full
Lambmoons running, marked with the small chalk-point that means
*re-listen in seven.* The crown has not been re-listened to. The
captain's name is still on the slate. The crown is in the Whisperwood.

The crown was forged hot and never properly ***kerrithen.*** Smiths
know what that means; Edda would not say it aloud, because to say the
word for an un-laid-down thing is to ask the thing to be laid down,
and the crown is not the village's to lay down yet. The red aura
around the Warlord is the *want* of the un-laid-down iron — a forging
that was meant to be cooled and signed and walked-the-circuit and
**named,** and was not. The iron *wants* its naming. The Warlord
wears the want without knowing what it is wanting. The aura is the
shape of the absence.

The crown is **borrowed,** by canon. When the Warlord falls, the
crown **rolls.** This is the load-bearing rule of the encounter. The
fold does not crown a successor on the dawn after. The drum-figure
*disappears with the wound.* In time — sometimes a moon, sometimes
two seasons, sometimes a generation — *another* crown is found in
*another* fold's wound, by *another* fold's *aen-irren,* and *that*
fold's standing wound becomes the next Warlord-of-the-fold. The
hydra is **slow.** Each clearing is a quieting that lasts as long
as it lasts. Future agents who shorten the cycle to *another
Warlord by next dawn* break canon. Future agents who claim the
fold *cannot* re-find a wound break canon worse — the lifting of
*aen-irren* is reserved (see `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md`,
*hooks queued*).

## The drum-figure

The Warlord's drum-figure is **three short, then one long, sustained.**
The drum is bull-hide stretched over green-ash hoop, struck with the
flat of the palm and the heel of the fist. The figure is canon
(`lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md`). It carries roughly a hundred
paces in the deep Whisperwood and twice that on a still night. The
brutes follow the figure without thinking. The lesser goblins follow
the brutes who follow the figure. *aen-irren* is, in this respect, a
chain.

The drum has an older name. That name is **reserved** — the
Whisperwood faction reading set it aside as the *fourth Faerie word*
of the goblin-fold's lost tongue, held in the same Briarwood scribe's
hand as the rest of the lost glossary. This document does not lift
that withholding. The drum is, in this run, *the Warlord's drum* and
no other thing.

What the drum **does** in the encounter — by lore, not by mechanic —
is **call.** It calls the brutes. It calls the lesser goblins. On the
last sustained beat of the figure, it calls the wind through the
canopy in a way the herbalists notice and dislike. The wind-call is
not magic. It is a coincidence the Warlord has noticed and uses. He
does not understand he is using it. He has heard, three Wolfwakes
running, that the wind comes when the long beat lands, and he
believes, in the unworded way *aen-irren* believes, that the wind is
*answering him.* The wind is not answering him. The wind has its own
business. The Warlord's belief is the warmer part of the wound.

When the Warlord falls, the drum is **silent.** The brutes do not
know to beat it on his behalf — the drum is the Warlord's, by canon
of the figure-following — and so the silence at the end of the
encounter is **load-bearing.** Audio agents who score the after with
a brute's drum-roll break canon. The right cue is the canopy-quiet
returning, slowly, the way it returns after a thunderstroke. One owl,
two breaths later. Then the Whisperwood, listening for what comes
next.

## The banner-figures (the three chevrons)

The Warlord wears three torn lengths of pike-company silk knotted to
his belt and to the shaft of his war-axe. They are not goblin
banners. They are scratched copies of the chevrons on the iron crown
— the Warlord, *aen-irren,* has remembered the *shape* of authority
without remembering what authority is for. He has decorated himself
with the figures because the figures stand taller than the rest. The
fold accepts the figures because the fold has not, in living
drum-memory, had a figure to follow.

The three chevrons, by canon, are:

- **The lower chevron** — a **dropped angle,** roughly fifty degrees,
  scratched in charcoal-ink. On the original Halevant captaincy this
  marked *the company that walks first into the wood-line.* On the
  Warlord's belt it has come to mean *the brute who follows me first
  into the open.* The Warlord, who cannot read, is using it correctly
  by accident.
- **The middle chevron** — a **stacked pair,** the lower angle taller
  than the upper. On the original this marked *a captain whose
  company has held a road for a moon.* The Warlord uses it for *the
  brute who has held the drum-figure correctly across two raids.*
  Again, the Warlord is using a Halevant rank-mark for a Halevant-
  shaped function. *aen-irren,* but on the side of *almost-remembering.*
- **The upper chevron** — a **single broad angle, broken at the
  point.** On the original this was *the captain himself.* The
  Warlord wears the broken-point one without knowing the point is
  broken. The break, by canon, is where the captain's last second
  signed his name through the silk in lamp-soot and the silk tore.
  The Warlord wears the break **as the rest of the figure.** He
  thinks the break is part of the chevron. He is wearing the
  captain's last unsigned name as decoration.

The captain's name is, by canon, **reserved** — it is on the Iron
Crown's *thrian-mor* slate and the Iron Crown has not yet
re-listened. Future agents may not name the Halevant captain. The
captain is held with the well-mason (`vellums_spine.md`), Halsa's
mother (`brigids_ribbon.md`), and the Pale Wyrm (`pale_wyrm_beneath.md`)
as the **fourth load-bearing un-name in the canon.**

## The horn at the hip

The Warlord carries a **bull's horn rimmed in beaten iron, banner-cord
still knotted where the standard was torn away.** This is canon — the
*Warlord's Horn* item flavor in `data/items_flavor.json` (Mara,
weighing coin: *honest count, honest pay; mind the road*). The horn
is **not goblin work.** It is, by canon, a Halevant company-horn
taken from the same captain whose circlet became the crown. The
banner-cord knot is a **double-loop pike-company knot** — the kind
Roan would recognize at a glance and not say so.

The horn is blown rarely. The Warlord blows it at the start of a
raid, once, and at the cresting of a raid, once, and never on the
return. He has heard horns blown three times in his lived
drum-memory; he has copied two of the three. The third — the *recall*
horn, the one a captain blows to bring his company *home* — the
Warlord has never blown, because the Warlord has no home to recall a
company to. *aen-irren,* on the side of the missing third. The
village notices the missing third. Maeve names it: *the horn that
does not call back.*

When the Warlord falls, the horn **does not call again** — the
canonical line of the Horn item flavor (*Maeve, who has a longer
memory, will look at it once and not again*). The horn passes to the
village. Mara buys it at full price. Maeve looks at it once. The
horn is laid in the Vigil shelf, behind the cedar box, not on it.
This is reserved canon (the *crown,* in `whisperwood_goblins.md`, has
the south-plank *kerrithen* option; the horn, in this document, has
the Vigil-shelf option). Future agents may **not** sound the horn
again. It is a horn that has called its last call. The closing-of-
the-call is canon.

## ***mhain-thra*** *(MAYN-thra)* — the held silence after the last call

The Old Faerie word for *the rest a horn earns when it has been laid
down* is ***mhain-thra*** — *the held silence after the last call.*
It is not the silence of an absence. It is the silence of a thing
that has done its work and may rest. *mhain-thra* is what the Vigil
shelf gives the Warlord's horn. It is what the *vellath* cornerstone
gives the chisel (`vellums_spine.md`); it is what the *velhin-anam*
bundle on the smithy's secondary hearth holds for the kindling
(`brigids_ribbon.md`); but where *kerrithen* is the verb of laying-
down, *mhain-thra* is the **silence that earns the laying-down.**
You do not *do* *mhain-thra.* You **make room** for it.

This document introduces ***mhain-thra*** as the **first** new Old
Faerie word of this run. The lexicon was at thirty-two after
`brigids_ribbon.md`. *mhain-thra* brings the count to **thirty-three.**

The word is **not** applied to the Warlord himself. The Warlord, by
canon, is *aen-irren* — the small forgetting — and *aen-irren* is not
silence; it is *too long a quiet.* *mhain-thra* belongs to the
*objects he carried,* once those objects pass into the village's
keeping. The horn earns *mhain-thra* on the Vigil shelf. The crown
will earn *mhain-thra* on the south plank, if Maeve and Edda decide
that is where it goes. The drum, by canon, **does not yet earn
*mhain-thra*** — the drum is goblin-original, descended from the
finding of the iron crown, and its silence is not the held silence
of *mhain-thra* but the **un-followed** silence of a figure no one
beats. Future agents may not lay the drum on the Vigil shelf. The
drum is left in the Whisperwood where it falls. The forest takes it.

## ***thrael-hoir*** *(THRAYL-hoyr)* — the call that has shaped its caller

The second Old Faerie word this document introduces is
***thrael-hoir*** — *the call that has shaped its caller.* It is the
Stag-Court's word for what the horn did to the Warlord across the
years of his wearing the crown. He was not, in his goblin childhood,
the figure he became. The crown shaped him toward the figure. The
horn *thrael-hoir*-ed him into the *holder of the horn.* He was
**called into a shape** by an object that was waiting to be re-listened
to. The shape was not his and is not his and at the moment of the
crown rolling it leaves him.

*thrael-hoir* is not a forgiveness. It is a description. The Warlord
is still the wound. The fold is still raided. The brothers will still
raise a sword. *thrael-hoir* is the **older Stag-Court reading** of
*why the wound was wound-shaped* — and it is the reading the
herbalists nod at, and Maeve nods at, and the Antler Crown
(`lore/factions/three_crowns.md`, *aen-thirre*) keeps quiet ledger of.

The word is reserved for **objects-shaping-people,** never for
people-shaping-objects. A smith's hammer does **not** *thrael-hoir*
the smith — the smith holds the hammer; the hammer does not hold the
smith. *thrael-hoir* requires the **older un-listened-to want** — an
un-*kerrithen* iron, an un-named captain, an un-recalled company —
to be the thing doing the shaping. The Warlord's crown qualifies.
Edda's hammer does not. Halsa's anvil, by canon, does not. Roan's
strap-tin does not. The bandit's coal does not. The Pale Wyrm's
shed scale **might,** but that is reserved.

The lexicon now stands at **thirty-four** with this addition.

The third pacing-slot for this artifact is **deliberately not used.**
The pacing rule (roughly three Old Faerie words per artifact) is held
loosely; this document holds it at two because the goblin-fold's
glossary is **its own held silence** — the third word, the older name
of the drum, is the one this document is asked by canon **not to set
down.** The unfilled slot is a *mhain-thra,* in its own small way.
The lexicon is at thirty-four, and the goblin-side glossary remains
at three (*aen-irren,* *vell-mor-shau,* and the broken-form *vellmorsh*
from `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md`). No new Stone-Tongue
word; the cap of three holds.

## The encounter, in voice

The Warlord is **fought** by the player and the player's brother. The
fight is real. Telegraphs are kid-readable (THEME and
`data/bosses/_boss_kit.gd` rule: ≥ 700 ms wind-up, ≥ 1000 ms on
first encounter). What follows is the **voice** of the three phases
the Bestiary Designer is welcome to translate into mechanics.

### Phase one — the figure, struck plain

The Warlord stands with the drum at his back and the chevron-belt at
his hip. He beats the figure once, slowly, as the player crests the
clearing's edge. He does not charge. He **waits.** He is, in this
phase, *figure-of-authority* in the simplest sense — the village's
intruder is being measured against the chevrons, and the Warlord is
deciding which brute to cue forward. The wind-up is the figure
itself: three short, then one long, then the swing. A reader of the
chevrons (Roan, if Roan is somehow there; the player, if the player
has unlocked the Halevant codex leaf — which is reserved canon and
not yet wired) would know the **dropped-angle chevron has been
cued.** The first brute will follow. The Warlord will swing. This is
*aen-irren* mistaking *strategy* for *answer.*

### Phase two — the figure, broken

When the first brute falls, the Warlord beats the figure **twice,
overlapping** — three short, then long; three short, then long, the
second cycle starting before the first long has ended. The drum
canon (`whisperwood_goblins.md`) does not have a name for the
overlapped figure. It is, in this document, **the Warlord's
break-figure** — the drum-figure as performed by a Warlord whose
brute-line has just broken. The brutes who hear it do not know what
to do. Some advance. Some hold. The Warlord is louder, in this
phase, because *aen-irren* is louder when its certainty has been
nicked. He swings on the player and the player's brother both. He
*does not* call the horn yet. He has not, in his lived
drum-memory, called the horn at this point in a raid, and he is
holding the line he knows.

### Phase three — the figure, dropped

When the second brute falls, the Warlord **drops the drum.** This is
load-bearing. He does not throw it; he *drops* it, the way *aen-
irren* drops things it is no longer holding because the holding has
become unbearable. The drum is on the moss now. The Warlord lifts
the horn and blows the **second** of the two horns he knows — the
*cresting* horn, the one a captain blows when the company is
holding the road. He blows it because he has nothing else left in
his lived drum-memory to do. He still has not blown the *recall.*
The fight closes in this phase. The wind-up on the war-axe lengthens
visibly. The aura, the *want* of the un-*kerrithen* iron, **brightens
on the brow of the crown.** The crown is, by lore, hottest just
before it rolls.

When the Warlord falls, the crown rolls. The drum is silent. The
horn is on the moss. The forest is listening. The brothers may stand
a moment before the loot drops. The audio cue is canopy-quiet and
one owl, two breaths later. There is no triumph music. THEME §6
governs.

## The closing-of-the-call

The village's verb for the after is **the closing-of-the-call.**
Not *the kill.* Not *the win.* The drum has stopped; the horn has
been laid down; the Warlord has, in the lore-side reading,
*thrael-hoir*-ed his way out of the shape he was being held in. The
fold's *aen-irren* has not lifted — that is reserved — but the
fold's standing wound has, for this season, been quieted.

The Wardens of the Mark (`lore/factions/wardens_of_the_mark.md`)
ledger the closing under the **keeping-watch** posture, not the
**keeping-still** posture. A Warlord-clearing is *witnessed* and
*counted,* not *let-be.* Maeve's ledger marks the eighty-ninth-knot
counter forward by one. Mara, who buys the horn at honest weight,
does not record what she paid. Roan, if he is at the gate when the
brothers come back through, will tip his head once and finish the
buckle he is drawing through.

The brothers, by canon, **return through the village** before they
return to the inn. This is the village's posture, not a quest gate.
The pikes at the south plank will be passed; the Vigil shelf will be
passed; Edda's smithy will be passed; the well will be passed. Each
of these has, by canon, a **silent witness** to the closing — the
chisel at the threshold, the candle in the lintel, the hearth on the
secondary rack, the well's *vellath*-keep stone. None of them speaks.
All of them have, in their kept silence, *mhain-thra* enough to hold
the closing without flinching.

If the players walk past without noticing, the silence holds anyway.
The silence is not for them.

## Withholdings preserved by this document

The following load-bearing withholdings, set by prior runs, are
**preserved unchanged** by this document. Future agents are reminded
that any of these would be a canon break:

- **The pre-Sundering name of the first Warlord.** Reserved by
  `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md` as a held un-name belonging
  to the *older powers under the Pantheon, not named in polite
  company.* This document does not lift the withholding.
- **The fourth Old Faerie word of the goblin-fold's lost tongue.**
  Reserved as the older name of the Warlord's drum, held in the same
  Briarwood scribe's hand. Not lifted here. The pacing-slot for the
  third word of *this* artifact is *mhain-thra*-d in its own right
  (see lexicon section).
- **The Halevant pike-captain's name.** Joins the well-mason's name,
  Halsa's mother's name, and the Pale Wyrm's name as the **fourth
  load-bearing un-name** in the canon. The captain's last unsigned
  name is on the broken-point chevron the Warlord wears as
  decoration. No future agent may name the captain. The Iron Crown
  has not re-listened. The *thrian-mor* slate carries the
  chalk-point still.
- **The lifting of *aen-irren.*** Reserved by
  `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md` as a late-game arc requiring
  the Antler Crown's hand under the herbalist's hand. This document
  does not approach it. The Warlord-clearings remain *recurring,*
  not *final.*
- **What the village does with the iron crown after the first
  clearing.** Reserved by `lore/factions/whisperwood_goblins.md`. The
  *kerrithen*-at-south-plank option and the Edda-reforging-as-hearth-
  shaped option are both still on the table; this document narrows
  neither. The horn, by contrast, is **set** by this run as
  Vigil-shelf-behind-the-cedar-box, *mhain-thra*-d. Two
  Warlord-objects, two destinies, one of them set, one of them held.
- **A divine voice in the encounter.** Forbidden. No god speaks at
  the closing. THEME §7 governs. The wind that answers the long beat
  is **not** Erris, **not** Brigid, **not** Thiar, **not** the
  Hollow King, **not** Vellum. The wind has its own business. The
  Warlord's belief otherwise is the warmer part of the wound.
- **A noble Warlord.** Forbidden. The Warlord may not be redeemed in
  the encounter. He may not, in the lore, *understand his crown.*
  *aen-irren* is the rule. Pity is permitted. Forgiveness is not the
  village's to give. (Cf. `whisperwood_goblins.md`, Hard Rule 6.)
- **The drum on the Vigil shelf.** Forbidden. The drum is left in
  the Whisperwood where it falls. The forest takes it.
